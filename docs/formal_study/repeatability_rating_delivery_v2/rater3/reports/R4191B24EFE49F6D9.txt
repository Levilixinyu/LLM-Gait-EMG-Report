## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

RF and ERS have the greatest reported threshold coverage. RF, VL, BF, ST, TA and ERS explicitly remain above threshold across the initial-contact cycle boundary at the **mean-envelope level**. These results do not establish physiological onset, offset, continuous contraction, inactivity outside retained intervals, or the same pattern in every stride. Without individual events and matched muscle-specific timing references, intervals cannot be assigned gait-phase functions or characterized as early, delayed or prolonged.

All listed maximum-|z| locations are **above the phase-specific reference mean**. They identify the largest standardized difference, not necessarily peak envelope amplitude or a gait event. These are extreme-value statistics across 1001 points; a conventional 2-SD classification is inappropriate. Among the explicit muscle-specific calibration values supplied, ERS 5.8 exceeds its pooled healthy 95th percentile of 3.65, while TA 3.3 is below its corresponding 4.61 value. These are not matched leave-one-out limits.

**Whole-cycle CCI:**
- **TA–GAS: 0.60**, compared with empirical interval 0.24–0.59 and median 0.39. The reported value is 0.01 higher than the displayed upper endpoint.
- **RF–BF: 0.66**, within empirical interval 0.48–0.83 and slightly below median 0.68.

CCI measures whole-cycle overlap of continuous normalized envelopes, including sub-threshold contributions—not simultaneous activation time, muscular force or phase-specific behavior. Its interpretation depends on normalization and envelope methods; these comparisons do not establish normal muscle function, pathological co-activation or stiffness.
