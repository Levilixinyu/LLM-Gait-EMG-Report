These are **ensemble-averaged envelopes**, normalized using a participant-, side-, and muscle-specific maximum shared across available strides before averaging, without further amplitude normalization. Their amplitudes do not measure force or strength, and the averages do not establish stride-to-stride variability.

These findings describe threshold coverage and continuity of averaged envelopes—not physiological onset, offset, continuous contraction, or inactivity elsewhere. They do not establish the same pattern in every stride. Without intermediate events and matched timing references, the intervals cannot be assigned gait-phase functions or characterized as early, delayed, or prolonged.

At every listed maximum absolute z location, the envelope is **above the phase-specific reference mean**. These locations identify the largest standardized differences, not necessarily peak envelope amplitude or gait events.

CCI is a method-dependent whole-cycle overlap index of normalized continuous envelopes, including sub-threshold contributions. It is not simultaneous activation time, muscular force, or a phase-specific result; these comparisons do not establish normal or pathological co-activation.
