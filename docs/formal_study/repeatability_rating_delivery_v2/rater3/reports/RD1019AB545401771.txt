## 1. Objective Findings

**Participant and side.** Sub36_P is recorded in the **stroke** group, and the analyzed limb is the **paretic** side. The released cycle for this limb runs from initial contact of the paretic limb (0%) to the next initial contact of the same limb (100%); these are two successive occurrences of the same event, not one instant. No intermediate event or phase boundary is supplied, so all timing below is expressed as a percentage of the analysis cycle only.

**Spatiotemporal values as supplied.**
- Gait speed **0.23 m/s**, which falls in the "household ambulator (<0.4 m/s)" speed band. This label denotes a speed band only; it is not a measure of walking independence, safety, or real-world community mobility.
- Cadence **64.9 steps/min**.
- Step length symmetry index **−35.9%**, computed with the **successive-footfall** definition (each heel at its own initial contact). By the stated sign convention, a negative value indicates the **paretic step is shorter** than the non-paretic step. The alternative instantaneous definition can yield a different number for some subjects; that alternative value is not supplied here.

**Reference-comparability limit (applies to every comparison in this report).** The deviation reference actually used for this subject is **138 pooled healthy subjects, 1001 phase points (main_cohort_excel)**. That cohort pools both body sides and all ages and is not matched to this participant. Critically, the healthy reference cohort's self-selected speeds span **0.80–1.79 m/s**, and this subject walks at 0.23 m/s — **no speed-matched healthy reference exists in this dataset for this participant**. Every difference from the reference described below therefore conflates very-slow-walking effects with stroke-related control differences; the two cannot be separated from these data, and no quantity here establishes a disease effect, a severity grade, or a clinical diagnosis. Cohort-wide percentiles quoted below are pooled calibration values, not matched leave-one-out normal limits for this subject.

**What is in this input and what is not.** Kinematics are **sagittal plane only**; coronal and transverse channels are not included in this summary. Individual toe-off timing, muscle-specific matched normal timing references, stride-to-stride variability, raw-signal quality checks, and expert plots are **not supplied in this task** — their absence here says nothing about whether they exist in the source release. Age, sex, time post-stroke, stroke clinical scores, and non-paretic-side data are not provided in this input. Seven EMG channels (GAS, RF, VL, BF, ST, TA, ERS) and four sagittal joint/segment channels (ankle, knee, hip, pelvis) are present.

## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

**How these numbers must be read.** All EMG values are properties of an **ensemble-averaged envelope** per muscle, normalized to a shared maximum across available strides for this participant, side, and muscle (the denominator is not shared between sides). Amplitudes are therefore **relative envelope values within each curve** — they are not neural activation, force, or strength, and muscles must not be ranked against one another from them. "Above-threshold interval" means the region where the averaged envelope exceeds **20% of that curve's own peak**, retained only if ≥3% of the cycle. These intervals are detection outputs at the mean-envelope level: they are **not** physiological onset, offset, contraction duration, or inactivity outside the intervals, and they do not establish that the same pattern occurred in every stride. An onset at 0% or an offset at 100% is the analysis-window boundary.

**Coverage and boundary observations.** Six of seven averaged envelopes (GAS, RF, VL, BF, ST, TA) remained above their own 20% threshold **across the cycle-defining initial-contact boundary**, so for those curves the detected region is continuous through 100%/0% rather than starting there. ERS has a single retained interval ending at the analysis-window boundary (100%), which is a window edge, not a detected offset. Retained coverage ranges from 36% (BF) to 88% (GAS and RF) of the cycle. Portions of the cycle outside the retained intervals are simply **not covered by a retained interval** — that is compatible with sub-threshold envelope values or with above-threshold segments too short to satisfy the 3% duration rule, and the supplied evidence does not distinguish these. GAS and TA each show multiple separate retained intervals in addition to a wrapping one; this is a description of the averaged envelope's threshold crossings, not of burst counts in individual strides. No interval here is assigned to stance, swing, loading, push-off, clearance, or weight acceptance, and none can be called early, delayed, or prolonged, because individual toe-off and matched muscle-specific timing references are not supplied.

**Max |z| values.** Max |z| is an **extreme-value statistic** — the largest standardized difference across all 1001 phase points — not a single-observation z-score, and its location marks where the standardized difference from the phase-specific reference mean is largest, **not** necessarily the time of peak envelope amplitude or any gait event. "Above/below reference" means relative to the reference mean at that phase point, not relative to a healthy percentile limit. The conventional 2-SD rule does not apply: in this dataset every one of 109 healthy controls exceeds |z| = 2 on at least one muscle, and per-muscle healthy 95th percentiles for this statistic span **3.65 (ERS) to 4.61 (TA)**, higher still under leave-one-out. Against that framing: BF (2.3), VL (3.0), and RF (3.5) fall below the lowest per-muscle 95th percentile quoted; ST (3.8) falls inside the 3.65–4.61 band, and its muscle-specific percentile is not supplied, so it cannot be classified either way; TA (5.0) exceeds its own stated 95th percentile of 4.61; ERS (6.3) exceeds the stated ERS value of 3.65; GAS (18.9) exceeds the entire quoted band by a wide margin. Exceeding a pooled calibration percentile is a descriptive statement about distance from an unmatched reference at one phase point — it does not by itself establish pathology, and at 0.23 m/s the slow-walking contribution cannot be separated out.

**Co-contraction indices.** CCI here is the Falconer–Winter index computed on the **full continuous normalized envelopes over the whole cycle** (2 × common area / summed areas). It is a whole-cycle overlap index — not simultaneous activation time, not muscular force, not a phase-specific result — and it can be non-zero even where detected intervals do not overlap, because sub-threshold envelope area contributes. It depends on the normalization and envelope methods used here, so values and thresholds from other studies or algorithms do not transfer.
- **TA vs GAS = 0.58**, against the supplied healthy reference median 0.39 (2.5th–97.5th pct 0.24–0.59): above the median and **inside the interval but close to its upper endpoint**.
- **RF vs BF = 0.51**, against the supplied median 0.68 (0.48–0.83): below the median and **inside the interval but close to its lower endpoint**.

Lying inside these intervals does not establish physiologically normal co-activation or normal muscle function, and proximity to an endpoint does not establish abnormality, stiffness, impaired control, or a diagnosis.

**Composite (GPS-like) score: 14.68.** This is the RMS of the per-joint GVS values across the supplied sagittal channels — it is a "GPS-like" composite, not the validated 9-variable GPS. Relative to the supplied composite reference (median 5.1, 95th percentile 10.7), this subject's value is above both. GVS and the composite are **summary magnitudes of difference from the reference across the cycle**: neither localizes the difference to a phase of the cycle, and neither indicates the direction of the waveform difference (e.g., more or less flexion) at any point. Within the sagittal channels supplied, the largest per-joint deviation scores are at the knee (21.86) and ankle (16.68), with hip (8.79) and pelvis (5.35) lower; this ordering describes distance from the unmatched reference, not a mechanism or a lesion location.

**ROM values on their own do not establish a clinical restriction** in the absence of an appropriate matched comparison, and no ROM reference values are supplied here. Because no speed-matched healthy reference exists at 0.23 m/s, the elevated composite and per-joint scores cannot be attributed wholly, or in any specified proportion, to stroke pathology; slow-walking effects are inseparably included. These comparisons do not establish a disease effect or support a clinical diagnosis. The same interpretive limits would apply to a control participant compared against this reference.

## 4. Questions for Further Clinical Assessment

These are open questions raised by the limits of the supplied data. None presupposes that the mechanism or measurement issue named has already been observed, and none implies a treatment.

1. **Event timing.** Are individual toe-off and other gait events available in the source release for this participant? Without them, no retained interval above can be placed in stance or swing, or in any named sub-phase, and no timing can be described as early, delayed, or prolonged.
2. **Stride-level data.** Are the individual stride envelopes and a stride-count/variability summary retrievable? The averaged curve cannot show whether the multiple retained intervals in GAS and TA, or the boundary-crossing continuity in six channels, recur in each stride or reflect averaging across differing stride patterns.
3. **Signal quality.** Would a review of raw EMG traces, electrode placement records, and cross-talk checks for the channels with the largest standardized differences — GAS (18.9), ERS (6.3), TA (5.0) — help establish whether a measurement factor contributes, in addition to or instead of a physiological one? The audit note that 86 of 700 channel combinations lacked valid nonzero paired samples in the checked release suggests a per-channel data-integrity check is worth confirming for this subject.
4. **Speed-appropriate comparison.** Is any slow-speed healthy or clinical comparison set available (or could a speed-controlled healthy protocol be referenced) so that deviations at 0.23 m/s can be interpreted against something other than a 0.80–1.79 m/s cohort?
5. **Contralateral and non-sagittal data.** Are non-paretic-side envelopes and kinematics, and coronal/transverse channels, available? The step length asymmetry (−35.9%, successive-footfall) is a between-limb measure, but only paretic-side channels are supplied here; note also that the EMG normalization denominator is side-specific, so cross-side amplitude comparisons would require explicit handling.
6. **Step-length definition sensitivity.** Does the instantaneous-definition symmetry value for this subject differ materially from the successive-footfall value reported, and which definition is preferred for the intended comparison?
7. **Clinical context.** Are age, sex, time post-stroke, and stroke clinical scores available in the release for this participant? They are not in this input, and any physiological interpretation of the findings above would require them alongside a matched examination.
