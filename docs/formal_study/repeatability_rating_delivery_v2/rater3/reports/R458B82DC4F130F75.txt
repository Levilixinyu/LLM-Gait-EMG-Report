RF, VL, BF, ST, TA and ERS have explicitly documented above-threshold continuity across the initial-contact cycle boundary at the **mean-envelope level**. These results do not establish continuous contraction, inactivity outside retained intervals or the same pattern in every stride. No gait-phase function or normal/early/delayed timing can be assigned from these percentages.

Normalization used a shared maximum across available strides for each participant, side and muscle before averaging, without further amplitude normalization here. These are relative envelope amplitudes—not strength, force or directly comparable measures of muscle capacity.

All listed maximum absolute z values occur **above the phase-specific healthy-reference mean** at their respective locations. Those locations mark the largest standardized differences, not necessarily envelope peaks or gait events. These are extreme-value statistics over 1001 points, so a conventional 2-SD rule is inappropriate. For the two supplied muscle-specific calibration values, TA’s 3.3 is below its pooled healthy 95th percentile of 4.61, whereas ERS’s 5.8 exceeds its corresponding value of 3.65.

**Whole-cycle CCI comparisons:**
- **TA–GAS: 0.60**, versus median **0.39** and empirical 2.5th–97.5th percentile interval **0.24–0.59**. The displayed value is just above the upper endpoint.
- **RF–BF: 0.66**, versus median **0.68** and interval **0.48–0.83**. It lies within that interval and below the median.

CCI quantifies whole-cycle overlap of normalized continuous envelopes, including sub-threshold contributions. It is not simultaneous activation time, muscular force or a phase-specific finding, and depends on the envelope and normalization methods.
