2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings
EMG findings are derived from ensemble-averaged envelopes normalized to each muscle's maximum across the available strides. Above-threshold regions are defined by the averaged envelope exceeding 20% of its own peak with a minimum retained interval of 3% of the cycle. 
- BF, ST, and ERS: These envelopes met the threshold criteria for 100% of the gait cycle, maintaining above-threshold activity across the cycle-defining initial contact boundary, with no below-threshold segments detected. Peak normalized amplitudes were 0.52 for BF, 0.60 for ST, and 0.48 for ERS.
- RF: 96% of the cycle was above threshold, consisting of an interval from 59%-56% that wraps across the initial contact boundary. Peak normalized amplitude was 0.46.
- TA: 83% of the cycle was above threshold, with retained intervals of 68%-87% and 90%-54% (wrapping across the initial contact boundary). Peak normalized amplitude was 0.50.
- GAS: 82% of the cycle was above threshold, with discrete intervals of 0%-74%, 78%-82%, and 96%-100%. Peak normalized amplitude was 0.49.
- VL: 69% of the cycle was above threshold, with an interval from 88%-54% that wraps across the initial contact boundary. Peak normalized amplitude was 0.62.

Co-contraction indices (CCI) were calculated using the Falconer-Winter method on the full continuous normalized envelopes, reflecting whole-cycle overlap rather than phase-specific simultaneous activation time:
- TA vs GAS: CCI was 0.59. This falls exactly at the upper endpoint of the supplied healthy-cohort 95% reference interval (median 0.39, 2.5th-97.5th percentile 0.24-0.59).
- RF vs BF: CCI was 0.76. This is within the supplied healthy-cohort 95% reference interval (median 0.68, 2.5th-97.5th percentile 0.48-0.83).
Inclusion within or proximity to an empirical reference interval does not establish physiologically normal co-activation or muscle function, nor does the index represent muscular force. CCI values are strongly dependent on the specific normalization and envelope methods applied.

3. Kinematic and Reference Comparisons
Reference comparisons utilize a pooled dataset of 138 healthy subjects spanning all ages. 
Speed limitation: The participant's gait speed of 0.34 m/s is below the healthy reference cohort's lower bound of 0.80 m/s. Consequently, no speed-matched healthy reference exists in this dataset. Deviations from the reference conflate slow-walking effects with stroke-specific control changes, and the two cannot be separated from these data. These comparisons cannot by themselves establish a disease effect or a clinical diagnosis.
