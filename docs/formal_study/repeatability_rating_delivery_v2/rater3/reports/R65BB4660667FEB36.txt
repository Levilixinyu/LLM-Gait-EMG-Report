All seven mean envelopes are reported above threshold across the initial-contact cycle boundary. GAS, BF, and ERS have explicitly reported full-cycle above-threshold intervals. RF intervals are preserved as supplied rather than merged from their displayed endpoints.

These are **mean-envelope threshold findings**, not physiological onset, offset, contraction duration, or evidence of inactivity outside retained intervals. They do not establish the same pattern in every stride. Without individual events and matched muscle-specific timing references, no interval can be assigned a gait-phase function or characterized as early, delayed, or prolonged.

Maximum |z| identifies the largest standardized reference difference across 1001 cycle points—not necessarily peak envelope amplitude or a gait event. “Above” and “below” refer to the phase-specific reference mean at the listed location. Among the muscle-specific calibration percentiles supplied, ERS 6.5 exceeds the pooled 95th percentile of 3.65, while TA 3.8 is below 4.61. A conventional |z| > 2 rule is not appropriate for these extreme-value statistics.

**Whole-cycle envelope overlap:**

- **TA–GAS CCI: 0.59**, compared with median 0.39 and empirical interval 0.24–0.59. It equals the displayed upper endpoint; rounding does not resolve its exact unrounded position relative to that endpoint.
- **RF–BF CCI: 0.63**, below median 0.68 and within the supplied empirical interval 0.48–0.83.

CCI is a whole-cycle overlap index of the full normalized envelopes, including subthreshold contributions. It is not simultaneous activation time, force, or a phase-specific result. These method-dependent values do not establish physiologically normal or pathological co-activation.
