## 1. Objective Findings

**Participant and cycle definition.** This report covers one participant in the stroke group, with the paretic limb analyzed. The released cycle runs from an initial contact of that limb (0%) to the next initial contact of the same limb (100%); these are two successive occurrences of that boundary, not a single instant. No intermediate gait events (including individual toe-off) are supplied in this input, so all timing below is expressed strictly as a percentage of the analysis cycle. Percentages are not converted into stance/swing or any named sub-phase, and no conventional split is substituted.

**Spatiotemporal summary (as supplied).**
- Gait speed 0.68 m/s, which falls in the labelled band "limited community ambulator (0.4–0.8 m/s)." This label denotes a speed band only; it is not a measurement of walking independence, safety, or real-world community mobility.
- Cadence 90.8 steps/min.
- Step length symmetry index +5.7%, computed with the successive-footfall definition (each heel at its own initial contact). By the stated sign convention, a negative value would indicate a shorter paretic step; this positive value indicates the paretic step is the longer of the two by this index. The alternative instantaneous definition can yield a different value in some subjects, so this number should be read as definition-specific.
- Composite sagittal kinematic deviation score ("GPS-like") 9.77 — the RMS of the four supplied sagittal Gait Variable Scores, not the validated 9-variable GPS.

**Reference used and its limits.** The kinematic deviation reference for this subject is 138 pooled healthy subjects over 1001 phase points (source: main_cohort_excel); the pooled cohort is not age- or speed-matched to this participant. The healthy reference cohort's self-selected speeds span 0.80–1.79 m/s, and this participant walks at 0.68 m/s — **below that floor, so no speed-matched healthy reference exists in this dataset.** Any deviation reported below therefore conflates slow-walking effects with stroke-related control changes, and these data cannot separate the two. Distance from the reference is a summary difference from an empirical comparison group; it does not by itself establish a disease effect, a mechanism, or a clinical diagnosis.

**Channels available and not available.** Seven EMG envelope channels are supplied (GAS, RF, VL, BF, ST, TA, ERS) plus four sagittal kinematic channels (ankle, knee, hip, pelvis) and two CCI pairs. Not supplied in this input: individual toe-off and other event timings, matched muscle-specific normal timing references, stride-level variability, raw-signal quality checks, expert plots, coronal and transverse plane kinematics (excluded by the metric definitions), non-paretic-side data, age, sex, time post-stroke, and stroke clinical scores. These are absent from *this summary*; that is not evidence that they are absent from the source release. No channel is documented here as a failed or absent recording.

## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

**How these numbers must be read.** All EMG values come from ensemble-averaged envelopes. Normalization uses a shared maximum across the available strides before averaging, with the denominator specific to participant, side, and muscle in the stroke release and not shared between sides; the released mean curves are used without further amplitude normalization. Amplitudes are therefore **relative envelope values only** — not neural drive, force, or strength — and muscles must not be ranked as stronger or weaker from them. An averaged curve cannot demonstrate stride-to-stride variability, so the pattern described here is a property of the mean envelope and should not be assumed to recur in every individual stride. Detected intervals are regions where the averaged envelope exceeds 20% of that same curve's own peak for at least 3% of the cycle; they are not physiological onsets, offsets, contraction durations, or proof of inactivity elsewhere. Coverage values are rounded, so a stated percentage below 100% does not by itself establish where or whether sub-threshold segments occurred. An interval written X%–Y% with X > Y continues across the 100%→0% boundary, i.e., the mean envelope remains above threshold across the cycle-defining initial-contact boundary.

Six of seven averaged envelopes (RF, VL, BF, ST, TA, ERS) remain above their own 20% threshold across the cycle-defining boundary. The GAS envelope's single retained interval begins at 4%, numerically close to the 0% initial-contact boundary but not at the analysis-window edge. RF shows the highest coverage (91%) and BF the most fragmented set of retained intervals (three). None of these descriptions is converted here into a functional role such as clearance, weight acceptance, or push-off; anatomically, GAS acts as an ankle plantar flexor, TA as a dorsiflexor, RF/VL as knee extensors (RF also a hip flexor), BF/ST as knee flexors and hip extensors, and ERS as a trunk extensor — but no interval is mapped to a gait-phase function, and no timing here is labelled normal, early, delayed, or prolonged, since the matched muscle-specific timing references required for such a label are not supplied.

**Max |z| interpretation.** Each max |z| is an extreme-value statistic: the single largest standardized difference from the phase-specific reference mean across all 1001 phase points. Its location marks where that standardized difference is largest, **not** necessarily where the envelope peaks and not a gait event. "Above reference" means above the phase-specific reference *mean* at that point, not above a healthy percentile limit. The conventional 2-SD rule does not apply: in this dataset every one of 109 healthy controls exceeds |z| = 2 on at least one muscle, and per-muscle healthy 95th percentiles for this statistic range from 3.65 (ERS) to 4.61 (TA), higher still under leave-one-out. Against those stated endpoints: BF (3.1) and TA (3.3) lie below the lowest listed 95th percentile and below TA's own 4.61, respectively; ST (5.6), ERS (5.8, versus ERS's own 3.65), RF (7.4), VL (8.3), and especially GAS (20.3) exceed the upper end of that percentile range. Exceeding these pooled calibration percentiles is a descriptive statement about distance from an unmatched, non-speed-matched reference; it does not establish abnormality, pathology, or a diagnosis, and the leave-one-out limits would be more permissive still.

**Co-contraction indices.** CCI here is the Falconer–Winter index computed over the full continuous envelopes (2 × common area / summed areas) for the whole cycle. It is a whole-cycle overlap index of normalized envelopes — not a duration of simultaneous activation, not muscular force, and not a phase-specific result — and it can be non-zero even where detected bursts do not overlap, because sub-threshold activity contributes. It is also dependent on this normalization and envelope pipeline, so values and thresholds from other studies or algorithms are not transferable.
- **TA vs GAS = 0.60**, against the supplied healthy median 0.39 (2.5th–97.5th pct 0.24–0.59). This value sits just outside the upper endpoint of that empirical interval, by 0.01.
- **RF vs BF = 0.66**, against the supplied healthy median 0.68 (0.48–0.83). This value lies inside the interval, slightly below the median.

Being inside the interval does not establish physiologically normal co-activation or normal muscle function; being marginally outside it does not establish pathological co-activation, spasticity, stiffness, impaired motor control, or any diagnosis — particularly given that no speed-matched reference exists for this participant.

A dataset-level audit note is recorded for context: the local array audit verified 614 evaluable muscle/phase combinations of 700, with 86 lacking valid nonzero paired samples. This is a property of the released dataset audit as stated and is not a statement about which of this participant's channels were or were not evaluable.

The participant's composite lies above the reference median and below the stated 95th percentile. Because 9.77 and 10.7 are both rounded, values near a boundary should not be used to classify a hidden unrounded value.

**What GVS and the composite do and do not convey.** Each GVS is a summary magnitude of difference from the stated reference over the cycle; neither a GVS nor the composite localizes *when* in the cycle the difference occurs, in which direction the waveform differs, or which phase is involved. The ankle (13.71) and knee (12.00) channels carry the largest summary differences from the reference, and the hip (4.94) and pelvis (5.08) the smallest, but this ordering describes distance from a pooled reference curve, not a mechanism.

**ROM.** The reported ROM values are descriptive ranges for this participant. ROM alone does not establish a clinical restriction in the absence of an appropriate matched comparison, and no ROM-specific normative limits are supplied here.

**Comparability caveat (applies to every comparison above and in Section 2).** The reference is a pooled healthy group of 138 subjects, not age- or speed-matched; this participant's 0.68 m/s is below the healthy cohort's 0.80 m/s floor, so slow-walking effects and stroke-related changes are confounded and cannot be separated from these data. A speed falling inside some overall range would not by itself prove that a suitable matched reference exists, and these pooled calibration percentiles are not leave-one-out matched normal limits. Accordingly, no finding in this report establishes a disease effect, a severity grade, statistical significance, or a clinical diagnosis. The same limits would apply to a control participant: healthy-group membership would not prove normal physiology, just as deviation from this reference does not prove disease.

## 4. Questions for Further Clinical Assessment

These are open questions for the referring clinician or study team. None presupposes that the mechanism or measurement issue named has already been observed in these data, and none is a treatment recommendation.

1. **Matched reference.** Can an age- and speed-matched (or speed-stratified) healthy subset, or a leave-one-out reference, be constructed for a participant walking at 0.68 m/s — and if not, how should the deviation scores and max |z| values be qualified in the study report?
2. **Event data.** Are individual toe-off and other gait-event timings available elsewhere in the release for this participant? Without them, no interval in Section 2 can be assigned to a gait phase or assessed for timing normality.
3. **Signal provenance.** Are raw-signal quality checks, electrode placement records, or crosstalk assessments available for the paretic-side channels — particularly for GAS, whose max |z| of 20.3 is far above the other channels? Whether an amplitude-normalization or signal-quality factor contributes is an open question that this summary cannot answer.
4. **Normalization effects.** Given that the stroke-release denominator is participant-, side-, and muscle-specific and not shared between sides, how should relative amplitudes and CCI values be handled if any between-side or between-subject comparison is planned?
5. **Stride-level behaviour.** Can per-stride envelopes or a variability measure be retrieved, so that the mean-envelope pattern described here (including the boundary-crossing coverage in six of seven muscles) can be checked against individual strides?
6. **Step-length definition.** Would the instantaneous step-length definition change the +5.7% symmetry index for this participant, and which definition does the study protocol treat as primary?
7. **Clinical context.** Are time post-stroke, stroke clinical scores, age, sex, and non-paretic-side recordings available in the release for this participant? None is supplied here, and their status (not supplied versus not recorded) should be confirmed before any clinical correlation is attempted.
8. **Out-of-plane motion.** Is coronal/transverse kinematic data available? Sagittal-only ROM and GVS cannot address out-of-plane contributions to the ankle and knee deviation scores.
9. **CCI context.** Should the TA-vs-GAS value of 0.60, sitting 0.01 above the stated 97.5th-percentile endpoint, be re-evaluated against a speed-matched subset before it is carried into any group-level analysis?
