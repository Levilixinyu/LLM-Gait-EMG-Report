RF and ERS have reported full-cycle retained intervals and explicit above-threshold boundary continuity. VL, BF, ST and TA have wrapping intervals that remain above threshold across the 100%→0% boundary. This boundary corresponds to successive paretic initial contacts.

These are **mean-envelope threshold findings**, not physiological onset, offset, continuous contraction or evidence of inactivity outside retained intervals. They do not establish the same pattern in each stride. Without intermediate gait events and matched timing references, the intervals cannot be assigned gait-phase functions or characterized as normal, early, delayed or prolonged.

Normalization uses a participant-, side- and muscle-specific maximum shared across available strides before averaging, without further amplitude normalization here. Peak values therefore describe relative envelope amplitude—not force, strength or absolute neural activation—and cannot establish stride-to-stride variability.

The maximum \|z\| location identifies the largest standardized difference from the phase-specific reference mean, not necessarily peak envelope amplitude or a gait event. “Above” and “below” refer to that mean at the stated location. Of the supplied muscle-specific 95th-percentile anchors, ERS 4.7 exceeds 3.65, whereas TA 2.3 is below 4.61. These are across-cycle extreme-value comparisons; a conventional \|z\| > 2 abnormality rule is inappropriate.

**Whole-cycle CCI:**
- **TA–GAS:** 0.43, above the supplied median of 0.39 and within the empirical interval 0.24–0.59.
- **RF–BF:** 0.77, above the supplied median of 0.68 and within the empirical interval 0.48–0.83.

CCI measures overlap of the full normalized envelopes, including sub-threshold contributions. It is not simultaneous activation time, muscular force or a phase-specific result. These within-interval values do not establish physiologically normal co-activation or muscle function; interpretation depends on the supplied normalization and envelope method.
