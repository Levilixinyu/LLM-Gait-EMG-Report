**1. Objective Findings**
Participant Sub22_P was analyzed as part of the stroke group, with findings generated from the paretic side. The analyzed gait cycle is defined by successive initial contacts (0% and 100%). Gait speed was 0.45 m/s with a cadence of 78.8 steps/min. Step length symmetry index, using the successive-footfall definition, was 5.7%. The composite kinematic deviation score (GPS-like, representing sagittal channels) was 15.30.

**2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings**
EMG findings are derived from ensemble-averaged, normalized envelopes evaluated against a threshold of 20% of each muscle's peak amplitude, with a minimum duration of 3% of the cycle. Detected above-threshold intervals describe the mean envelope properties; they do not represent physiological onsets or offsets, and they do not confirm stride-to-stride consistency. Portions of the cycle not meeting these detection criteria do not signify muscular inactivity. Seven sagittal muscles were assessed, with no missing channels. 

- The ERS average envelope maintained 100% coverage, remaining above threshold across the entire cycle and the initial contact boundary (0%-100%). Peak normalized amplitude was 0.64.
- BF and ST showed 80% and 73% cycle coverage, respectively. Both demonstrated above-threshold activity wrapping across the initial contact boundary, with detected intervals of 59%-38% for BF and 62%-35% for ST.
- VL and RF mean envelopes also wrapped the initial contact boundary. VL presented intervals at 69%-81% and 91%-54% (75% total coverage), while RF presented a single interval from 88%-53% (68% total coverage). 
- TA demonstrated 77% above-threshold coverage in a single interval wrapping the boundary from 61%-37%.
- GAS demonstrated above-threshold coverage for 60% of the cycle across three intervals: 0%-13%, 14%-19%, and 31%-64%. 

Co-contraction indices (CCI), calculated as a whole-cycle overlap index of the continuous normalized envelopes, were 0.56 for TA vs. GAS and 0.71 for RF vs. BF. 

**3. Kinematic and Reference Comparisons**
Participant kinematics and EMG were compared to a pooled cohort of 138 healthy controls. Because the participant's gait speed (0.45 m/s) is below the self-selected speed range of the healthy reference group (0.80-1.79 m/s), no speed-matched reference exists in this dataset. Deviations from the empirical reference conflate the mechanical effects of slow walking with stroke-specific control changes, and these factors cannot be separated using the supplied data. Deviations from this reference do not inherently establish disease or pathology.

- The participant's composite kinematic deviation score (15.30) is higher than the healthy cohort median (5.1) and 95th percentile (10.7).
- Sagittal joint ranges of motion (ROM) and summary differences from the reference (Gait Variable Scores, GVS) were: Hip ROM 30.2 degrees (GVS 15.64); Knee ROM 27.0 degrees (GVS 15.32); Ankle ROM 24.6 degrees (GVS 18.23); and Pelvis ROM 3.0 degrees (GVS 11.16).
- The CCI for TA vs. GAS (0.56) falls near the upper limit of the empirical healthy reference interval (median 0.39, 2.5th-97.5th percentiles 0.24-0.59). The RF vs. BF CCI (0.71) is within the empirical reference interval (median 0.68, 2.5th-97.5th percentiles 0.48-0.83). These relative proximities do not verify clinically normal or pathological co-activation.
- Maximum absolute z-scores (max |z|) represent the largest standardized difference from the phase-specific reference mean; they do not indicate the time of peak envelope amplitude. Findings were: GAS 10.1 (above reference mean at 57% of the cycle); ERS 5.8 (above reference at 80%); VL 4.5 (above reference at 23%); TA 2.5 (above reference at 18%); RF 2.5 (above reference at 20%); BF 2.3 (above reference at 10%); and ST 2.3 (below reference at 0%).

Metric definitions (read before interpreting any number below):
- All joint angles and ROM values are SAGITTAL PLANE only. Coronal/transverse data are not included.
- The released EMG curves are ensemble-averaged envelopes. For each participant and recorded muscle, normalization uses a shared maximum across the available strides before averaging; individual strides are not independently rescaled to their own peaks. This pipeline uses the released mean curves without further amplitude normalization. In the checked stroke release, the denominator is participant-, side-, and muscle-specific; it is not shared between sides. The local array audit verified 614 evaluable combinations, not all 700; 86 lacked valid nonzero paired samples. Amplitudes are relative envelope values, not absolute neural activation, force, or strength. Do not rank muscles as stronger or weaker from these amplitudes, or infer stride-to-stride variability from an average curve.
- Above-threshold intervals are the regions where the averaged envelope exceeds 20% of that curve's own peak, minimum retained interval 3% of the cycle (by sample count). An interval written as (X%-Y%) with X > Y wraps across the 100%->0% cycle boundary (envelope remains above threshold across the cycle-defining event). An onset of 0% is the analysis-window boundary, not necessarily a physiological onset. "No interval detected" means no region met the threshold + duration criteria; it does NOT mean the muscle is inactive or non-functional.
- CCI is the Falconer-Winter co-contraction index computed on the full continuous envelopes (2*common area / summed areas), so it can be non-zero even when the DETECTED burst intervals of the pair do not overlap (sub-threshold activity contributes).
- The composite kinematic deviation score is the RMS of the per-joint Gait Variable Scores over the sagittal channels provided ("GPS-like", not the validated 9-variable GPS).
- Step length symmetry index: 200*(paretic - non-paretic)/(paretic + non-paretic), in %. NEGATIVE means the paretic-side step is SHORTER. Two operational step-length definitions exist (successive-footfall: each heel's position at its own initial contact; instantaneous: both heels at the same instant) and give different values for some subjects -- the definition actually used is stated next to the symmetry value.
- "Max |z| deviation" is the LARGEST absolute z-score across all 1001 phase points of the cycle, comparing this curve to the healthy-cohort mean/SD at each phase. This is an extreme-value statistic, NOT a single-observation z-score: in this dataset every one of 109 healthy controls exceeds |z| = 2 on at least one muscle, and per-muscle healthy 95th percentiles for this statistic run 3.65 (ERS) to 4.61 (TA) -- higher still under leave-one-out. Do not label a value abnormal by the conventional 2-SD rule; use the healthy reference range given per muscle where provided.
- Healthy-cohort CCI reference (this dataset): RF-vs-BF median 0.68 (2.5th-97.5th pct 0.48-0.83); TA-vs-GAS median 0.39 (0.24-0.59). Composite score reference: median 5.1, 95th percentile 10.7.
