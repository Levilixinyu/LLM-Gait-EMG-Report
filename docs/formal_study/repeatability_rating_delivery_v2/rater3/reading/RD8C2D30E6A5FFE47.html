## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

**How these numbers must be read.** The curves are **ensemble-averaged envelopes**. Normalization uses a shared maximum across available strides before averaging, with a participant-, side- and muscle-specific denominator (not shared between sides). Amplitudes are therefore **relative envelope values** — not absolute neural activation, force, or strength — and cannot be used to rank muscles as stronger or weaker, or to compare this side against the other. Detected intervals are properties of the averaged envelope under the stated rule (envelope exceeds 20% of that curve's own peak; minimum retained interval 3% of the cycle). They are **not** physiological onsets, offsets, contraction durations, or evidence of inactivity outside the interval, and the pattern of a mean curve cannot be assumed to recur in every individual stride. An interval written X%–Y% with X > Y wraps the 100%→0% boundary.

**Description.** All seven averaged envelopes remain above their own 20%-of-peak threshold across the cycle-defining initial-contact boundary, each yielding a single retained wrapping interval. Coverage of the mean envelope ranges from 63% (BF) to 100% (ERS). For ERS the single interval spans the full analysis window (0%–100%); the 0% onset is the analysis-window boundary rather than a physiological onset. For ERS, 100% coverage with one boundary-to-boundary interval does indicate that the supplied averaged curve did not fall below its own relative threshold within this window. For the remaining muscles, the reported coverages below 100% correspond to regions of the mean curve outside the retained interval; these regions are sub-threshold relative to that curve's own peak, which is a statement about the averaged envelope and the 20% criterion only — not about muscle inactivity, and not necessarily reproduced stride by stride. Note also that a rounded coverage figure alone would not license a claim that no sub-threshold segment exists.

Peak normalized amplitudes cluster between 0.41 (ERS) and 0.63 (VL). Because each curve is scaled to its own participant/side/muscle-specific maximum, these values are not comparable across muscles as measures of output.

**Max |z| values.** These are extreme-value statistics — the largest absolute standardized difference across all 1001 phase points versus the phase-specific healthy mean/SD — not single-observation z-scores. The conventional 2-SD rule does not apply: in this dataset every one of 109 healthy controls exceeds |z| = 2 on at least one muscle, and per-muscle healthy 95th percentiles for this statistic run 3.65 (ERS) to 4.61 (TA), higher still under leave-one-out. Against those supplied per-muscle percentiles, RF (4.1) and TA (3.9) sit **below** their respective muscle's healthy 95th percentile figure where given for TA (4.61); ERS at 2.5 sits below the ERS 95th percentile of 3.65. BF at 5.1 is the largest value in this set. Comparable per-muscle 95th-percentile values for GAS, RF, VL, BF and ST are not individually supplied here, only the 3.65–4.61 range across muscles, so muscle-specific classification of those values is not possible from this input. The **location** of each max |z| marks where the standardized difference from the reference is greatest — it is not necessarily the time of peak envelope amplitude and does not identify a gait event. "Above reference" means above the phase-specific reference mean at that location, not above a healthy percentile limit. Six channels show their maximum deviation above the reference mean; ERS shows it below. Because no speed-matched reference exists for this participant, none of these differences can be attributed to stroke-specific control changes rather than slow walking.

**Co-contraction indices.** CCI here is the Falconer–Winter index computed on the full continuous normalized envelopes over the whole cycle (2·common area / summed areas). It is a whole-cycle overlap index of envelope shape — **not** simultaneous activation time, not muscular force, and not a phase-specific result. It can be non-zero even when detected intervals do not overlap, because sub-threshold activity contributes.

- **TA vs GAS: 0.54.** The supplied healthy reference for this pair is median 0.39 (2.5th–97.5th pct 0.24–0.59). This value lies within that interval, in its upper portion and moderately close to the 0.59 upper endpoint.
- **RF vs BF: 0.73.** Healthy reference median 0.68 (0.48–0.83). This value lies within the interval, above the median.

Falling inside these empirical intervals does not establish physiologically normal co-activation or normal muscle function, just as falling outside would not establish pathological co-activation, stiffness or impaired motor control. CCI values depend on the normalization and envelope pipeline used here and must not be compared with values or thresholds from other studies or algorithms.

It is a magnitude only: it does not indicate the direction of the waveform difference (greater or lesser flexion/extension), does not localize the difference to any part of the cycle, and does not identify a phase or event. Among the four channels the knee carries the largest summary difference (12.84), followed by ankle (8.94), hip (7.74) and pelvis (4.43); per-joint healthy reference limits are not supplied in this input, so these individual GVS values cannot be classified against normal limits here.

The small pelvic sagittal ROM (1.3°) is reported as measured; interpreting it would require both a matched reference and information about marker/model handling that is not part of this input.

**Comparability limits.** The composite reference percentiles are **pooled calibration values**, not matched leave-one-out normal limits, and the pooled cohort is not age- or speed-matched to this participant.
