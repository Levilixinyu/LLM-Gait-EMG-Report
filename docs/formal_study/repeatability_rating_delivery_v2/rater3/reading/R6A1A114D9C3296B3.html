## 1. Objective Findings

The analysis cycle runs from initial contact of that limb (0%) to its next initial contact (100%); these are two successive occurrences of the same boundary event, not one instant. No intermediate gait events (including toe-off) are supplied in this input, so all timing below is expressed purely as a percentage of the analysis cycle and is **not** assigned to stance, swing, loading response, pre-swing, mid-stance, or any other phase.

## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

**How these numbers were produced.** All EMG values are properties of an **ensemble-averaged envelope** per muscle, normalized to a participant-, side-, and muscle-specific shared maximum across available strides (individual strides are not rescaled to their own peaks). Amplitudes are therefore **relative envelope values** — they are not neural drive, force, or strength, and muscles must not be ranked as stronger or weaker from them. An average curve cannot demonstrate stride-to-stride consistency: the patterns below describe the mean envelope, not every individual stride. "Above-threshold interval" means the averaged envelope exceeded **20% of that curve's own peak** for at least **3% of the cycle**; these are algorithmic threshold crossings, not physiological onsets, offsets, contraction durations, or evidence of inactivity elsewhere.

**Seven muscles are reported** (GAS, RF, VL, BF, ST, TA, ERS). All seven show an interval that **wraps the 100%→0% boundary**, i.e., the mean envelope remained above its 20% threshold across the cycle-defining initial-contact boundary.

**Reading of the interval structure.** Several channels show **fragmented** retained intervals with short gaps between them (e.g., BF at approximately 46–48%, 51–52% and 62–63%; RF at 49–51%, 56–82%, 85–90% and 94–97%; ST at 44–48% and 61–77%). At the mean-envelope level these gaps are regions that did not meet the threshold-plus-duration criterion; they do not establish that the muscle was electrically silent, nor that the same fragmentation occurred in every stride. Coverage values are high across the board (64%–100%).

For **ERS**, the single retained interval spans the entire analysis window (0%–100%) with 100% coverage, which is consistent with the averaged envelope remaining above its own 20% threshold throughout the cycle. For all other muscles, including **TA at 98%**, the reported coverage is a rounded summary value and does not by itself establish that no below-threshold segment existed; where gaps are explicitly implied by the interval endpoints (e.g., TA between roughly 7% and 10%), those are the documented sub-threshold regions of the mean curve. Anatomically, these are ankle plantarflexor (GAS), dorsiflexor (TA), knee extensor (RF, VL), knee flexor/hip extensor (BF, ST) and erector spinae channels; no interval here is assigned a gait-phase function such as clearance, push-off or weight acceptance, and no timing is characterized as normal, early, delayed or prolonged, because the matched event and timing references required for that are not supplied.

**Max |z| interpretation.** Max |z| is an **extreme-value statistic** — the largest standardized difference from the phase-specific reference mean across all 1001 phase points — not a single-observation z-score, and the conventional 2-SD rule does not apply. In this dataset every one of 109 healthy controls exceeds |z| = 2 on at least one muscle, and per-muscle healthy 95th percentiles for this statistic span **3.65 (ERS) to 4.61 (TA)**. Against that context:
- **GAS (6.5)** exceeds the upper end of that entire per-muscle 95th-percentile span, making it the clearest standardized difference from the reference in this record.
- **RF (3.8)** and **ST (3.7)** fall inside the 3.65–4.61 band of per-muscle 95th percentiles; without the specific 95th-percentile value for those two muscles, they cannot be classified either way.
- **TA (3.4)**, **VL (3.0)**, **BF (2.2)** and **ERS (2.2)** fall at or below the lower end of that band (and TA and ERS below their own stated or bounding percentiles).

The location of each max |z| marks where the standardized difference from the reference was largest — **not** necessarily where envelope amplitude peaked and not a gait event. "Above reference" / "below reference" means relative to the phase-specific reference **mean** at that location, not relative to a healthy percentile limit. All of these comparisons inherit the speed and matching limitations described in Section 1.

**Co-contraction indices.** CCI here is the Falconer–Winter index computed over the **full continuous envelopes across the whole cycle** (2 × common area / summed areas). It is a whole-cycle overlap index of normalized envelopes — not simultaneous activation time, not muscular force, not a phase-specific result — and it can be non-zero even where detected burst intervals do not overlap, because sub-threshold activity contributes.
- **TA vs GAS = 0.71**, above the upper bound of the supplied healthy 2.5th–97.5th percentile interval of 0.24–0.59 (median 0.39).
- **RF vs BF = 0.72**, inside the supplied healthy interval of 0.48–0.83 and modestly above the supplied median of 0.68.

Being outside the empirical interval (TA–GAS) does **not** establish pathological co-activation, spasticity, stiffness, impaired motor control or any diagnosis; being inside it (RF–BF) does **not** establish physiologically normal co-activation or normal muscle function. CCI values depend on the normalization and envelope pipeline used here and must not be compared with thresholds or values from other studies or algorithms.

7. **Frontal/transverse motion.** Given that only sagittal channels exist here, is three-dimensional kinematic data available or obtainable, and is out-of-plane motion a clinically relevant question for this participant?
8. **Additional co-contraction context.** Should CCI be recomputed on windows of interest or with alternative normalization, would the pipeline dependence of the index be documented so that values are not compared across methods?
