## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

**How these numbers must be read.** The curves are ensemble-averaged envelopes. Normalization used a shared maximum across available strides per participant, side and muscle (in the checked stroke release the denominator is participant-, side- and muscle-specific and is not shared between sides); individual strides were not rescaled to their own peaks, and no further amplitude normalization was applied. Amplitudes are therefore **relative envelope values** — not neural drive, force or strength — and muscles must not be ranked as stronger or weaker from them. Detected intervals are regions where the *averaged* envelope exceeds 20% of its own peak for at least 3% of the cycle. They are properties of that averaged curve under that rule, not physiological onsets, offsets, contraction durations or proof of inactivity elsewhere; they also do not imply that every individual stride followed the same pattern, and an average curve cannot show stride-to-stride variability.

**Boundary continuity.** For RF, VL, BF, ST, TA and ERS the averaged envelope remained above its 20% threshold across the 0%/100% cycle-defining boundary; this is a continuity property of the mean envelope at that boundary and is the only event-related statement the data support. GAS is the one channel whose detected interval (9%–53%) lies wholly within the cycle without wrapping; its onset value is a detection result under the stated rule, not a physiological onset.

**Coverage.** Coverage values range from 30% (VL) to 95% (ERS). High coverage means a large fraction of the averaged curve sat above that curve's own 20% peak-relative threshold; it does not establish continuous contraction. Equally, the complement of coverage is not established as sub-threshold in every stride, and the supplied evidence does not establish that any channel had *no* below-threshold segment. RF shows four retained intervals, of which one wraps the boundary; TA shows two. Muscle actions may be named anatomically (e.g., TA as an ankle dorsiflexor, GAS as a plantarflexor, RF/VL as knee extensors, BF/ST as knee flexors/hip extensors, ERS as an erector spinae channel), but no interval here can be mapped to a functional role such as clearance, push-off or weight acceptance, and no interval can be called normal, early, delayed or prolonged, because matched muscle-specific timing references and individual events are not supplied.

**Max |z| values.** Each max |z| is an extreme-value statistic — the largest standardized difference across all 1001 phase points — not a single-observation z-score, and the conventional 2-SD rule does not apply. In this dataset every one of 109 healthy controls exceeds |z| = 2 on at least one muscle, and per-muscle healthy 95th percentiles for this statistic run 3.65 (ERS) to 4.61 (TA), higher still under leave-one-out. Against that background, the values of 2.0–2.3 for GAS, VL, BF, ST and TA, and 3.5 for RF, are below the lowest per-muscle 95th percentile supplied (3.65) and are not distinguishable from the healthy calibration range on this statistic. **ERS at 6.8 exceeds the ERS healthy 95th percentile of 3.65**; this is the largest standardized difference from the pooled reference in this subject's data, located at 90% of the cycle. That location marks where the standardized difference was greatest — not necessarily where the envelope peaked and not a gait event. "Above reference" and "below reference" here mean relative to the phase-specific reference mean at that location, not relative to a healthy percentile limit. For TA, the largest standardized difference was *below* the reference mean at 72%. None of these values, individually or together, establishes pathology, and the ERS value in particular cannot be separated from the limited comparability of a pooled, unmatched reference (including possible electrode-placement and normalization differences for a trunk channel).

**Co-contraction indices.** CCI is the Falconer–Winter index computed on the full continuous normalized envelopes (2 × common area / summed areas) over the whole cycle. It is not simultaneous activation time, not muscular force, and not phase-specific; it can be non-zero even when detected bursts do not overlap, because sub-threshold envelope area contributes. It also depends on the normalization and envelope pipeline used here, so values and thresholds from other studies or algorithms are not transferable.
- **TA vs GAS = 0.38**, essentially at the healthy median of 0.39 and within the supplied 2.5th–97.5th percentile interval 0.24–0.59.
- **RF vs BF = 0.71**, above the healthy median of 0.68 and within the supplied interval 0.48–0.83.

Falling inside these empirical intervals does not establish physiologically normal co-activation or normal muscle function; falling outside would not establish pathological co-activation, stiffness or impaired motor control.
