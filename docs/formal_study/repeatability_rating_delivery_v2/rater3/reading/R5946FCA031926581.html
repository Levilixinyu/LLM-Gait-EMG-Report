## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

**How to read these numbers.** All EMG results are properties of an **ensemble-averaged envelope** under a stated rule: regions where that curve exceeds **20% of its own peak**, with a minimum retained interval of **3% of the cycle**. In this stroke release the normalization denominator is participant-, side-, and muscle-specific and is *not* shared between sides; amplitudes are therefore **relative envelope values**. They do not indicate neural drive, force, or strength, they cannot be used to rank muscles against each other as stronger or weaker, and an averaged curve cannot establish what happened on any individual stride. "No interval detected" would mean the threshold + duration criteria were not met, not that a muscle was inactive; conversely, a retained interval is not a physiological onset, offset, or continuous contraction. An onset written as 0% is the analysis-window boundary.

**Detected intervals and coverage (mean-envelope level):**

|---|---|---|---|---|

| RF | 59%–56% (wraps boundary) | 96% | 0.46 | 3.2 above reference, at 38% |

**Description.** BF, ST and ERS are reported as above threshold across the entire averaged cycle including the cycle-defining boundary (100% coverage). RF, VL and TA are each flagged as above threshold across the cycle boundary, with coverage of 96%, 69% and 83% respectively; for these the mean envelope drops below threshold, or fails the 3%-duration rule, over the remaining portions (e.g. TA between the two retained intervals, and VL over the segment not covered by 88%–54%). GAS is listed as three separate intervals totalling 82% coverage, **without** a boundary-continuity flag in this summary, so continuity across the 100%→0% transition should not be asserted for GAS from the supplied evidence. For every muscle with coverage below 100%, the non-covered portions are regions where the *averaged* curve did not meet threshold-plus-duration; this does not establish sub-threshold activity or silence in individual strides. Conversely, for the 100%-coverage muscles the supplied evidence does establish that no segment of the mean envelope met the below-threshold criterion.

Because no toe-off or phase boundary is supplied, none of these percentages can be assigned to stance, swing, loading, pre-swing or push-off, and a conventional 60% split must not be substituted. Anatomically these are the triceps surae (GAS), quadriceps (RF, VL), hamstrings (BF, ST), ankle dorsiflexor (TA) and erector spinae (ERS), but no interval here can be mapped to a gait-phase function such as clearance, weight acceptance or propulsion. Nothing in this summary supports labelling any timing as normal, early, delayed or prolonged.

**Max |z| interpretation.** Max |z| is an **extreme-value statistic** — the largest standardized difference across all 1001 phase points — not a single-observation z-score, and the conventional 2-SD rule does not apply. In this dataset every one of 109 healthy controls exceeds |z| = 2 on at least one muscle, and per-muscle healthy 95th percentiles for this statistic run from **3.65 (ERS) to 4.61 (TA)**, higher still under leave-one-out. Against those supplied anchors:
- **GAS 16.8** is far above the entire 3.65–4.61 band — by a wide margin the largest standardized difference in this record.
- **VL 5.4** and **TA 5.1** exceed the upper end of that band (TA above its own stated 4.61 percentile).
- **ERS 3.9** exceeds its own stated 95th percentile of 3.65.
- **BF 3.9** and **ST 3.9** fall inside the 3.65–4.61 band; their muscle-specific percentiles are not individually supplied, so their standing cannot be resolved here.
- **RF 3.2** is below the lower end of the band.

"Above reference" in each row means the curve lies above the **phase-specific reference mean at that location** — not above a healthy percentile limit. The location of max |z| (e.g. 57% for GAS) marks the largest standardized difference, **not** necessarily the time of peak envelope amplitude and not a gait event. These are pooled, unmatched, non-speed-matched percentiles, not leave-one-out matched normal limits, and they do not establish disease.

**Co-contraction indices.** CCI here is the Falconer–Winter index computed over the **full continuous envelopes across the whole cycle** (2 × common area / summed areas). It is a whole-cycle overlap index of normalized envelopes — not a duration of simultaneous activation, not muscular force, and not a phase-specific result. It can be non-zero even where detected bursts do not overlap, because sub-threshold envelope area contributes.
- **TA vs GAS = 0.59**, which sits exactly at the upper endpoint of the supplied healthy 2.5th–97.5th percentile interval (0.24–0.59) and well above the supplied median of 0.39.
- **RF vs BF = 0.76**, inside the supplied interval (0.48–0.83) and above the supplied median of 0.68, nearer the upper endpoint.

Being inside these intervals does not establish physiologically normal co-activation or normal muscle function; being at or outside an endpoint does not establish pathological co-activation, spasticity, stiffness, impaired motor control, or any diagnosis. These values are specific to this normalization and envelope pipeline and must not be compared with CCI values or thresholds from other studies or algorithms. The same unmatched-reference and speed limitations described in Section 1 apply to this comparison.

5. **Stride-level behaviour.** Can single-stride envelopes or a variability measure be obtained? The released averaged curves cannot indicate whether the pattern described in Section 2 recurs in every stride or reflects averaging across dissimilar strides.
6. **Contralateral and out-of-plane data.** Are non-paretic-side recordings, and coronal/transverse kinematics, available for this participant? Both are outside the present input and would change the interpretive picture, particularly for the step-length asymmetry finding.
7. **Step-length definition.** Would the instantaneous definition yield a different symmetry value for this subject? The two definitions diverge in some cases, and only the successive-footfall value is supplied.
8. **Participant context.** Are age, sex, time post-stroke and clinical scores retrievable for this participant so that any future comparison can be better matched? These fields are not supplied in this input.
