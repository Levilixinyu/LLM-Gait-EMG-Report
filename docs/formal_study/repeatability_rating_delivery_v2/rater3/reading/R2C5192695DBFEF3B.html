## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

**How to read these numbers.** The released curves are **ensemble-averaged envelopes**. For each participant, side and muscle, a shared maximum across available strides was applied before averaging, with a participant-, side- and muscle-specific denominator (not shared between sides). Amplitudes are therefore **relative envelope values** — not neural drive, force, or strength — and muscles must **not** be ranked as stronger or weaker from them. An averaged curve cannot demonstrate stride-to-stride variability, and the pattern described below should not be assumed to recur identically in every individual stride.

"Above-threshold interval" means the region where the **averaged envelope** exceeds **20% of that curve's own peak**, with a minimum retained interval of 3% of the cycle. These are properties of the averaged envelope under that rule; they are **not** physiological onset, offset, burst duration, continuous contraction, or proof of inactivity outside the intervals. An interval written X%–Y% with X > Y wraps the 100%→0% boundary, i.e. the mean envelope remains above threshold across the cycle-defining initial-contact boundary. Because no intermediate event is supplied, **no interval is assigned to stance, swing, loading response, pre-swing or mid-stance**, and no interval is described as normal, early, delayed or prolonged.

**Coverage and boundary continuity (mean-envelope level).** Six of the seven averaged envelopes (RF, VL, BF, ST, TA, ERS) remain above their own 20%-of-peak threshold **across the cycle boundary**. GAS is the one muscle whose retained intervals both begin and end within the cycle; its first retained interval starts at 2%, i.e. numerically close to the initial-contact boundary, which is the only boundary this input names. Reported coverage ranges from 44% (BF) to 90% (ERS). None of these coverage values establishes that a below-threshold segment did or did not occur anywhere in the cycle — coverage below 100% and "no retained interval" are algorithm outputs, not demonstrations of silence or of continuous activation. Anatomically, GAS is a plantar flexor, TA a dorsiflexor, RF and VL knee extensors (RF also a hip flexor), BF and ST knee flexors/hip extensors, and ERS a trunk extensor; these actions are named without mapping any interval to a functional task such as clearance, push-off or weight acceptance.

**Standardized deviations (max |z|).** Max |z| is an **extreme-value statistic** over 1001 phase points — the single largest standardized difference from the phase-specific reference mean — and it locates the largest *standardized* difference, **not** the time of peak envelope amplitude and **not** a gait event. The conventional 2-SD rule does not apply: in this dataset every one of 109 healthy controls exceeds |z| = 2 on at least one muscle, and per-muscle healthy 95th percentiles for this statistic run **3.65 (ERS) to 4.61 (TA)**, higher still under leave-one-out.

- **GAS 5.8** (above the phase-specific reference mean, at 70%) exceeds the **upper end (4.61)** of the stated per-muscle 95th-percentile range, so it is high on this statistic irrespective of which muscle-specific cut in that range applies to GAS.
- **ERS 5.5** (above reference, at 91%) exceeds the **ERS-specific 95th percentile of 3.65**.
- **RF 2.9, BF 2.9, ST 2.3, VL 1.9** and **TA 1.4** all fall **below the lowest** of the stated per-muscle 95th percentiles (3.65) and are therefore not elevated by this dataset's own reference range, despite several exceeding 2.
- "Above/below reference" in this table means relative to the **phase-specific reference mean** at that phase point, not relative to a healthy percentile limit. TA's value is the only one in the below-mean direction.

These comparisons use a pooled, unmatched healthy EMG reference (effective n ≈ 106–109 per muscle) and are sensitive to the normalization and envelope pipeline described above. They do not establish abnormal muscle physiology or a stroke-specific control deficit.

**Co-contraction indices.** CCI here is the **Falconer–Winter index over the full continuous envelopes** (2 × common area / summed areas) for the **whole cycle**. It can be non-zero even when detected bursts do not overlap, because sub-threshold activity contributes. It is **not** simultaneous activation time, **not** muscular force, and **not** a phase-specific result; it depends on the normalization and envelope method, so values and thresholds from other studies or algorithms do not transfer.

- **TA vs GAS = 0.53** — inside this dataset's healthy interval (0.24–0.59), above the healthy median of 0.39 and nearer the upper endpoint.
- **RF vs BF = 0.67** — inside the healthy interval (0.48–0.83) and essentially at the healthy median of 0.68, marginally below it.

Lying inside these intervals does **not** establish physiologically normal co-activation or normal muscle function, just as a value outside them would not establish pathological co-activation, stiffness, impaired motor control or any diagnosis.

**What GVS and the composite can and cannot say.** GVS and the GPS-like composite are **summary magnitudes of difference** from the reference mean waveform across the cycle. Neither localizes the deviation to a phase of the cycle, and neither indicates the **direction** of the waveform difference (e.g. more flexed vs more extended) or which part of the curve drives it. Within this subject, the **hip** channel carries the largest summary difference (17.74), followed by the **knee** (11.07), with **pelvis** (6.77) and **ankle** (5.53) lower; this ordering is descriptive of the supplied numbers only.

**ROM.** The listed ROM values are excursion magnitudes in the sagittal plane. **ROM alone does not establish a clinical restriction** in the absence of an appropriate matched comparison, and no per-joint ROM reference values were supplied here. The small sagittal pelvic excursion (2.5°) is reported as measured; it is not interpretable as restricted, normal or compensatory from this input, and small-magnitude pelvic angles are additionally sensitive to marker and model definitions that are not documented in this summary.

**Comparability.** As with the EMG, the kinematic reference pools sides and ages and is not matched to this participant. Deviation magnitudes here therefore blend any stroke-related control differences with reference-composition and gait-speed effects, and these contributions **cannot be separated from these data**. ## 4. Questions for Further Clinical Assessment

The following are open questions for the treating clinician, posed without presupposing that any mechanism or measurement issue has already been observed. None implies a recommended intervention.

1. **Matched reference availability.** Can a reference subgroup matched to this participant's age, sex, side and walking speed be assembled — ideally leave-one-out — to re-derive the GVS, composite and per-muscle max |z| values? Would the hip and knee GVS ordering, and the composite's position relative to the 95th percentile, persist under such a reference?
2. **Timing context.** Are individual gait events (notably paretic toe-off) and contralateral events available in the release for this subject? Adding them would allow the retained envelope intervals — including the six boundary-crossing envelopes — to be placed relative to the participant's own events rather than expressed as bare cycle percentages.
3. **Stride-level behaviour.** Do the underlying individual strides show a consistent envelope pattern, or does the averaged curve conceal stride-to-stride differences? The ensemble average supplied here cannot answer this.
4. **Signal-quality and channel review.** Has a raw-signal review (electrode placement, crosstalk, baseline, saturation, ECG contamination for the ERS channel) been performed for this recording? The GAS and ERS max |z| values are the two that exceed the stated per-muscle 95th-percentile range; clarifying whether any acquisition factor contributes would be informative before those values are weighted.
5. **Normalization sensitivity.** Because amplitudes use a participant-, side- and muscle-specific shared-stride maximum, would an alternative normalization (e.g. a task- or MVC-referenced denominator, where obtainable) change the relative envelope amplitudes, the 20%-of-peak intervals, the coverage values, or the two CCI values?
6. **Bilateral and out-of-plane data.** Are non-paretic-side EMG and kinematics, and coronal/transverse joint data, available? Neither is in this input, and both would be needed before any statement about asymmetry beyond the supplied step-length index or about non-sagittal mechanics.
7. **Clinical context fields.** Are time post-stroke, stroke-specific clinical scores, assistive-device and orthosis use, and observed walking safety documented in the source record? The "community ambulator" label here reflects a speed band only and should be checked against clinical observation.
8. **Step-length definition.** Should the symmetry index also be computed under the instantaneous-heel definition, given that the two definitions diverge for some subjects, before the −6.6% paretic-shorter value is used in any longitudinal or between-subject comparison?
