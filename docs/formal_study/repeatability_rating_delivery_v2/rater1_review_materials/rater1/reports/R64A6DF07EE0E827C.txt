All seven mean envelopes are reported above threshold across the **100%→0% initial-contact cycle boundary**. These are mean-envelope threshold findings—not physiological onset, offset, contraction duration, or evidence of inactivity outside retained intervals. They do not establish the same pattern in every stride. Without intermediate events or matched timing references, the intervals cannot be assigned gait-phase functions or characterized as normal, early, delayed, or prolonged.

Normalization uses a participant-, side-, and muscle-specific maximum shared across available strides before averaging, without further amplitude normalization here. Peak values therefore describe relative envelope amplitude, not force or strength, and cannot rank muscles by strength.

At every listed maximum-|z| location, the envelope is **above the phase-specific reference mean**. These locations mark the largest standardized differences, not necessarily peak envelope amplitude or gait events. TA’s 5.9 and ERS’s 8.2 exceed their supplied pooled healthy 95th-percentile calibration values of 4.61 and 3.65, respectively. A conventional |z| > 2 rule is inappropriate for this across-cycle extreme statistic; these calibration percentiles are not matched leave-one-out normal limits.

**Whole-cycle CCI:**
- **TA–GAS:** 0.40, close to the supplied median of 0.39 and within the empirical interval 0.24–0.59.
- **RF–BF:** 0.54, below the supplied median of 0.68 and within the empirical interval 0.48–0.83, toward its lower endpoint.

CCI measures whole-cycle overlap of normalized continuous envelopes, including sub-threshold contributions. It is not simultaneous activation time, muscular force, or a phase-specific measure. These comparisons do not establish normal muscle function or physiological co-activation; values depend on the normalization and envelope methods.
