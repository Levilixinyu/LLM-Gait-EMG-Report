All seven mean envelopes are explicitly reported above threshold across the **initial-contact cycle boundary**. The cycle extends from paretic initial contact at 0% to the next paretic initial contact at 100%. These findings do not establish identical patterns in individual strides, continuous physiological contraction within intervals, or inactivity outside them. No intermediate event or gait-phase assignment is supported.

Normalization uses a participant-, side- and muscle-specific maximum shared across available strides before averaging, without further amplitude normalization here. Peak values therefore represent relative envelopes, not strength, force or absolute neural activation.

At every listed maximum-|z| location, the envelope is **above the phase-specific reference mean**. These locations identify the largest standardized differences, not necessarily peak envelope amplitude or a gait event. Maximum |z| is an extreme statistic across 1001 points; the conventional 2-SD rule is inappropriate. TA 5.9 and ERS 8.2 exceed their supplied pooled healthy 95th-percentile calibration values of 4.61 and 3.65, respectively. Exact muscle-specific percentile values for the other listed muscles are not supplied.

**Whole-cycle CCI**

These comparisons do not establish normal muscle function or pathological co-activation; values depend on the normalization and envelope methods used.

The knee has the largest supplied GVS, followed by the ankle, hip and pelvis. These scores summarize waveform differences from the reference; they do not identify their direction or cycle location. The ROM values alone do not establish clinical restriction.

The **GPS-like score of 6.81** is above the supplied pooled calibration median of 5.1 and below its 95th percentile of 10.7. It is an RMS composite of the supplied sagittal GVS values, not the validated nine-variable GPS.

The stated deviation reference comprises **138 pooled healthy subjects**, evaluated at 1001 cycle points, from `main_cohort_excel`. Comparability is limited: this is not a matched reference, and 0.75 m/s is below the healthy cohort’s 0.80–1.79 m/s speed range. Consequently, no speed-matched healthy reference exists in this dataset, and slow-walking effects cannot be separated from stroke-related effects. This limitation applies to the EMG and kinematic comparisons. Pooled calibration percentiles are not matched leave-one-out normal limits, and these comparisons cannot establish a disease effect or clinical diagnosis.
