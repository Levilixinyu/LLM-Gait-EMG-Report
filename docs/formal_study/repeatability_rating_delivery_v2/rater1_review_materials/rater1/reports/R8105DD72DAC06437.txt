## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

Retained intervals identify where the **averaged envelope exceeds 20% of its own peak**, with a minimum interval of 3% of the cycle by sample count. These are mean-envelope threshold findings—not physiological activation onset, offset or contraction duration—and do not establish inactivity elsewhere or identical behavior in every stride.

Normalization uses a participant-, side- and muscle-specific shared maximum across available strides before averaging, without further amplitude normalization here. Peak values are relative envelope amplitudes, not force or strength.

| GAS | 0%–13%; 14%–19%; 31%–64% | 60% | 0.61 | 10.1; above; 57% |
| RF | 88%–53%, wrapping boundary | 68% | 0.45 | 2.5; above; 20% |
| VL | 69%–81%; 91%–54%, wrapping boundary | 75% | 0.59 | 4.5; above; 23% |

| TA | 61%–37%, wrapping boundary | 77% | 0.50 | 2.5; above; 18% |
| ERS | 0%–100% | 100% | 0.64 | 5.8; above; 80% |

RF, VL, BF, ST and TA have explicitly reported above-threshold continuity across the initial-contact cycle boundary. ERS has a full-cycle retained interval and explicit boundary continuity. GAS beginning at 0% identifies the analysis-window boundary, not a physiological onset.

Maximum |z| identifies the **largest standardized reference difference**, not necessarily the envelope-amplitude peak or a gait event. Above/below refers to the phase-specific reference mean. The conventional |z| > 2 rule is inappropriate for this extreme-value statistic. ERS 5.8 exceeds its supplied pooled healthy 95th percentile of 3.65; TA 2.5 is below its supplied percentile of 4.61. Exact muscle-specific percentile values for the other muscles are not supplied.

**Whole-cycle envelope overlap:**

| Pair | CCI | Supplied empirical interval | Supplied median |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| TA–GAS | 0.56 | 0.24–0.59 | 0.39 |
| RF–BF | 0.71 | 0.48–0.83 | 0.68 |

Both values lie within their supplied intervals and above their medians; TA–GAS is near the upper endpoint. CCI measures whole-cycle overlap of normalized continuous envelopes, including sub-threshold contributions. It is not simultaneous activation time, muscular force or a phase-specific result. Interval membership does not establish physiologically normal co-activation, and values depend on the normalization and envelope methods used.

It is the RMS of the supplied sagittal GVS values, not the validated nine-variable GPS. The ankle has the largest GVS among these channels. Neither GVS nor GPS-like localizes the direction or cycle location of waveform differences. ROM values alone do not establish clinical restriction.

The actual deviation reference comprised **138 pooled healthy subjects**, evaluated at **1001 cycle points**, from *main_cohort_excel*. Comparability is limited: the reference is not individually matched, and 0.45 m/s is below its 0.80–1.79 m/s speed range, so no speed-matched healthy reference exists in this dataset. Slow-walking effects cannot be separated from stroke-associated differences using these summaries. Across the EMG, CCI and kinematic comparisons, empirical departures or interval membership cannot establish a disease effect or clinical diagnosis. Pooled calibration percentiles are not matched leave-one-out normal limits.
