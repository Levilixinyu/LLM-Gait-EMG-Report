## 1. Objective Findings

**Participant and side.** Sub30_P is a member of the stroke cohort; the released data are from the **paretic** side. The analysis cycle runs from initial contact of that limb (0%) to its next initial contact (100%); these are two successive occurrences of the same boundary event, not one instant. No intermediate gait events (including toe-off) are supplied in this input, so all timing below is expressed purely as a percentage of the analysis cycle and is **not** assigned to stance, swing, loading response, pre-swing, mid-stance, or any other phase.

**Spatiotemporal values as supplied.**
- Gait speed **0.43 m/s**, falling in the 0.4–0.8 m/s band labelled "limited community ambulator." This label denotes a speed band only; it does not measure walking independence, safety, or real-world community mobility.
- Cadence **76.0 steps/min**.
- Step length symmetry index **+10.6%** using the **successive-footfall** definition. With the stated sign convention (negative = shorter paretic step), the positive value indicates the **paretic step is the longer of the two** by this index. The instantaneous-heel-position definition can yield a different value for some subjects; only the successive-footfall value is supplied here.
- Composite sagittal kinematic deviation score (**GPS-like**, RMS of the four supplied sagittal GVS channels — not the validated 9-variable GPS): **6.29**.

**Reference used and its limits.** The kinematic deviation reference for this subject is a **pooled cohort of 138 healthy subjects** (1001 phase points, main_cohort_excel), pooling both body sides and all ages; it is not age- or speed-matched. Critically, this subject's speed of **0.43 m/s lies below the healthy reference cohort's self-selected speed range of 0.80–1.79 m/s**, so **no speed-matched healthy reference exists in this dataset** for this participant. Any difference from the reference therefore conflates slow-walking effects with stroke-related control changes, and these data cannot separate the two. Differences below should be read as differences from an imperfectly comparable empirical reference — they do not by themselves establish a disease effect, a severity grade, or a clinical diagnosis.

**What is not supplied in this input.** Age, sex, time post-stroke, stroke clinical scores, and non-paretic-side data are not provided here (some may exist in the release; their absence from this summary is not evidence of absence from the source dataset). Also outside this input: individual toe-off and matched muscle-specific normal timing, stride-to-stride variability, raw-signal quality checks, expert plots, and all coronal/transverse-plane kinematics. Only sagittal-plane angles are included.

## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

**How these numbers were produced.** All EMG values are properties of an **ensemble-averaged envelope** per muscle, normalized to a participant-, side-, and muscle-specific shared maximum across available strides (individual strides are not rescaled to their own peaks). Amplitudes are therefore **relative envelope values** — they are not neural drive, force, or strength, and muscles must not be ranked as stronger or weaker from them. An average curve cannot demonstrate stride-to-stride consistency: the patterns below describe the mean envelope, not every individual stride. "Above-threshold interval" means the averaged envelope exceeded **20% of that curve's own peak** for at least **3% of the cycle**; these are algorithmic threshold crossings, not physiological onsets, offsets, contraction durations, or evidence of inactivity elsewhere.

**Seven muscles are reported** (GAS, RF, VL, BF, ST, TA, ERS). All seven show an interval that **wraps the 100%→0% boundary**, i.e., the mean envelope remained above its 20% threshold across the cycle-defining initial-contact boundary.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| GAS | 91–67 (wraps) | 78% | 0.31 | 6.5 at 56%, above reference |
| RF | 51–56; 82–85; 90–94; 97–49 (wraps) | 65% | 0.40 | 3.8 at 6%, below reference |
| VL | 54–57; 89–48 (wraps) | 64% | 0.43 | 3.0 at 30%, above reference |
| BF | 35–46; 48–51; 52–62; 63–34 (wraps) | 95% | 0.32 | 2.2 at 56%, above reference |
| ST | 36–44; 48–61; 77–32 (wraps) | 79% | 0.54 | 3.7 at 54%, above reference |

| ERS | 0–100 | 100% | 0.41 | 2.2 at 51%, below reference |

**Reading of the interval structure.** Several channels show **fragmented** retained intervals with short gaps between them (e.g., BF at approximately 46–48%, 51–52% and 62–63%; RF at 49–51%, 56–82%, 85–90% and 94–97%; ST at 44–48% and 61–77%). At the mean-envelope level these gaps are regions that did not meet the threshold-plus-duration criterion; they do not establish that the muscle was electrically silent, nor that the same fragmentation occurred in every stride. Coverage values are high across the board (64%–100%).

For **ERS**, the single retained interval spans the entire analysis window (0%–100%) with 100% coverage, which is consistent with the averaged envelope remaining above its own 20% threshold throughout the cycle. For all other muscles, including **TA at 98%**, the reported coverage is a rounded summary value and does not by itself establish that no below-threshold segment existed; where gaps are explicitly implied by the interval endpoints (e.g., TA between roughly 7% and 10%), those are the documented sub-threshold regions of the mean curve. Anatomically, these are ankle plantarflexor (GAS), dorsiflexor (TA), knee extensor (RF, VL), knee flexor/hip extensor (BF, ST) and erector spinae channels; no interval here is assigned a gait-phase function such as clearance, push-off or weight acceptance, and no timing is characterized as normal, early, delayed or prolonged, because the matched event and timing references required for that are not supplied.

**Max |z| interpretation.** Max |z| is an **extreme-value statistic** — the largest standardized difference from the phase-specific reference mean across all 1001 phase points — not a single-observation z-score, and the conventional 2-SD rule does not apply. In this dataset every one of 109 healthy controls exceeds |z| = 2 on at least one muscle, and per-muscle healthy 95th percentiles for this statistic span **3.65 (ERS) to 4.61 (TA)**. Against that context:
- **GAS (6.5)** exceeds the upper end of that entire per-muscle 95th-percentile span, making it the clearest standardized difference from the reference in this record.
- **RF (3.8)** and **ST (3.7)** fall inside the 3.65–4.61 band of per-muscle 95th percentiles; without the specific 95th-percentile value for those two muscles, they cannot be classified either way.
- **TA (3.4)**, **VL (3.0)**, **BF (2.2)** and **ERS (2.2)** fall at or below the lower end of that band (and TA and ERS below their own stated or bounding percentiles).

The location of each max |z| marks where the standardized difference from the reference was largest — **not** necessarily where envelope amplitude peaked and not a gait event. "Above reference" / "below reference" means relative to the phase-specific reference **mean** at that location, not relative to a healthy percentile limit. All of these comparisons inherit the speed and matching limitations described in Section 1.

**Co-contraction indices.** CCI here is the Falconer–Winter index computed over the **full continuous envelopes across the whole cycle** (2 × common area / summed areas). It is a whole-cycle overlap index of normalized envelopes — not simultaneous activation time, not muscular force, not a phase-specific result — and it can be non-zero even where detected burst intervals do not overlap, because sub-threshold activity contributes.
- **TA vs GAS = 0.71**, above the upper bound of the supplied healthy 2.5th–97.5th percentile interval of 0.24–0.59 (median 0.39).
- **RF vs BF = 0.72**, inside the supplied healthy interval of 0.48–0.83 and modestly above the supplied median of 0.68.

Being outside the empirical interval (TA–GAS) does **not** establish pathological co-activation, spasticity, stiffness, impaired motor control or any diagnosis; being inside it (RF–BF) does **not** establish physiologically normal co-activation or normal muscle function. CCI values depend on the normalization and envelope pipeline used here and must not be compared with thresholds or values from other studies or algorithms.

All kinematics are **sagittal plane only**; coronal and transverse channels are not part of this dataset and no statement about them can be made.

**Composite (GPS-like) = 6.29**, versus the supplied healthy composite reference **median 5.1, 95th percentile 10.7**. This subject's composite is therefore **above the reference median but below the reference 95th percentile**. This is a summary magnitude of difference from the pooled reference across the four sagittal channels; it does not localize waveform direction, does not identify which portion of the cycle contributes, and does not grade severity.

Among the individual channels, the **knee GVS (7.20)** is the largest and the **pelvis GVS (4.14)** the smallest; per-joint GVS reference distributions are **not supplied**, so each joint's value can be ranked within this subject but not classified against a normal limit. Likewise, the ROM figures are descriptive: **ROM alone does not establish a clinical restriction** in the absence of an appropriate matched comparison, and none is supplied for ROM here. The small sagittal pelvic excursion (2.6°) is reported as measured and should not be read as a finding of restricted or excessive pelvic motion without such a comparison.

These comparisons use a pooled, non-age- and non-speed-matched reference whose speed range does not extend down to this subject's 0.43 m/s. The reference percentiles supplied are **pooled calibration percentiles**, not matched leave-one-out normal limits (leave-one-out runs use one subject fewer and give wider limits for the extreme-value statistics). Accordingly, the differences described in this section and Section 2 are differences from an empirical reference under acknowledged comparability limits; they cannot establish a disease effect, attribute the pattern specifically to stroke rather than to slow walking, or support a clinical diagnosis. The same caution applies symmetrically — group membership alone (healthy or stroke) never establishes normal or abnormal physiology.

## 4. Questions for Further Clinical Assessment

These are open questions raised by the limits of the supplied summary. None presupposes that the mechanism or measurement issue named has already been observed, and none implies a treatment direction.

1. **Event data.** Are individual gait events (including paretic and non-paretic toe-off) available in the release for this participant? Access to them would allow the interval percentages above to be placed in a phase framework, which this summary cannot support.
2. **Speed-comparable reference.** Is a reference sample walking at or near 0.43 m/s obtainable (speed-matched healthy walkers, or a within-subject comparison at differing speeds)? Without one, slow-walking effects and stroke-specific effects remain confounded in every deviation reported here.
3. **Contralateral data.** Are non-paretic-side EMG and kinematic curves available? Note that the EMG normalization denominator is side-specific, so between-side amplitude comparisons would require explicit handling of that.
4. **Stride-level behaviour.** Can per-stride envelopes or a variability metric be retrieved, to determine whether the fragmented interval structure (notably BF, RF, ST) reflects a consistent mean-curve feature or averaging across dissimilar strides?
5. **Signal quality.** Have raw-signal quality checks (electrode placement, crosstalk, baseline noise, ECG or movement artefact — particularly relevant to the trunk ERS channel showing full-cycle above-threshold coverage, and to the high-coverage TA and GAS channels) been performed? This summary contains no such check, and a measurement explanation for high-coverage envelopes has not been excluded.
6. **Clinical context.** Are age, sex, time post-stroke, and stroke clinical scores available for this participant? They are not supplied in this input and would materially affect how the reference comparison should be framed.
7. **Frontal/transverse motion.** Given that only sagittal channels exist here, is three-dimensional kinematic data available or obtainable, and is out-of-plane motion a clinically relevant question for this participant?
8. **Additional co-contraction context.** Should CCI be recomputed on windows of interest or with alternative normalization, would the pipeline dependence of the index be documented so that values are not compared across methods?

*All findings above derive solely from the supplied summary for Sub30_P. Information not present in this summary is not thereby established as absent from the source dataset.*
