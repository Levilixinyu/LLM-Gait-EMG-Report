## 1. Objective Findings

The released cycle runs from initial contact of that limb (0%) to the next initial contact of the same limb (100%); these are two successive occurrences of the same event, not one instant. **Spatiotemporal values as supplied.**

- Step length symmetry index **−6.6%**, using the **successive-footfall** definition (each heel at its own initial contact). The negative sign means the **paretic step is the shorter of the two** by this index. The alternative instantaneous definition can give a different value in some subjects; that alternative value is not supplied here.

**Reference used and its limits.** Kinematic deviation is referenced to **138 pooled healthy subjects**, 1001 phase points (source: main_cohort_excel). That cohort pools both body sides and all ages and is **not age- or speed-matched** to this participant. Critically, the healthy reference self-selected speeds span 0.80–1.79 m/s, and this subject walks at **0.79 m/s — below that floor**, so **no speed-matched healthy reference exists in this dataset** for him. Any deviation from the reference therefore conflates slow-walking effects with stroke-related control changes, and these two contributions **cannot be separated from these data**. Differences from the reference are descriptive; they do not by themselves establish a disease effect, a severity grade, or a clinical diagnosis.

**Channels available vs. not supplied.**

- **Not supplied in this task** (not the same as documented absent from the source release): age, sex, time post-stroke, stroke clinical scores, non-paretic-side data, individual toe-off/gait-event timing, matched muscle-specific normal timing references, stride-level variability, raw-signal quality checks, and expert plots.

## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

**How these numbers should be read.** All values come from **ensemble-averaged envelopes**. Normalization uses a shared maximum across available strides before averaging, participant-, side-, and muscle-specific; individual strides are not rescaled to their own peaks, and no further amplitude normalization was applied. Amplitudes are therefore **relative envelope values, not neural drive, force, or strength**, and muscles must not be ranked as stronger or weaker from them. An average curve cannot show stride-to-stride variability, and a pattern in the mean envelope should not be assumed to recur in every individual stride. "Above-threshold interval" means the averaged envelope exceeded **20% of that same curve's own peak** for at least 3% of the cycle; it is a property of the averaged envelope under that rule, not a physiological onset, offset, contraction duration, or evidence of inactivity outside the interval. An onset written as 0% is the analysis-window boundary.

| RF | 0%–100% (continuous across boundary) | 100% | 0.72 | 2.9 above, at 22% |

**Boundary continuity.** Five channels (RF, VL, BF, ST, TA) and ERS show the averaged envelope remaining above its own 20% threshold across the 100%→0% cycle-defining boundary; their intervals wrap. GAS is the only channel whose retained interval lies wholly within the cycle, beginning at 5% and ending at 58%. For RF and ERS the coverage is **reported as 100%** with no separate retained below-threshold segment listed; because this is a rounded summary figure, it should be read as "no below-threshold segment was retained/reported," not as proof that no sub-threshold sample exists anywhere in the curve.

**What cannot be said about timing.** Without individual toe-off and matched muscle-specific normal timing, no interval here can be assigned to stance or swing, to loading response, mid-stance or pre-swing, or to a functional role such as clearance, push-off or weight acceptance, and a conventional 60% stance/swing split must not be substituted. Anatomical actions may be named (e.g., GAS as an ankle plantar flexor, TA as a dorsiflexor, RF/VL as knee extensors, BF/ST as knee flexors and hip extensors, ERS as a trunk extensor), but no interval is being mapped to a gait-phase function. Nothing here can be labelled early, delayed, prolonged or normal in timing.

**Max |z| values — extreme-value statistic, read against the per-muscle reference.** Max |z| is the **largest absolute standardized difference across all 1001 phase points**, so it is not a single-observation z-score and the conventional 2-SD rule does not apply: in this dataset every one of 109 healthy controls exceeds |z| = 2 on at least one muscle, and per-muscle healthy 95th percentiles for this statistic run **3.65 (ERS) to 4.61 (TA)**, higher again under leave-one-out. Accordingly:
- **ERS 4.7** exceeds the supplied ERS 95th percentile of 3.65. That percentile is a **pooled calibration value, not a matched leave-one-out normal limit**, and given the speed and matching limits above, exceeding it does not establish abnormality or pathology.
- **TA 2.3** lies below the supplied TA 95th percentile of 4.61.
- **GAS 4.5** and the remaining channels have no muscle-specific percentile supplied in this input; GAS falls inside the overall 3.65–4.61 span of per-muscle percentiles, but the GAS-specific limit is not available and should not be inferred.
- The **location** of each max |z| (e.g., 56% for GAS, 65% for ERS, 99% for VL) is where the standardized difference from the reference is largest — not necessarily where envelope amplitude peaks, and not a gait event.
- "Above/below reference" in this table means relative to the **phase-specific reference mean at that location**, not relative to a healthy percentile limit. VL (1.5, below) and TA (2.3, below) are the two channels whose largest standardized difference sits below the reference mean.

**Co-contraction indices.** CCI here is the Falconer–Winter index computed over the **full continuous envelopes of the whole cycle** (2×common area / summed areas). It is a whole-cycle overlap index of normalized envelopes — not time spent simultaneously active, not force, and not a phase-specific result. It can be non-zero even when detected burst intervals do not overlap, because sub-threshold activity contributes. It is also method-dependent (normalization and envelope processing), so values and thresholds from other studies or algorithms are not transferable.
- **TA vs GAS = 0.43**, inside the supplied healthy interval 0.24–0.59 and above the supplied median of 0.39.
- **RF vs BF = 0.77**, inside the supplied healthy interval 0.48–0.83, above the supplied median of 0.68, and **closer to the upper endpoint** of that interval.

Being inside these intervals does not establish physiologically normal co-activation or normal muscle function; being near or outside an endpoint would not establish pathological co-activation, stiffness, impaired motor control, or any diagnosis. The same limited-comparability caveat applies here as elsewhere, since the reference is pooled and unmatched.

**Composite (GPS-like) = 5.33**, the RMS of the four sagittal GVS values. This is **not** the validated 9-variable GPS. Against the supplied composite reference (median 5.1, 95th percentile 10.7), 5.33 sits just above the median and well below the 95th percentile. That placement is a descriptive comparison to a pooled, unmatched reference; it does not certify normal gait, and it does not quantify a disease effect.

**Distribution across joints.** Among the supplied channels, the **knee** carries the largest summary difference from the reference (GVS 8.78), followed by the ankle (4.68), hip (3.77), and pelvis (0.73). GVS and the GPS-like composite are **magnitude summaries of difference from the reference waveform**; neither localizes the difference to a direction (e.g., more flexion vs. more extension) nor to a portion of the cycle, and neither identifies which event or phase contributes.

**On ROM.** The ROM values are descriptive amplitudes of the sagittal excursion over the analysis cycle. No matched normative ROM limits are supplied in this input, so **ROM alone does not establish a restriction, contracture, or excessive motion** — that would require an appropriate matched comparison. The small pelvic ROM (3.1°) with the lowest GVS (0.73) is reported as measured; it is not interpreted here as either normal or restricted.

**Overall comparability statement.** Because the reference is a pooled, non-age-matched, non-speed-matched cohort whose speed range begins at 0.80 m/s — just above this subject's 0.79 m/s — these kinematic and EMG comparisons have limited comparability. A speed that falls near or inside an overall range does not demonstrate that an appropriate matched reference subgroup exists. No finding in this report, singly or combined, establishes a disease effect, a clinical diagnosis, a severity grade, or statistical significance.

## 4. Questions for Further Clinical Assessment

These are open questions for the treating clinician, posed without presupposing that any mechanism or measurement issue has already been observed. No management or treatment is suggested.

1. **Event data.** Can individual toe-off and other event timings for this cycle be retrieved from the release, so that the envelope intervals above could be positioned relative to actual stance/swing boundaries rather than cycle percentages alone?
2. **Matched reference.** Is an age- and speed-matched (or at minimum slow-speed) healthy comparison group available, so that slow-walking effects could in principle be distinguished from stroke-related differences? Would leave-one-out per-muscle limits for max |z| be obtainable for GAS, RF, VL, BF and ST, which are not supplied here?
3. **Whole-cycle envelope coverage.** For RF and ERS, whose coverage is reported at 100%, would examination of the unrounded envelope and of the raw signal clarify whether genuinely continuous supra-threshold averaged activity is present, or whether signal-quality factors (e.g., baseline offset, crosstalk, motion artifact, ECG contamination for the trunk channel) could contribute? Raw-signal quality checks are not part of this summary.
4. **Stride-level behaviour.** Are individual stride envelopes retrievable, so that it could be determined whether the averaged pattern reflects consistent strides or averaging across dissimilar ones? Variability cannot be assessed from mean curves.
5. **Contralateral data.** Is non-paretic-side EMG and kinematic data available for this participant? Note that the EMG normalization denominator is side-specific and not shared between sides, which constrains direct cross-side amplitude comparison.
6. **Clinical context not supplied here.** Are age, sex, time post-stroke and stroke clinical scores documented in the release for this subject, and do bedside findings correspond in any way to the knee-dominant kinematic difference (GVS 8.78) and to the RF-vs-BF CCI of 0.77 sitting near the upper end of the supplied healthy interval?
7. **Step length definition.** Would the instantaneous step-length definition yield a different symmetry value for this subject than the −6.6% obtained with the successive-footfall definition, and which definition is preferred for this participant's ongoing records?
8. **Out-of-plane motion.** Since only sagittal data are included, are coronal and transverse kinematics recorded and available for review?
