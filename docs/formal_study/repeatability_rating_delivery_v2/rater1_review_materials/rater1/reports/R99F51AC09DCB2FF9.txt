## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

These are **ensemble-averaged envelopes**, normalized using a shared maximum across available strides within each participant, side, and muscle—not independent peak normalization of each stride or a denominator shared between sides. Peak values are relative envelope amplitudes, not strength, force, or absolute neural activation.

Retained intervals exceed **20% of each mean curve’s own peak**, with a minimum duration criterion of **3% of the cycle by sample count**.

GAS and RF have the greatest reported threshold coverage, both 88%. Above-threshold continuity across the **initial-contact cycle boundary** is explicitly reported for GAS, RF, VL, BF, ST, and TA. The ERS interval reaches the 100% initial-contact endpoint; continuity across the boundary is not established for ERS.

These findings describe threshold behavior of the mean envelopes—not physiological onset, offset, continuous contraction, or inactivity elsewhere. They do not establish the same pattern in every stride. Without intermediate events and matched timing references, the intervals cannot be assigned gait-phase functions or characterized as early, delayed, or prolonged.

**Whole-cycle CCI:**

- **TA–GAS: 0.58**, within the supplied empirical interval of 0.24–0.59, near its upper endpoint and above the median of 0.39.
- **RF–BF: 0.51**, within 0.48–0.83, near its lower endpoint and below the median of 0.68.

CCI measures whole-cycle overlap of the full normalized envelopes, including sub-threshold contributions. It is not simultaneous activation time, force, or a phase-specific result. These interval comparisons do not establish physiologically normal co-activation or muscle function; values depend on the normalization and envelope methods used.

## 3. Kinematic and Reference Comparisons

The deviation reference actually used comprised **138 pooled healthy subjects**, evaluated over 1001 cycle points. Comparability is limited: it is not an individually matched reference, and the participant’s speed of **0.23 m/s** is below the healthy cohort’s **0.80–1.79 m/s** range. No speed-matched healthy reference exists in this dataset at this speed. Consequently, these comparisons cannot separate slow-walking effects from stroke-specific differences, establish a disease effect, or support a clinical diagnosis.
