# Gait Report Interpretation — Sub16_P
**Version:** limited-evidence-v3.1-candidate
**Scope:** Research interpretation of a supplied quantitative summary. Not a diagnosis, not a treatment recommendation.

## 1. Objective Findings

**Participant and side.** Sub16_P is recorded in the stroke cohort; the analysed limb is the **paretic** side. All data below describe that side only. No non-paretic-side values, and no between-side comparisons, are supplied in this input.

**Cycle definition.** The analysis cycle runs from initial contact of the paretic limb (0%) to the next initial contact of the same limb (100%). These endpoints are two successive occurrences of the same event, not a single instant. **No intermediate gait event (including toe-off) is supplied**, so all timing below is expressed as a percentage of the analysis cycle. Percentages are not converted to stance/swing or to any named sub-phase, and no conventional 60% stance split is assumed.

**Spatiotemporal values as supplied.**
- Gait speed **1.36 m/s**. This falls inside the healthy reference cohort's self-selected speed span (0.80–1.79 m/s), so the speed-floor caveat that applies to most stroke subjects in this dataset does not apply here. Being inside an overall range, however, does **not** demonstrate that a speed-matched (or age-matched) healthy comparison group was actually constructed — the reference used here is a pooled cohort (see below).
- The label "full community ambulator (>0.8 m/s)" denotes a **speed band only**; it carries no information about measured walking independence, safety, endurance or real-world community mobility.
- Cadence **116.2 steps/min**.
- Step length symmetry index **−5.1%**, computed with the **successive-footfall** definition (each heel at its own initial contact). The negative sign indicates the **paretic step is the shorter of the two**. The alternative instantaneous definition can yield a different value in some subjects; that value is not supplied, so this figure should be read as definition-specific.

**Reference used.** Kinematic deviation is referenced to **138 pooled healthy subjects**, 1001 phase points, source `main_cohort_excel`. This reference pools both body sides and all ages and is not matched to this participant on age or speed. It is a pooled calibration cohort, not a matched leave-one-out normal limit.

**Normalization.** EMG curves are **ensemble-averaged envelopes**; normalization used a shared maximum across available strides per participant, side and muscle, with no further amplitude normalization in this pipeline. Consequently amplitudes are **relative envelope values** — not neural drive, force or strength — and are **not comparable between muscles or between sides**. An averaged curve also cannot establish stride-to-stride variability or confirm that any given pattern occurred in every stride.

**Not supplied in this input (distinct from documented absence).** Individual toe-off and other gait events; matched muscle-specific normal timing references; stride-level variability; raw-signal quality checks or expert waveform plots; coronal and transverse plane kinematics (the release is sagittal-only by design); age, sex, time post-stroke and stroke clinical scores; non-paretic-side data. None of these are described here as absent from the source dataset — they simply are not in this summary. Seven EMG channels (GAS, RF, VL, BF, ST, TA, ERS) are present with detected intervals; no channel is reported as a failed or absent recording. (The release-level audit of 614 evaluable combinations out of 700, with 86 lacking valid nonzero paired samples, is dataset context and is not a statement about this subject's channels.)

## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

All intervals below are properties of the **averaged envelope** under the stated rule: envelope exceeds **20% of that same curve's own peak**, minimum retained interval **3% of the cycle**. They are not physiological onsets or offsets, not contraction durations, and not evidence of inactivity outside the listed windows. An interval written with X > Y wraps the 100%→0% boundary, meaning the mean envelope remained above its own threshold across the cycle-defining initial-contact boundary. An interval edge at a window boundary is an analysis-window artefact, not a physiological event. Timing is **not** labelled early, delayed or prolonged, because no matched muscle-specific timing reference is supplied.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| GAS | 1–6; 9–15; 21–47 | 39% | 0.88 | 3.4 above, at 35% |
| RF | 30–33; 40–48; 58–67; **82–13 (wraps)** | 55% | 0.72 | 3.1 above, at 43% |
| VL | 22–27; 28–34; 36–46; **82–18 (wraps)** | 61% | 0.63 | 1.5 below, at 98% |
| BF | 28–43; 60–64; **85–27 (wraps)** | 61% | 0.63 | 2.8 above, at 35% |
| ST | 50–64; **79–42 (wraps)** | 79% | 0.84 | 4.6 above, at 37% |
| TA | 59–75; **94–7 (wraps)** | 30% | 0.74 | 2.2 below, at 76% |
| ERS | 47–64; 79–82 | 23% | 0.80 | 2.8 above, at 56% |

**Reading of the interval pattern.** Five of seven curves (RF, VL, BF, ST, TA) show mean-envelope continuity across the initial-contact boundary. ST shows the highest reported coverage (79%) with only two retained intervals; GAS, ERS and TA show lower coverage (39%, 23%, 30%). Coverage values describe the proportion of the averaged cycle above each curve's own relative threshold — they do not quantify how long a muscle "worked." Where no interval is listed in a region, the supplied evidence establishes only that no region there met the threshold-plus-duration criteria; it does **not** establish that the envelope was below threshold throughout, and it certainly does not establish muscle inactivity. Anatomically these are knee extensors (RF, VL), knee flexors/hip extensors (BF, ST), ankle plantarflexor (GAS), ankle dorsiflexor (TA) and erector spinae (ERS); no interval is mapped to a functional task such as clearance, push-off or weight acceptance, since that mapping requires event data not supplied.

**Max |z| interpretation.** Max |z| is an **extreme-value statistic** — the largest standardized difference across all 1001 phase points versus the pooled reference mean/SD at each phase. It marks the point of greatest standardized difference, **not** the time of peak envelope amplitude and not a gait event. "Above/below reference" refers to the phase-specific reference mean at that location, not to a healthy percentile limit. The conventional 2-SD rule does not apply: in this dataset every one of 109 healthy controls exceeds |z| = 2 on at least one muscle, and per-muscle healthy 95th percentiles for this statistic run from **3.65 (ERS) to 4.61 (TA)**, higher under leave-one-out.

Against the supplied endpoints: ERS (2.8) is below its stated 3.65 percentile, and TA (2.2) is below its stated 4.61 percentile. GAS (3.4), RF (3.1), BF (2.8) and VL (1.5) all fall below **3.65**, the lowest per-muscle 95th percentile supplied, so each is below its own muscle's percentile whatever its exact value. **ST (4.6)** falls inside the 3.65–4.61 span; ST's specific 95th percentile is not supplied, so it cannot be classified as inside or outside its own healthy range from this input, and no severity grade or statistical significance can be assigned to it.

**Co-contraction indices.** CCI is the Falconer–Winter index (2 × common area / summed areas) computed on the **full continuous normalized envelopes over the whole cycle**. It can be non-zero even when detected bursts of the pair do not overlap, because sub-threshold activity contributes. It is not a duration of simultaneous activation, not muscular force, and not a phase-specific result; it depends on the normalization and envelope methods used here, so values and thresholds from other studies or algorithms do not transfer.

- **TA vs GAS = 0.38**, against a healthy median of 0.39 (2.5th–97.5th pct 0.24–0.59): within the supplied interval and essentially at the median.
- **RF vs BF = 0.73**, against a healthy median of 0.68 (0.48–0.83): within the supplied interval, above the median, and nearer the upper endpoint than the lower.

Lying within these empirical intervals does **not** establish physiologically normal co-activation or normal muscle function, just as a value outside them would not establish pathological co-activation, stiffness, impaired motor control or any diagnosis.

## 3. Kinematic and Reference Comparisons

All angles and ROM values are **sagittal plane only**; no coronal or transverse data are included, so nothing here speaks to frontal or rotational mechanics.

| Channel (sagittal) | ROM (deg) | Gait Variable Score |
|---|---|---|
| Ankle | 27.1 | 1.84 |
| Knee | 56.4 | 10.83 |
| Hip | 45.0 | 3.14 |
| Pelvis | 2.1 | 9.38 |

**Composite (GPS-like) = 7.39** — the RMS of the four sagittal GVS values. This is *not* the validated 9-variable GPS. Against the supplied composite reference (median 5.1, 95th percentile 10.7), 7.39 sits **above the median and below the 95th percentile**.

**Per-joint pattern.** The composite is driven mainly by the **knee (10.83)** and **pelvis (9.38)** channels, with ankle (1.84) and hip (3.14) contributing far less. GVS is a whole-cycle summary difference from the reference mean curve: it does **not** localize the deviation to any phase of the cycle, does not indicate the direction of the waveform difference (e.g. more flexed versus more extended), and does not identify which part of the curve produced the value. No per-joint GVS reference values are supplied, so the individual GVS figures cannot be placed against healthy limits — only the composite has a supplied reference.

**ROM.** Sagittal pelvic ROM of 2.1 deg is numerically small, but **no ROM reference distribution is supplied for any joint**, so ROM alone cannot establish a clinical restriction here; a comparison against an appropriate reference would be required. Likewise the ankle, knee and hip ROM values are reported descriptively only.

**Comparability limits.** The reference is a pooled cohort of 138 healthy subjects spanning both sides and all ages, not matched to this participant. This subject's speed sits inside the reference cohort's overall speed span, which removes the speed-floor problem that affects most stroke subjects in this dataset, but it does not create a matched comparison. Any difference from this reference is therefore a **summary difference from a pooled reference distribution** and cannot be attributed to stroke pathology, to a specific impairment, or to any disease mechanism from these data alone. The same limitation would apply to a healthy control run through this pipeline: group membership does not certify normal physiology, and deviation from an empirical reference does not by itself establish disease.

## 4. Questions for Further Clinical Assessment

These are open questions raised by the limits of this summary. None presupposes that the mechanism or measurement issue in question has been observed.

1. **Gait events.** Can individual toe-off and other event timings be retrieved from the release for this participant? Without them, no interval in Section 2 can be placed within a gait phase, and no timing can be assessed as early, delayed or prolonged.
2. **Matched reference.** Is an age- and speed-matched (ideally leave-one-out) healthy subset available? The pooled 138-subject reference limits the interpretability of the composite score of 7.39 and the per-muscle max |z| values.
3. **ST-specific percentile.** Can the ST-specific healthy 95th percentile for the max |z| extreme-value statistic be obtained? ST's value of 4.6 falls within the unresolved 3.65–4.61 span and cannot currently be classified.
4. **Knee and pelvis channels.** Would phase-resolved waveform plots for the knee and pelvis clarify where in the cycle, and in which direction, those GVS values arise — and could the 2.1 deg sagittal pelvic ROM reflect marker placement, model definition or segment-tracking factors rather than participant motion? Reference-only plots would not answer this; patient waveforms are required.
5. **Signal quality and stride count.** Are raw-signal quality checks, stride counts and per-stride envelopes retrievable? These would indicate whether the averaged envelope pattern (including the five channels continuous across the initial-contact boundary) reflects a consistent pattern or the averaging of dissimilar strides.
6. **Contralateral data.** Are non-paretic-side kinematics and EMG available? Note that the EMG normalization denominator is participant-, side- and muscle-specific in this release, so envelope amplitudes could not be compared across sides even if both were supplied; interval timing and coverage could be.
7. **Clinical context.** Are age, sex, time post-stroke and clinical scores available in the release for this participant? None are supplied in this input, and their absence here constrains how the step length asymmetry of −5.1% and the composite score of 7.39 can be contextualized.
8. **Step-length definition.** Would the instantaneous step-length definition yield a materially different symmetry value for this participant, given that the two definitions diverge in some subjects?

*No treatment recommendation is made or implied. Findings above are descriptive summary measures of averaged data compared with a pooled empirical reference and do not establish a diagnosis, a disease effect or a mechanism.*
