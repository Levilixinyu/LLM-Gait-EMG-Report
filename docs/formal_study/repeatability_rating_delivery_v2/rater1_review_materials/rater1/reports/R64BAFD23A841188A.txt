The speed band label denotes a **speed range only**. It does not describe measured walking independence, safety, assistive-device use, or real-world community mobility, none of which is supplied here.

The step length symmetry index of **+8.6%** is positive under the stated convention (200 × (paretic − non-paretic)/(paretic + non-paretic)); by that formula a positive value corresponds to a **longer paretic step** than non-paretic step, with a negative value indicating a shorter paretic step. This value was computed with the **successive-footfall** definition; the alternative instantaneous definition can yield a different number in some subjects, so the value is definition-dependent and not directly comparable to symmetry indices computed the other way.

**Critical comparability limit (applies to every reference comparison in this report).** This participant's gait speed of 0.22 m/s lies **well below the 0.80–1.79 m/s** range of self-selected speeds in the healthy reference cohort. **No speed-matched healthy reference exists in this dataset for this subject.** The reference used here (138 pooled healthy subjects, 1001 phase points, source: main_cohort_excel) also pools body sides and ages and is not age-matched. Consequently every deviation reported below conflates the effects of walking at a very low speed with any stroke-related control changes, and these two contributions **cannot be separated from these data**. Deviation from this reference does not by itself establish a disease effect, a pathological mechanism, or a clinical diagnosis; equally, agreement with the reference would not establish normal physiology.

**Supplied vs. not supplied.** Supplied: mean-curve EMG metrics, algorithm-detected above-threshold intervals, co-contraction indices, sagittal-plane ROM and Gait Variable Scores, and the spatiotemporal values above. **Not supplied in this task:** individual toe-off and other intra-cycle event boundaries, muscle-specific matched normal timing references, stride-level variability, raw-signal quality checks, expert plots, age, sex, time post-stroke, stroke clinical scores, and non-paretic-side data. Additional fields may exist in the wider release; their absence from this input must not be read as absence from the source dataset, and "not supplied here" is distinct from "documented as not recorded."

**Plane limitation.** All kinematic values are **sagittal plane only**. Coronal and transverse plane data are not included, so no statement can be made about frontal/rotational mechanics.

## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

**How these numbers must be read.** The curves are **ensemble-averaged envelopes**. For each participant, side and muscle, normalization used a shared maximum across available strides before averaging (participant-, side- and muscle-specific denominator; not shared between sides), and this pipeline used the released mean curves without further amplitude normalization. Therefore:

- Amplitudes are **relative envelope values**, not neural activation, force or strength. Muscles must **not** be ranked as stronger or weaker from these values, and values are not comparable across sides or subjects.
- Above-threshold intervals are regions where the **averaged envelope** exceeds 20% of **that curve's own peak**, with a minimum retained interval of 3% of the cycle. They are properties of the averaged envelope under this threshold-plus-duration rule — **not** physiological onset, offset, contraction duration, or continuous contraction, and not evidence of silence outside the intervals.
- A mean-curve pattern does **not** establish that the same pattern occurred in every individual stride, and an averaged curve carries **no** information about stride-to-stride variability.
- Percentages locate position in the cycle only. They do not identify stance, swing, loading response, mid-stance or pre-swing, and no conventional 60% stance/swing split is applied.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| RF | 87%–57% (wraps) | 73% | above threshold across cycle boundary | 0.48 | 4.5, above reference, at 39% |

**Observations at the mean-envelope level.** Coverage values are high across all seven recorded channels (73%–100% of the cycle above each curve's own 20%-of-peak threshold), and every channel's envelope remains above its threshold across the cycle-defining initial-contact boundary. GAS and BF are reported at 100% coverage with a single interval spanning 0%–100%; on the supplied evidence these two curves have no below-threshold segment in the averaged envelope. For the remaining channels, the reported coverage is below 100%, so below-threshold regions of the averaged curve exist between the listed intervals — but this reflects the relative threshold rule applied to a mean curve and does **not** mean the muscle was inactive there. ST is the only channel with two separately retained intervals (46%–77% and a wrapping 91%–36%), meaning its averaged envelope crossed below and back above threshold within the cycle. ERS at 98% coverage is a rounded value near the ceiling; a rounded figure alone does not establish either the presence or the absence of a hidden below-threshold segment.

Because the threshold is set to **each curve's own peak**, high coverage indicates an envelope with limited relative modulation across the cycle (a flatter normalized profile), not a stronger or more forceful muscle. Anatomically these channels correspond to plantarflexors (GAS), dorsiflexors (TA), knee extensors (RF, VL), knee flexors/hip extensors (BF, ST) and erector spinae (ERS); naming those actions is permissible, but **no interval is mapped to a gait-phase function** such as clearance, push-off or weight acceptance, because no event boundaries or matched timing references are supplied. For the same reason, no timing here can be called normal, early, delayed or prolonged.

**Max |z| values.** Max |z| is an **extreme-value statistic** — the largest standardized difference across all 1001 phase points — not a single-observation z-score, and its location marks the point of largest standardized difference from the phase-specific reference mean, **not** necessarily the peak of the envelope and not a gait event. "Above reference" means above the reference **mean at that phase point**, not above a healthy percentile limit. The conventional 2-SD rule does **not** apply: in this dataset all 109 healthy controls exceed |z| = 2 on at least one muscle, and per-muscle healthy 95th percentiles for this statistic run from 3.65 (ERS) to 4.61 (TA), higher still under leave-one-out. Against those pooled calibration percentiles — which are **not** matched leave-one-out normal limits for this subject — RF (4.5) and TA (4.6) sit near or just below their respective muscle percentiles, while VL (6.1), BF (5.1), ST (6.3), ERS (4.8) and especially **GAS (13.7)** are numerically larger. The GAS value is by a wide margin the largest standardized difference in this dataset row. Given the absence of a speed-matched reference, these magnitudes describe the size of the difference from a non-matched reference and cannot be attributed to stroke pathology, to a specific neuromuscular mechanism, or to any diagnosis.

**Co-contraction indices.** CCI here is the Falconer–Winter index computed over the **full continuous envelopes** (2 × common area / summed areas) across the **whole cycle**. It is a whole-cycle overlap index of normalized envelopes — **not** simultaneous activation time, not muscular force, not a phase-specific result — and it can be non-zero even when detected burst intervals do not overlap, because sub-threshold activity contributes. It is dependent on the normalization and envelope method used here and cannot be compared with values from other studies or algorithms.

- **TA vs GAS: 0.78.** The supplied healthy reference for this pair is median 0.39 (2.5th–97.5th pct 0.24–0.59). This value lies **above the upper endpoint** of that supplied interval.
- **RF vs BF: 0.65.** The supplied healthy reference is median 0.68 (0.48–0.83). This value lies **within** that interval and slightly below the supplied median.

Being inside the supplied interval (RF–BF) does **not** establish physiologically normal co-activation or normal muscle function; being outside it (TA–GAS) does **not** establish pathological co-activation, spasticity, stiffness, impaired motor control or any diagnosis. These reference intervals come from the pooled, non-speed-matched cohort, so the TA–GAS difference cannot be separated from very-low-speed walking effects.

**Composite (GPS-like) score: 10.56.** This is the RMS of the per-joint GVS values over the sagittal channels supplied — it is **not** the validated 9-variable GPS, because coronal and transverse variables are not included. Against the supplied composite reference (median 5.1, 95th percentile 10.7), this subject's value of 10.56 is above the median and **just below** the supplied 95th percentile. That percentile comes from the pooled calibration cohort and is **not** a matched leave-one-out normal limit; the closeness of 10.56 to 10.7 should not be used to classify the subject on either side of that boundary, and near-boundary rounded values do not justify classifying hidden unrounded values.

**Per-joint GVS.** GVS and the composite are **summary magnitudes of difference** from the stated reference across the cycle. They do **not** indicate the direction of the waveform difference (e.g. more flexed vs. more extended), do not localize the difference to any part of the cycle, and do not identify a phase. Numerically, the knee GVS (14.96) is the largest of the four channels, followed by hip (11.51), ankle (6.91) and pelvis (6.48); the composite is dominated accordingly. No per-joint reference limits were supplied, so these individual GVS values cannot be classified as within or outside normal limits.

**ROM.** The reported ROM values are descriptive ranges for this subject. **ROM alone does not establish a clinical restriction** in the absence of an appropriate matched comparison, and no ROM reference values were supplied here. A small sagittal pelvic range (4.1 deg) and the ankle, knee and hip ranges are therefore reported as measured values only; whether any represents a restriction, and whether it reflects joint mobility, motor control, the very low walking speed, or marker/model factors, cannot be determined from this input.

**Overall comparability.** The reference for this subject is 138 pooled healthy subjects, not matched for age, sex or speed, and — as stated in Section 1 — no speed-matched healthy comparator exists in this dataset at 0.22 m/s. The kinematic and EMG deviations reported should therefore be read as **differences from a non-matched empirical reference**, which cannot establish a disease effect, a specific impairment mechanism, or a clinical diagnosis. This limit applies symmetrically: it would apply equally to a healthy-group participant compared with the same reference.

## 4. Questions for Further Clinical Assessment

These are open questions raised by the limits of this summary. None presupposes that the mechanism or measurement issue named has been observed, and none implies a treatment.

1. **Event timing.** Can individual gait events (toe-off in particular) and stride timing be retrieved from the release for this subject, so that the detected envelope intervals can be positioned relative to actual intra-cycle boundaries rather than to percentage alone?
2. **Speed-matched comparison.** Is any speed-matched or slow-walking reference dataset available that could help separate very-low-speed walking effects from stroke-related control changes in the deviations reported here? Failing that, would a within-subject comparison against the non-paretic side (using its own side-specific normalization, with the resulting non-comparability of amplitudes explicitly kept in mind) be informative?
3. **Signal quality.** Have raw-signal quality checks, electrode placement records and any crosstalk assessment been reviewed for these channels — particularly GAS, which shows both 100% coverage and by far the largest max |z| (13.7)? Would such a review change the interpretation of the flat, high-coverage envelopes?
4. **Stride-level behaviour.** Are individual stride envelopes available? The mean curve cannot show whether the high coverage and the boundary continuity are consistent across strides or driven by a subset, and it carries no variability information.
5. **Clinical context.** Can the subject-level fields relevant to the stroke cohort (time post-stroke, clinical scores, age, sex, assistive-device and orthosis use, walking supervision status) be retrieved to place these numbers in context? The speed band label alone does not describe actual walking independence or safety.
6. **Out-of-plane mechanics.** Were coronal and transverse kinematics collected? If so, would a full 9-variable GPS change the picture relative to the sagittal-only composite reported here?
7. **Symmetry definition.** Was the instantaneous step-length definition also computed for this subject, and does it yield a materially different symmetry index from the +8.6% successive-footfall value?
8. **Reference construction.** Would a leave-one-out or otherwise matched normal limit be available for the max |z| statistic and the composite score, so that the near-boundary composite value (10.56 vs. the pooled 95th percentile of 10.7) can be interpreted with an appropriate limit rather than a pooled calibration percentile?
