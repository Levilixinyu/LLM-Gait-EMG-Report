All seven mean envelopes have reported above-threshold continuity across the initial-contact cycle boundary. This does not establish continuous physiological contraction or the same pattern in every stride. Without individual events and matched timing references, these intervals cannot be assigned gait-phase functions or characterized as early, delayed, or prolonged.

Maximum |z| identifies the largest standardized reference difference across 1001 cycle points—not necessarily the envelope amplitude peak. “Above” or “below” refers to the reference mean at that location. A conventional 2-SD abnormality rule is inappropriate. GAS, VL, and BF values exceed the upper end of the supplied pooled per-muscle 95th-percentile range, 3.65–4.61. TA 2.3 and ERS 2.7 are below their supplied pooled 95th percentiles of 4.61 and 3.65, respectively.

**Whole-cycle envelope overlap:**
- **TA–GAS CCI: 0.47**, above the supplied median of 0.39 and within the empirical interval 0.24–0.59.
- **RF–BF CCI: 0.62**, below the supplied median of 0.68 and within the empirical interval 0.48–0.83.

These CCIs quantify whole-cycle overlap of normalized continuous envelopes, including sub-threshold contributions. They are not simultaneous activation time, force, or phase-specific measures, and depend on the normalization and envelope methods. Interval membership does not establish physiologically normal co-activation.
