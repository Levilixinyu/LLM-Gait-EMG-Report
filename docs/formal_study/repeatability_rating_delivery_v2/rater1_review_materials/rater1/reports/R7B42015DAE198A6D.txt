## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

**Method constraints on all statements in this section.** These are **ensemble-averaged envelopes**. Normalization used a shared maximum across available strides before averaging, with a participant-, side- and muscle-specific denominator; individual strides were not rescaled to their own peaks. Amplitudes are therefore **relative envelope values** — not absolute neural activation, not force, not strength — and muscles cannot be ranked as stronger or weaker from them. An average curve carries no information about stride-to-stride variability, so none of the patterns below can be assumed to recur in every individual stride. Detected intervals are properties of the averaged envelope under the stated rule (>20% of that curve's own peak, minimum retained interval 3% of the cycle by sample count); because the threshold is relative to each curve's own peak, coverage values are **not** comparable across muscles as amplitude statements. "No interval detected" and the complement of a retained interval do **not** establish muscle inactivity or physiological silence, and none of these intervals should be read as physiological onset, offset, contraction duration, or a gait-phase function such as clearance, push-off or weight acceptance. Without matched muscle-specific timing references, no timing here can be called normal, early, delayed or prolonged.

**Descriptive reading.**
- **Boundary continuity.** At the mean-envelope level, VL, BF, ST, TA, RF and ERS were above threshold across the 100%→0% cycle boundary (the cycle-defining initial contact), i.e. their retained intervals wrap rather than terminate there. **GAS** is the one channel whose retained intervals do not include that boundary: its first interval begins at 1%, numerically adjacent to the initial-contact boundary, and its second ends at 78%.
- **Two continuous-coverage channels.** RF and ERS are reported with a single interval spanning the full analysis window and 100% coverage. This is consistent with the mean envelope remaining above 20% of its own peak throughout, but 100% is a rounded display value; a brief sub-threshold sample cannot be excluded or confirmed from what is supplied. Note also that an interval endpoint of 0% (or 100%) is the **analysis-window boundary**, not a demonstrated physiological onset.
- **Partial-coverage channels.** For GAS (69%), VL (75%), BF (87%), ST (70%) and TA (72%), the portions of the cycle outside the retained intervals did not meet the combined threshold-plus-3%-duration criterion. That is a statement about the detection rule on the mean curve; it does not establish that the envelope was below threshold everywhere in those regions (a sub-3% above-threshold excursion would also be discarded), and it does not establish inactivity.
- **Max |z| locations.** Each max |z| is an **extreme-value statistic** — the largest standardized difference from the phase-specific reference mean across 1001 phase points — not a single-observation z-score, not necessarily the location of peak envelope amplitude, and not a gait event. "Above/below reference" is relative to the reference **mean at that phase point**, not relative to a healthy percentile limit. The conventional 2-SD rule does not apply: in this dataset every one of the 109 healthy controls exceeds |z| = 2 on at least one muscle, and the per-muscle healthy 95th percentiles for this statistic run from 3.65 (ERS) to 4.61 (TA), higher still under leave-one-out. Against that stated span: **GAS 7.7, RF 6.6 and BF 5.3 exceed even the highest per-muscle 95th percentile quoted (4.61)**; **ERS 4.8 exceeds the ERS-specific 95th percentile of 3.65**; **TA 3.5 is below the TA-specific 95th percentile of 4.61**; and **VL 4.2 and ST 3.7 fall inside the 3.65–4.61 span**, so no determination is possible for those two muscles without their own per-muscle percentiles, which are not supplied. Exceeding a pooled percentile is a statement about distance from an unmatched empirical reference; given the absence of any speed-matched control, it does not by itself establish abnormal physiology, impaired control or disease.

**Co-contraction indices.** CCI here is the Falconer–Winter index over the **full continuous normalized envelopes** for the whole cycle (2 × common area / summed areas). It is a whole-cycle overlap index of envelope shape, **not** simultaneous activation time, not muscular force, and not a phase-specific result; it can be non-zero even where detected bursts do not overlap, because sub-threshold activity contributes to the areas. Values depend on this normalization and envelope pipeline, so thresholds or values from other studies or algorithms do not transfer.
- **TA vs GAS = 0.62**, above the upper endpoint of the healthy 2.5th–97.5th interval for this dataset (0.24–0.59) and above the healthy median of 0.39.
- **RF vs BF = 0.78**, **within** the healthy interval 0.48–0.83 and above the median of 0.68, positioned closer to the upper endpoint.

Being inside that interval (RF–BF) does not establish physiologically normal co-activation or normal muscle function; being outside it (TA–GAS) does not establish pathological co-activation, spasticity, stiffness, impaired motor control or any diagnosis. Both values share the same unmatched, non-speed-matched reference limitation described in Section 1.

Relative to the supplied composite reference (median 5.1, 95th percentile 10.7), 17.20 lies above both. **Interpretive limits.** GVS and the GPS-like composite are **summary magnitude differences** from the stated reference waveforms. Neither localizes the deviation in the cycle, neither indicates the direction of the waveform offset (e.g. more flexed vs more extended), and neither attributes the difference to a specific phase or event. The ROM values are descriptive ranges only; ROM alone does not establish a clinical restriction or contracture in the absence of an appropriate matched comparison, and none of the joint ROM values has a supplied reference. Because the reference is a pooled, unmatched cohort and no control in it walked as slowly as 0.33 m/s, the elevated composite score cannot be partitioned between slow-walking mechanics and stroke-related changes, and it does not establish a clinical severity grade, a disease effect, or a diagnosis. A speed falling inside some overall cohort range would not have demonstrated matched comparability either; here the speed falls outside the range entirely. No statistical significance testing is supplied and none is implied.

## 4. Questions for Further Clinical Assessment

These are open questions about what the supplied summary cannot resolve. None presupposes that the mechanism or measurement issue named has already been observed.

1. **Gait events.** Can individual toe-off and other event timings be retrieved from the release for this participant? Without them, no interval above can be placed in stance or swing, or linked to a functional task.
2. **Matched reference.** Is a slow-speed (or age-matched) healthy reference, or a leave-one-out normal limit set, obtainable? If not, is the team prepared to report all deviations as differences from an unmatched pooled reference only?
3. **Per-muscle percentiles.** Can the per-muscle healthy 95th percentiles for the max |z| statistic be supplied for GAS, RF, VL, BF and ST, so that VL (4.2) and ST (3.7) can be positioned rather than left indeterminate?
4. **Signal quality.** Have raw-signal quality, crosstalk, electrode placement and baseline checks been performed for this recording? This input contains no such audit, so whether any measurement artifact exists is currently unknown — particularly relevant for the channels with continuous above-threshold coverage (RF, ERS) and the largest max |z| values (GAS, RF).
5. **Stride-level data.** Are individual strides available? The averaged envelopes cannot show whether the wrapping intervals and coverage values reflect a consistent pattern across strides or an average of dissimilar strides.
6. **Contralateral data.** Are non-paretic-side kinematics and EMG available for a within-subject side comparison? Note that the EMG normalization denominator is side-specific in this release, so amplitude values would not be directly comparable between sides.
7. **Step-length definition.** Would the instantaneous definition change the +16.1% symmetry value for this participant, and which definition does the study protocol treat as primary?
8. **Contextual fields.** Are age, sex, time post-stroke and stroke clinical scores present in the release for this subject, and was any orthosis, assistive device or handrail/treadmill support in use during capture? Any of these could bear on how the speed and deviation values are framed.
9. **Sample accounting.** For any pooled EMG analysis using this subject, which denominator applies — the 614 evaluable combinations verified in the local audit, rather than the nominal 700 (86 lacked valid nonzero paired samples)?
10. **Clinical examination.** Would a bedside sagittal ROM, tone and selective-motor-control examination be undertaken to determine whether the measured ankle (18.4°), knee (42.7°) and hip (29.4°) ranges correspond to any passive restriction, which these data cannot address?
