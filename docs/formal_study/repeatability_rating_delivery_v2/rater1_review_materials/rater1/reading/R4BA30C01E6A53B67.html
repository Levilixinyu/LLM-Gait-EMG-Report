## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

**How these numbers must be read.** All curves are **ensemble-averaged envelopes**. Normalization uses a denominator that is participant-, side-, and muscle-specific (not shared between sides) in the checked stroke release, and the released mean curves are used without further amplitude normalization. Values are therefore **relative envelope amplitudes** — not neural drive, force, or strength — and muscles cannot be ranked as stronger or weaker from them. An average curve cannot show stride-to-stride variability, so nothing below describes what happened in any individual stride.

Detected intervals are regions where the **averaged** envelope exceeds **20% of that same curve's own peak**, with a minimum retained interval of 3% of the cycle. "Coverage" is the proportion of the averaged cycle above that threshold. These are properties of the averaged envelope under this rule; they are **not** physiological onset, offset, contraction duration, or inactivity outside the intervals. An interval written with X > Y wraps the 100%→0% boundary, i.e. the mean envelope remains above threshold across the cycle-defining initial-contact boundary.

**Interval structure.** All seven averaged envelopes are above their own 20% threshold across the initial-contact boundary, and coverage is high throughout (71–100%). BF and ST each retain several intervals separated by short below-threshold segments in the mid-cycle region; GAS, RF, VL and TA each show one or two retained intervals with the remainder of the cycle below threshold at the mean-envelope level. For ERS the reported interval spans the full analysis window at 100% coverage; note that coverage is reported as a rounded percentage, and a below-threshold segment shorter than the 3% retention rule or the displayed rounding cannot be excluded from these fields alone. The same caution applies to the coverage gaps elsewhere: they describe the averaged curve under this threshold, not silence in the muscle and not the same pattern in every stride. No interval is mapped here to clearance, push-off, weight acceptance, or any other phase function, and none is labelled early, delayed, or prolonged, since matched muscle-specific timing references and individual events are not supplied.

**Max |z| interpretation.** Max |z| is an **extreme-value statistic** — the largest standardized difference at any of 1001 phase points against the phase-specific healthy mean/SD — so the conventional 2-SD rule does not apply. In this dataset every one of 109 healthy controls exceeds |z| = 2 on at least one muscle, and per-muscle healthy 95th percentiles for this statistic run **3.65 (ERS) to 4.61 (TA)**, higher still under leave-one-out. Against that:
- **GAS 10.7** and **ERS 7.7** exceed the upper end of the supplied per-muscle 95th-percentile band (and ERS exceeds its own stated 3.65).
- **TA 4.1** is below TA's own 95th percentile of 4.61.
- **BF 2.3** is below the lower end (3.65) of the per-muscle band.
- **RF 4.3, VL 4.2, ST 4.1** fall inside the 3.65–4.61 band; the muscle-specific percentiles for these channels are not supplied, so they cannot be classified relative to their own healthy limits here.

"Above reference" in each row means the curve sat above the **phase-specific reference mean** at that location — not above a healthy percentile limit. The location of maximum |z| is the point of largest standardized difference; it is not necessarily where the envelope peaked and does not identify a gait event.

**Co-contraction indices.** CCI is the Falconer–Winter whole-cycle overlap index of the normalized envelopes (2 × common area / summed areas). It is not simultaneous activation time, not muscular force, and not a phase-specific result; it can be non-zero even when detected bursts do not overlap, because sub-threshold activity contributes. It depends on the normalization and envelope pipeline used here, so values and thresholds from other studies or algorithms do not transfer.
- **TA vs GAS = 0.67**, above the supplied healthy 2.5th–97.5th percentile interval of 0.24–0.59 and above the median of 0.39.
- **RF vs BF = 0.72**, inside the supplied interval of 0.48–0.83 and slightly above the median of 0.68.

Being outside that empirical interval does not establish pathological co-activation, spasticity, stiffness, or impaired control; being inside it does not establish physiologically normal co-activation or normal muscle function. Both comparisons inherit the reference limitation described in Section 1 (no speed-matched healthy data at 0.25 m/s).

**Per-joint reading.** GVS is an RMS summary of the difference from the reference mean waveform across the cycle. It does **not** indicate the direction of the difference (e.g. more flexion vs more extension) and does not localize the difference to any part of the cycle. Within this subject's four channels the largest GVS is at the knee (22.25), followed by ankle (16.27), hip (11.32) and pelvis (7.17); no per-joint healthy reference values are supplied in this input, so none of these can be classified as within or outside normal limits.

**ROM.** The reported ROMs describe the excursion of the averaged sagittal curves. ROM alone, without an appropriate matched comparison (which is not available at this walking speed), does not establish a clinical restriction at any joint. ## 4. Questions for Further Clinical Assessment

These are open questions about what additional evidence would be needed; none presupposes that the mechanism or measurement issue in question has been observed.

1. Are individual gait events — particularly paretic toe-off and contralateral events — available in the wider release for this participant, so that the detected envelope intervals could be placed against subject-specific event timing rather than cycle percentages alone?
2. Are stride-level (non-averaged) envelopes retrievable? Coverage, interval boundaries and CCI here derive from a mean curve and cannot indicate stride-to-stride consistency.
3. Are raw-signal quality records available (electrode placement, baseline, saturation, possible cross-talk), especially for the channels with the largest standardized differences (GAS, ERS) and for the ERS channel that is above threshold across the full reported window?
4. Was any orthosis, assistive device, handrail or physical assistance used during the recorded trials, and is that documented? This is not stated in the supplied summary and would affect how the kinematic and EMG summaries should be read.
5. Is a slow-speed or otherwise better-matched comparison group obtainable? At 0.25 m/s the available reference cohort provides no speed-matched comparison, which limits every deviation statistic reported above.
6. Are the muscle-specific healthy 95th-percentile values for max |z| (and ideally leave-one-out versions) available for RF, VL, ST, BF and GAS, rather than only the range endpoints for ERS and TA?
7. Are non-paretic-side kinematics and EMG available? Note that the normalization denominator is side-specific, so amplitudes would not be directly comparable across sides even if supplied.
8. Are coronal and transverse joint data, and per-joint GVS reference distributions, available to complement this sagittal-only, composite-referenced summary?
9. Are participant descriptors relevant to interpretation — age, sex, time post-stroke, and stroke clinical scores — documented elsewhere in the release for this subject?
10. Do the two step-length definitions (successive-footfall vs instantaneous) diverge for this participant, and is the instantaneous value available for comparison with the reported −46.3%?
