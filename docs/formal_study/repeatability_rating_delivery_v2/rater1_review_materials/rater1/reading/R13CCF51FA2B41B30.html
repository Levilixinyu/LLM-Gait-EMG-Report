## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

**How these numbers must be read.** The curves are ensemble-averaged envelopes, normalized to a shared maximum across the available strides (participant-, side- and muscle-specific denominator), with no further amplitude normalization. Peak values are therefore **relative envelope amplitudes**, not neural drive, force or strength, and muscles cannot be ranked as stronger or weaker from them. Intervals are the regions where the **averaged** envelope exceeds 20% of its own peak for at least 3% of the cycle; they are properties of that averaged curve under that rule, not physiological onsets, offsets, contraction durations, or evidence of inactivity outside the intervals. The same pattern should not be assumed in every individual stride, and an average curve cannot establish stride-to-stride variability. An interval written X%–Y% with X > Y wraps the 100%→0% boundary, i.e. the mean envelope remains above threshold across the cycle-defining initial-contact boundary.

**Observations at the mean-envelope level.**
- All seven envelopes were above threshold across the cycle boundary, and reported coverage is high in every channel (64–100%). Coverage this high means the averaged envelope stayed above 20% of its own peak over most of the analysis window; because the threshold is relative to each curve's own peak, high coverage describes envelope shape (a comparatively flat, non-peaked average curve) and not amplitude, effort or continuous contraction.
- ERS is reported as one interval spanning the full analysis window with coverage 100%. At the displayed precision this indicates no retained below-threshold segment in the averaged envelope; a sub-threshold segment shorter than the displayed rounding or shorter than the 3%-of-cycle retention rule cannot be excluded from these fields alone. The same caution applies to TA (98%) and BF (95%): high coverage does not establish that there was no below-threshold segment.
- RF, VL, BF and ST show several short retained intervals adjacent to a long wrapping interval, i.e. brief dips of the averaged envelope below the 20% line separating otherwise contiguous supra-threshold regions. These are envelope-threshold crossings, not identified bursts, onsets or offsets.
- Onsets listed at 0% (ERS) are the analysis-window boundary, not a physiological onset.
- I cannot describe any of this timing as normal, early, delayed or prolonged: individual toe-off and matched muscle-specific timing references are not supplied, and gait-cycle percentages alone do not identify gait phases. Anatomically, GAS is an ankle plantar flexor, TA a dorsiflexor, RF and VL knee extensors (RF also a hip flexor), BF and ST knee flexors/hip extensors, and ERS a trunk extensor; these anatomical actions are named here without mapping any interval to a functional task such as clearance, push-off or weight acceptance.

**Max |z| interpretation.** Max |z| is an extreme-value statistic — the largest standardized difference from the phase-specific reference mean across all 1001 phase points — not a single-observation z-score, and its location marks the largest standardized difference, not necessarily the peak of the envelope and not a gait event. "Above/below reference" means relative to the phase-specific reference **mean**, not relative to a healthy percentile limit. The conventional 2-SD rule does not apply: in this dataset every one of 109 healthy controls exceeds |z| = 2 on at least one muscle. Per-muscle healthy 95th percentiles for this statistic are stated only as a range, 3.65 (ERS) to 4.61 (TA), with higher values under leave-one-out. Accordingly:
- **GAS 6.5** exceeds the upper end of that stated per-muscle 95th-percentile range, so it is a large standardized difference by any of the supplied per-muscle limits. It does not by itself establish abnormality of physiology or a stroke-specific effect, particularly given the absent speed match.
- **TA 3.4** is below its own supplied 95th percentile (4.61); **ERS 2.2** is below its own supplied 95th percentile (3.65).
- **RF 3.8, VL 3.0, BF 2.2, ST 3.7** cannot be classified: their specific per-muscle 95th percentiles are not supplied, only the 3.65–4.61 band endpoints for other muscles. RF and ST lie above the lowest endpoint of that band and VL and BF below it, but their own thresholds are unavailable.

**Co-contraction indices.** CCI here is the Falconer–Winter whole-cycle overlap index of the normalized envelopes (2 × common area / summed areas). It is not simultaneous activation time, muscular force, a phase-specific result, or a measure of stiffness or motor control, and it can be non-zero even where the detected intervals of a pair do not overlap, because sub-threshold envelope area contributes. It depends on the normalization and envelope pipeline used here; values and thresholds from other studies or algorithms are not transferable.
- **TA vs GAS = 0.71**, above the upper endpoint of the supplied healthy interval 0.24–0.59 (median 0.39). Lying outside this pooled interval does not establish pathological co-activation, spasticity, impaired control or a diagnosis; the reference is not speed- or age-matched, and this participant walks below the reference cohort's speed floor.
- **RF vs BF = 0.72**, inside the supplied healthy interval 0.48–0.83 and slightly above the supplied median of 0.68. Lying inside the interval does not establish physiologically normal co-activation or normal muscle function.

**Overall comparability.** Because the reference is pooled across sides and ages and contains no walker slower than 0.80 m/s, the kinematic and EMG deviations described above should be read as differences from that particular empirical reference under these particular processing choices. They cannot be attributed wholly, or in defined proportion, to stroke pathology, and they do not establish a clinical diagnosis or severity grade.
