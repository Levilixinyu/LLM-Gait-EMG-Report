## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

**How these numbers must be read.** The curves are ensemble-averaged envelopes; for each participant, side and muscle, normalization used a shared maximum across available strides before averaging, with a participant-, side- and muscle-specific denominator (not shared between sides). Amplitudes are therefore **relative envelope values** — not neural drive, force, or strength — and muscles cannot be ranked as stronger or weaker from them. An average curve carries no information about stride-to-stride variability, so the mean-envelope patterns below must not be assumed to recur in every individual stride. "Above-threshold interval" means only that the averaged envelope exceeded 20% of its own peak for at least 3% of the cycle; it is not physiological onset, offset, contraction duration, or inactivity elsewhere. An interval written X%–Y% with X > Y wraps the 100%→0% boundary, i.e., the mean envelope remained above threshold across the cycle-defining initial-contact boundary.

**Coverage and boundary pattern.** All seven recorded channels show a single retained interval that is continuous across the initial-contact boundary at the mean-envelope level, with coverage ranging from 63% (BF) to the reported 100% (ERS). For ERS the reported interval spans the full analysis window, so no below-threshold region met the retention criteria; because the coverage figure is rounded, it should not be read as excluding brief below-threshold samples at finer resolution. For the other six channels, the regions outside the retained intervals are simply regions that did not meet the 20%-of-own-peak plus 3%-duration criteria — not demonstrated silence. Anatomically these muscles are ankle plantarflexors (GAS), knee extensors (RF, VL), knee flexors/hip extensors (BF, ST), an ankle dorsiflexor (TA) and a trunk extensor (ERS); no interval here can be mapped to a gait-phase function such as clearance, push-off or weight acceptance, and no timing can be called normal, early, delayed or prolonged, because individual toe-off and matched muscle-specific timing references are not supplied.

**Standardized deviations (max |z|).** Max |z| is an extreme-value statistic across all 1001 phase points, not a single-observation z-score, and the conventional 2-SD rule does not apply: in this dataset every one of 109 healthy controls exceeds |z| = 2 on at least one muscle, and per-muscle healthy 95th percentiles for this statistic run from 3.65 (ERS) to 4.61 (TA), higher still under leave-one-out. Against that framing:
- **BF (5.1)** exceeds even the highest per-muscle 95th percentile quoted in this dataset (4.61), so it is large relative to any of the supplied per-muscle limits; the BF-specific percentile itself is not supplied, and leave-one-out limits would be higher.
- **TA (3.9)** lies below the supplied TA 95th percentile of 4.61.
- **ERS (2.5)** lies below the supplied ERS 95th percentile of 3.65, and is the only channel whose extreme deviation is *below* the phase-specific reference mean.
- **RF (4.1), GAS (3.5), VL (3.3), ST (3.2)** fall inside the 3.65–4.61 span of per-muscle limits or below its lower end; because the channel-specific percentiles are not supplied for these muscles, they cannot be individually classified as inside or outside the healthy range.

The location of each max |z| is where the standardized difference from the phase-specific reference mean is largest — it is not necessarily where the envelope peaks, and it does not identify a gait event. "Above reference" means above the reference *mean* at that phase point, not above a healthy percentile limit. Because no speed-matched reference exists for this subject, the direction and size of these differences cannot be attributed to stroke pathology as opposed to slow walking.

**Co-contraction indices.** CCI is the Falconer–Winter whole-cycle overlap index of the normalized envelopes (2 × common area / summed areas). It is a single index for the entire cycle — not simultaneous activation time, not muscular force, and not a phase-specific result — and it can be non-zero even where detected intervals do not overlap, because sub-threshold envelope area contributes. It is dependent on this normalization and envelope pipeline, so values and thresholds from other studies or algorithms do not transfer.
- **TA vs GAS = 0.54**, inside the supplied healthy interval 0.24–0.59 and above the supplied median of 0.39, lying near the upper endpoint of that interval.
- **RF vs BF = 0.73**, inside the supplied healthy interval 0.48–0.83 and above the supplied median of 0.68.

Falling inside these empirical intervals does not establish physiologically normal co-activation or normal muscle function, and falling outside them would not establish pathological co-activation, stiffness, impaired motor control, or any diagnosis. The same limited-comparability caveat applies as above.
