The speed band label denotes a speed interval only. It does not report measured walking independence, safety, assistive-device use, or real-world community mobility, and none of those were supplied.

The step length symmetry index of **−46.3%** is negative, which by the stated formula means the **paretic step is the shorter** of the two. This value was computed with the successive-footfall definition (each heel at its own initial contact); the alternative instantaneous definition can yield a different number for some subjects, so the magnitude is definition-dependent and should not be compared directly to symmetry values computed another way.

**Reference-comparability limit (applies to everything in Sections 2 and 3).** The deviation reference used for this subject is 138 pooled healthy subjects (1001 phase points, main_cohort_excel), pooling both body sides and all ages; it is not matched to this participant. At **0.25 m/s this subject falls well below that floor, so no speed-matched healthy reference exists in this dataset.** Every deviation reported below therefore conflates slow-walking effects with any stroke-related control changes, and these data cannot separate the two. Deviations are described as differences from an empirical reference; they do not by themselves establish a disease effect, a pathological mechanism, or a clinical diagnosis.

**Channels supplied / not supplied.** Seven EMG channels (GAS, RF, VL, BF, ST, TA, ERS) and four sagittal kinematic channels (ankle, knee, hip, pelvis) are supplied. Coronal and transverse plane kinematics are not included in this dataset. ## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

**What these numbers are.** The curves are ensemble-averaged envelopes. Normalization used a shared maximum across available strides per participant, side and muscle (the denominator is not shared between sides), with no further amplitude normalization in this pipeline. Amplitudes are therefore **relative envelope values** — not neural drive, force or strength — and muscles must not be ranked as stronger or weaker from them. An average curve cannot demonstrate stride-to-stride variability, and none of the patterns below can be assumed to recur in every individual stride.

Intervals below are properties of the **averaged envelope** under one rule: envelope exceeds 20% of that same curve's own peak, with a minimum retained interval of 3% of the cycle. They are not physiological onsets, offsets, contraction durations, or evidence of inactivity outside the listed regions. An interval written X%–Y% with X > Y wraps the 100%→0% boundary. Coverage percentages are as reported.

**Reading these rows.**

- All seven averaged envelopes remain above their own 20% threshold across the cycle-defining initial-contact boundary, and reported coverage is high in every channel (71%–100%). This describes the shape of the mean envelope relative to its own peak; it is not a statement that any muscle contracted continuously, nor that timing is "prolonged," which cannot be judged without matched muscle-specific timing references.
- **ERS** is reported as 0%–100% with 100% coverage. Under this rule that means no region of the mean envelope met the criteria for falling below threshold as a retained sub-threshold segment; the supplied summary does not itself establish that literally no sample fell below 20% of peak, and a rounded 100% cannot be unrounded here.
- For the other six muscles, the gaps between listed intervals (e.g. BF at roughly 76%–82%, TA at roughly 63%–75%) are regions where no interval met the threshold-plus-duration criteria. "No retained interval" is not evidence of muscle inactivity.
- **BF** and **ST** show several short retained intervals separated by narrow gaps in the mid-cycle range. This is a property of the averaged envelope crossing a fixed relative threshold, and short gaps near the 3%-duration rule are sensitive to that rule; they should not be read as discrete physiological bursts in individual strides.
- Peak normalized amplitudes cluster between 0.45 and 0.63. Because each curve is scaled by a participant-, side- and muscle-specific maximum, these values are **not comparable between muscles as an indication of relative effort or output**.

**Max |z| interpretation.** Max |z| is an extreme-value statistic — the largest standardized difference from the phase-specific pooled reference mean across all 1001 phase points — not a single-observation z-score. In this dataset every one of 109 healthy controls exceeds |z| = 2 on at least one muscle, and per-muscle healthy 95th percentiles for this statistic run 3.65 (ERS) to 4.61 (TA), higher under leave-one-out. The conventional 2-SD rule therefore does not apply. Against those per-muscle healthy percentiles: **BF (2.3)** and **TA (4.1)** sit below their respective healthy 95th-percentile values; **RF (4.3)**, **VL (4.2)** and **ST (4.1)** are of the same order as the quoted range of percentiles; **GAS (10.7)** and **ERS (7.7)** are substantially larger than any per-muscle 95th percentile quoted. "Above reference" means the curve lies above the reference **mean** at that phase point, not above a healthy percentile limit. The stated location (e.g. GAS at 68%) is where the largest standardized difference occurs — it is not necessarily where the envelope peaks and it does not identify a gait event or phase. Given the absence of any speed-matched reference, none of these values can be attributed to stroke pathology rather than to walking at 0.25 m/s.

**Co-contraction indices.** CCI here is the Falconer–Winter index over the full continuous envelopes (2 × common area / summed areas) for the whole cycle. It can be non-zero even when detected intervals do not overlap, because sub-threshold envelope activity contributes. It is **not** simultaneous activation time, muscular force, stiffness, or a phase-specific result, and it depends on the normalization and envelope methods used here — values and thresholds from other studies or algorithms do not transfer.

- **TA vs GAS = 0.67.** The healthy reference interval for this dataset is 0.24–0.59 (median 0.39). This value lies above the upper endpoint of that interval.
- **RF vs BF = 0.72.** The healthy reference interval is 0.48–0.83 (median 0.68). This value lies inside the interval, slightly above the median.

Lying inside the interval does not establish physiologically normal co-activation or normal muscle function; lying outside it does not establish pathological co-activation, impaired motor control, spasticity or any diagnosis. The same speed and matching limitation applies to both indices.

**Dataset audit note.** The local array audit verified 614 evaluable muscle/subject combinations out of 700; 86 lacked valid nonzero paired samples. This is background on the release and is not a statement about this subject's channels, all seven of which returned values above.

The composite score is the RMS of the per-joint Gait Variable Scores over the sagittal channels supplied — a "GPS-like" value, not the validated 9-variable Gait Profile Score, so published GPS cut-offs do not apply.

**Comparison with the supplied reference.** The composite score of 15.32 is above the supplied healthy composite reference median of 5.1 and above the 95th percentile of 10.7. These are pooled calibration percentiles from a reference not matched to this subject and not equivalent to matched leave-one-out normal limits. Among the individual channels, the knee GVS (22.25) is the largest contributor, followed by ankle (16.27), then hip (11.32) and pelvis (7.17); no per-joint healthy reference values were supplied, so these per-joint scores can be ranked against each other but not classified against a normal limit.

**What GVS and the composite do and do not tell you.** Both are summary magnitudes of difference from the stated reference across the cycle. Neither localizes *when* in the cycle the difference occurs, nor in which direction (e.g. more flexed vs more extended), nor which part of the waveform drives it. A larger score indicates a larger average discrepancy from the pooled reference mean curve — nothing more.

**ROM.** Ankle sagittal ROM of 11.6° and pelvic sagittal ROM of 11.4° are the smallest values in the set, and knee (54.3°) and hip (46.8°) are the largest. ROM alone, without an appropriate matched comparison (none is supplied for ROM here), does not establish a clinical restriction, contracture, or excessive motion. Because the subject's speed of 0.25 m/s lies far below the reference cohort's 0.80–1.79 m/s span, joint excursions and waveform shapes expected at very low speed cannot be distinguished from stroke-specific changes in these data. The kinematic deviations are accurately described as differences from this pooled empirical reference; they do not on their own establish a disease effect or support a diagnosis.

## 4. Questions for Further Clinical Assessment

The following are open questions raised by the limits of this summary. None presupposes that the mechanism or measurement issue in question has been observed.

1. **Speed-matched comparison.** Is a slow-walking reference cohort (or a within-subject repeat at a different self-selected speed) available or obtainable, so that deviations from the pooled reference can be examined separately from speed effects? Without this, the composite of 15.32 and the large GAS and ERS max |z| values remain uninterpretable as to cause.
2. **Event timing.** Can individual toe-off and other gait events for this subject be retrieved from the release? Their absence is why no interval in Section 2 could be assigned to a gait phase or function, and why no timing could be called early, delayed or prolonged.
3. **Contralateral data.** Are non-paretic-side kinematics and EMG available? Note that EMG normalization denominators are side-specific in this release, so cross-side amplitude comparison would need explicit handling.
4. **Signal quality.** Has a raw-signal quality review (baseline noise, movement artifact, ECG or cross-talk contamination, electrode placement documentation) been performed for these channels? This was not supplied here, and it is one factor that could be examined when a mean envelope shows very high coverage — as ERS (100%), GAS (90%) and BF (89%) do — before any physiological reading is entertained.
5. **Stride-level data.** Are individual stride envelopes and stride-count information retrievable? The averaged curves used here cannot show consistency or variability across strides, which limits how far any of the described envelope patterns can be generalized to individual steps.
6. **Threshold sensitivity.** Would the fragmented BF and ST interval structure persist under a different relative threshold or minimum-duration rule? The short intervals and gaps reported are properties of the 20%-of-own-peak / 3%-of-cycle criteria.
7. **Step-length definition.** Would the −46.3% symmetry index change materially under the instantaneous definition? Confirming which definition is used in any comparison dataset is needed before this value is compared externally.
8. **Clinical and demographic context.** Are age, sex, time post-stroke, clinical scores, and observational gait or examination findings available in the release to place these summary numbers in context? None were supplied to this interpretation.

**Overall.** This report describes averaged-envelope threshold findings, two whole-cycle co-contraction indices, and sagittal kinematic deviation magnitudes for the paretic side of one stroke-cohort participant, compared against a pooled, unmatched healthy reference with no speed overlap. The comparisons quantify differences from that reference only; they do not identify a mechanism, establish a disease effect, or support any clinical diagnosis, and no treatment recommendation is made or implied.
