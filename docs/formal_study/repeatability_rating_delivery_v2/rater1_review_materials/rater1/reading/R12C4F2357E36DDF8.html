## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

**How these numbers must be read.** Each curve is an **ensemble-averaged envelope** across the available strides, normalized to a shared maximum across strides for that participant, side, and muscle (in the checked stroke release the denominator is participant-, side- and muscle-specific and is not shared between sides). Amplitudes are therefore **relative envelope values within each muscle's own scale** — they are not neural drive, force, or strength, and muscles must not be ranked against each other as stronger or weaker. Detected intervals are properties of this averaged envelope under a **20%-of-own-peak threshold with a 3%-of-cycle minimum retained duration**; they are not physiological onsets, offsets, contraction durations, or evidence of inactivity outside the intervals. Because these are averages, no statement can be made about whether the same pattern occurs in any individual stride.

**Timing.** Because no toe-off and no matched muscle-specific normal timing are supplied, the percentages above cannot be assigned to stance, swing, loading response, mid-stance, or pre-swing, and no conventional 60% split may be substituted. **Max |z| locations.** Each max |z| is an **extreme-value statistic** — the largest standardized difference from the reference across all 1001 phase points — not a single-observation z-score, and its location marks the largest standardized difference, **not** necessarily the time of peak envelope amplitude or any gait event. The conventional 2-SD rule does not apply: in this dataset every one of 109 healthy controls exceeds |z| = 2 on at least one muscle, and per-muscle healthy 95th percentiles for this statistic run from 3.65 (ERS) to 4.61 (TA), higher still under leave-one-out. Against that framing: GAS (12.6), ST (8.6) and RF (7.2) are numerically well above the highest per-muscle healthy 95th percentile quoted; VL (5.5) and ERS (4.4) exceed and BF (4.8) exceeds the general upper end of those percentiles; TA (2.9) is below them. "Above reference" means above the **phase-specific reference mean at that location**, not above a healthy percentile limit. TA's max |z| of 2.9 is the one channel sitting **below** the phase-specific reference mean at its extreme location (71%). These magnitudes describe difference from a non-speed-matched pooled reference and cannot be attributed wholly to stroke pathology.

**Co-contraction indices.** CCI is the Falconer–Winter index over the **full continuous envelopes** of the whole cycle (2 × common area / summed areas). It is a whole-cycle overlap index of normalized envelopes — not time spent simultaneously active, not muscular force, and not a phase-specific result — and it can be non-zero even when detected bursts do not overlap, because sub-threshold envelope area contributes.
- **TA vs GAS = 0.46**, inside the supplied healthy interval 0.24–0.59 and above the supplied median of 0.39.
- **RF vs BF = 0.83**, which sits exactly at the upper endpoint of the supplied healthy interval 0.48–0.83 and above the supplied median of 0.68.

Being inside this empirical interval does not establish physiologically normal co-activation or normal muscle function, and sitting at or beyond an endpoint does not establish pathological co-activation, stiffness, impaired motor control, or any diagnosis. These values depend on the normalization and envelope pipeline used here and must not be compared with thresholds from other studies or algorithms.

3. **Signal quality.** Are raw-signal or per-stride quality checks (baseline noise, movement artefact, electrode crosstalk, saturation) available for the channels with continuous 100% above-threshold coverage — RF, VL, BF, ST, ERS? A question worth asking, not a finding: whether elevated baseline or artefact could contribute to a mean envelope that never falls below 20% of its own peak needs the raw data to answer.
4. **Stride-level data.** Can the individual strides behind these ensemble averages be examined? The averaged curves cannot show stride-to-stride variability, and it remains unknown whether the reported interval pattern (particularly the TA 88%–89% gap and the GAS 8%–13% gap) recurs in individual strides or is an artefact of averaging.
5. **Contralateral and non-sagittal data.** Are non-paretic-side EMG and kinematics, and coronal/transverse joint data, available for comparison? Note that EMG amplitudes are not comparable across sides in this release because the normalization denominator is side-specific.
6. **Clinical descriptors.** Are age, sex, time post-stroke, and stroke clinical scores available in the release for this participant? None were supplied here, and they would be needed before any of these quantitative differences could be placed in a clinical context.
7. **Step-length definition.** Would the instantaneous step-length definition yield a materially different symmetry index for this subject, and is a reference interval for this index available? The −4.8% value is definition-specific and currently uncompared.
8. **Within-participant follow-up.** If repeat sessions exist or are planned, are the acquisition, normalization, and envelope parameters identical? CCI and max |z| values from this pipeline are not transferable across algorithms or studies.
