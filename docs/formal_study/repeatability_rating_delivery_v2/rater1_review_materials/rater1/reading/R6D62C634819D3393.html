## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

**How these numbers must be read.** All EMG values come from ensemble-averaged envelopes. Normalization uses a shared maximum across the available strides before averaging, with the denominator specific to participant, side, and muscle in the stroke release and not shared between sides; the released mean curves are used without further amplitude normalization. Amplitudes are therefore **relative envelope values only** — not neural drive, force, or strength — and muscles must not be ranked as stronger or weaker from them. An averaged curve cannot demonstrate stride-to-stride variability, so the pattern described here is a property of the mean envelope and should not be assumed to recur in every individual stride. Detected intervals are regions where the averaged envelope exceeds 20% of that same curve's own peak for at least 3% of the cycle; they are not physiological onsets, offsets, contraction durations, or proof of inactivity elsewhere. Coverage values are rounded, so a stated percentage below 100% does not by itself establish where or whether sub-threshold segments occurred. An interval written X%–Y% with X > Y continues across the 100%→0% boundary, i.e., the mean envelope remains above threshold across the cycle-defining initial-contact boundary.

The GAS envelope's single retained interval begins at 4%, numerically close to the 0% initial-contact boundary but not at the analysis-window edge. RF shows the highest coverage (91%) and BF the most fragmented set of retained intervals (three). None of these descriptions is converted here into a functional role such as clearance, weight acceptance, or push-off; anatomically, GAS acts as an ankle plantar flexor, TA as a dorsiflexor, RF/VL as knee extensors (RF also a hip flexor), BF/ST as knee flexors and hip extensors, and ERS as a trunk extensor — but no interval is mapped to a gait-phase function, and no timing here is labelled normal, early, delayed, or prolonged, since the matched muscle-specific timing references required for such a label are not supplied.

**Max |z| interpretation.** Each max |z| is an extreme-value statistic: the single largest standardized difference from the phase-specific reference mean across all 1001 phase points. Its location marks where that standardized difference is largest, **not** necessarily where the envelope peaks and not a gait event. "Above reference" means above the phase-specific reference *mean* at that point, not above a healthy percentile limit. The conventional 2-SD rule does not apply: in this dataset every one of 109 healthy controls exceeds |z| = 2 on at least one muscle, and per-muscle healthy 95th percentiles for this statistic range from 3.65 (ERS) to 4.61 (TA), higher still under leave-one-out. Against those stated endpoints: BF (3.1) and TA (3.3) lie below the lowest listed 95th percentile and below TA's own 4.61, respectively; ST (5.6), ERS (5.8, versus ERS's own 3.65), RF (7.4), VL (8.3), and especially GAS (20.3) exceed the upper end of that percentile range. Exceeding these pooled calibration percentiles is a descriptive statement about distance from an unmatched, non-speed-matched reference; it does not establish abnormality, pathology, or a diagnosis, and the leave-one-out limits would be more permissive still.

**Co-contraction indices.** CCI here is the Falconer–Winter index computed over the full continuous envelopes (2 × common area / summed areas) for the whole cycle. It is a whole-cycle overlap index of normalized envelopes — not a duration of simultaneous activation, not muscular force, and not a phase-specific result — and it can be non-zero even where detected bursts do not overlap, because sub-threshold activity contributes. It is also dependent on this normalization and envelope pipeline, so values and thresholds from other studies or algorithms are not transferable.
- **TA vs GAS = 0.60**, against the supplied healthy median 0.39 (2.5th–97.5th pct 0.24–0.59). This value sits just outside the upper endpoint of that empirical interval, by 0.01.
- **RF vs BF = 0.66**, against the supplied healthy median 0.68 (0.48–0.83). This value lies inside the interval, slightly below the median.

Being inside the interval does not establish physiologically normal co-activation or normal muscle function; being marginally outside it does not establish pathological co-activation, spasticity, stiffness, impaired motor control, or any diagnosis — particularly given that no speed-matched reference exists for this participant.
