## 1. Objective Findings

**Participant and side.** Sub21_P is a member of the stroke cohort. All findings below describe the **paretic side**. The analysis cycle runs from initial contact of the paretic limb (0%) to the next initial contact of the same limb (100%); these are two successive occurrences of the same event, not a single instant.

**Supplied spatiotemporal values**
- Gait speed: **0.34 m/s**, which falls in the "household ambulator (<0.4 m/s)" speed band. This label denotes a speed band only; it does not measure independence, safety, or real-world community mobility.
- Cadence: **74.1 steps/min**.
- Step length symmetry index: **+15.1%**, using the **successive-footfall** definition (each heel at its own initial contact). By the stated formula, a positive value indicates the **paretic step is the longer** of the two. Note that the alternative instantaneous definition yields a different value in some subjects; the value here should not be quoted without its definition.
- Composite kinematic deviation score (GPS-like): **20.60** (RMS of the sagittal-plane Gait Variable Scores; not the validated 9-variable GPS).

**Reference used.** Kinematic deviations are computed against a **pooled reference of 138 healthy subjects** (1001 phase points, main_cohort_excel), pooling both body sides and all ages. It is neither age- nor speed-matched to this participant.

**Speed-support limitation (central to this report).** The healthy reference cohort's self-selected speeds span 0.80–1.79 m/s. At 0.34 m/s this participant is **well below that floor**, so **no speed-matched healthy reference exists in this dataset**. Every deviation reported below therefore conflates the effects of very slow walking with any stroke-related control changes, and the two cannot be separated from these data. Deviations should be read as differences from an unmatched empirical reference, not as measures of a disease effect, severity, or a clinical diagnosis.

**Channels available and not available.**
- Sagittal-plane kinematics supplied for ankle, knee, hip and pelvis. **Coronal and transverse plane data are not included** in this input.
- Seven EMG envelope channels supplied (GAS, RF, VL, BF, ST, TA, ERS), all on the paretic side.
- **Not supplied in this task:** individual toe-off or any intermediate gait event, muscle-specific matched normal timing references, stride-level variability, raw-signal quality checks, and expert plots. Age, sex, time post-stroke, clinical scores and non-paretic-side data are outside this input; their absence here does not mean they are absent from the source release.
- Because no intermediate event is supplied, all timing below is expressed as **percentages of the analysis cycle**. Percentages alone do not identify stance, swing, loading response, pre-swing or push-off, and no conventional 60% split is applied.

## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

**How to read these numbers.** Each row describes one **ensemble-averaged envelope**. Normalization used a shared maximum across available strides for that participant, side and muscle before averaging (denominator not shared between sides); individual strides were not rescaled to their own peaks. Amplitudes are therefore **relative envelope values** — not neural drive, force, or strength — and muscles must not be ranked as stronger or weaker from them. An averaged curve cannot establish stride-to-stride variability, and the pattern described here should not be assumed to recur in every individual stride.

Intervals are regions where the **averaged envelope** exceeded **20% of that curve's own peak**, with a minimum retained length of 3% of the cycle. These are properties of the averaged envelope under that rule — not physiological onset, offset, contraction duration, or inactivity outside the intervals. An onset written as 0% is the analysis-window boundary. Intervals written X%–Y% with X > Y wrap the 100%→0% boundary, i.e. the mean envelope remained above threshold across the cycle-defining initial-contact boundary.

**Observations at the mean-envelope level.**
- Coverage is high across all seven channels (69–100%). BF, ST and ERS are reported at **100% coverage with a single boundary-crossing interval**, i.e. the supplied evidence establishes no below-threshold segment in those averaged envelopes. For GAS, RF, VL and TA, coverage is reported below 100%; the segments outside the retained intervals were simply not above the 20%-of-own-peak criterion for at least 3% of the cycle, which does not establish muscular inactivity there.
- RF, VL, BF, ST, TA and ERS all show **boundary continuity** — the averaged envelope remained above threshold across the initial-contact boundary. GAS has a retained interval ending at 100% and another beginning at 0%, but is listed without the boundary-continuity flag, so continuity across the boundary is not asserted here.
- GAS shows two short retained fragments (78–82%, 96–100%) separated by sub-threshold regions of the mean curve; these fragmentation details are features of the averaged envelope under this threshold rule.
- Anatomically, these channels span ankle plantarflexors (GAS) and dorsiflexors (TA), knee extensors (RF, VL), knee flexors/hip extensors (BF, ST) and the erector spinae (ERS). **No interval is assigned to a gait-phase function** such as clearance, push-off or weight acceptance, and no timing is characterized as normal, early, delayed or prolonged, because matched muscle-specific timing references and intermediate events are not supplied.

**Max |z| values.** Max |z| is an **extreme-value statistic** — the largest standardized difference across all 1001 phase points against the pooled reference mean/SD at each phase. It is not a single-observation z-score, and the conventional 2-SD rule does not apply: in this dataset every one of 109 healthy controls exceeds |z| = 2 on at least one muscle, and per-muscle healthy 95th percentiles for this statistic range from 3.65 (ERS) to 4.61 (TA), higher still under leave-one-out. Against that background, RF (3.2), BF (3.9), ST (3.9) and TA (5.1) are broadly within or near the range this statistic reaches in healthy controls; ERS (3.9) sits above the quoted ERS 95th percentile of 3.65; VL (5.4) and especially **GAS (16.8)** are considerably larger. "Above reference" means the curve lay above the **phase-specific reference mean** at that location — not above a healthy percentile limit. The location of maximum |z| (e.g. 57% for GAS) is where the standardized difference was largest; it is **not** necessarily the time of peak envelope amplitude and does not identify a gait event. These percentiles are pooled calibration values, not matched leave-one-out limits for this participant, and none of these values establishes a disease effect.

**Co-contraction indices.** CCI here is the Falconer–Winter index computed on the **full continuous normalized envelopes over the whole cycle** (2 × common area / summed areas). It is a whole-cycle overlap index of envelope shape — **not** simultaneous activation time, not muscular force, and not a phase-specific result. It can be non-zero even where detected bursts do not overlap, because sub-threshold activity contributes. It depends on the normalization and envelope pipeline used here; values and thresholds from other studies or algorithms are not transferable.

- **TA vs GAS = 0.59** — at the upper endpoint of the supplied healthy interval (median 0.39, 2.5th–97.5th pct 0.24–0.59).
- **RF vs BF = 0.76** — within the supplied healthy interval (median 0.68, 2.5th–97.5th pct 0.48–0.83), above the median.

Lying within the supplied interval does not establish physiologically normal co-activation or normal muscle function; lying at or outside an endpoint does not establish pathological co-activation, spasticity, stiffness, impaired motor control or any diagnosis. The same speed-matching limitation applies to these indices.

**Interpretive limits.**
- GVS and the GPS-like composite are **scalar summaries of difference magnitude** from the stated reference. They do **not** localize the direction of the waveform difference (greater or lesser flexion/extension), do not identify which portion of the cycle contributes, and do not map to any gait phase.
- ROM alone does not establish a clinical restriction. The largest per-joint deviation scores here are hip (29.35) and knee (22.59), while the pelvic ROM (2.9°) is small in absolute terms — but without an appropriate matched comparison, neither observation establishes a restriction, a contracture, or a specific mechanical cause.
- The reference is a **pooled 138-subject healthy cohort**, unmatched for age and walking at 0.80–1.79 m/s. At 0.34 m/s this participant falls outside that speed span entirely; slow-walking effects and stroke-related changes are confounded in every number in this section. A speed inside an overall range would not itself prove a matched reference exists, and here the speed is not even inside the range.
- Consequently, these scores quantify **how different this participant's sagittal curves are from an unmatched empirical reference**. They do not establish a disease effect, a severity grade, statistical significance, or a clinical diagnosis. The same caution would apply to a control participant compared against this reference.

## 4. Questions for Further Clinical Assessment

These are open questions raised by the limits of the supplied data. None presupposes that the mechanism or measurement issue in question has been observed.

1. Are individual gait events (toe-off, opposite foot contact) available in the source release for this participant? If so, re-expressing the retained intervals against those events would allow timing statements that the present percentage-only framework cannot support.
2. Is a speed-matched or slow-walking reference cohort available from any source? Without one, the magnitude of the composite deviation (20.60) and the per-channel z-values cannot be separated from the effects of walking at 0.34 m/s.
3. Was raw-signal quality for the paretic GAS channel reviewed? The max |z| of 16.8 is far larger than the other six channels; whether this reflects envelope shape, the participant-, side- and muscle-specific normalization denominator, a low reference SD at 57% of the cycle, or a signal-quality factor such as crosstalk or electrode placement cannot be determined from the supplied summary and would require inspection of the raw and per-stride data.
4. Are per-stride envelopes available? The high coverage values and boundary continuity described here are properties of the averaged curves only; stride-level data would be needed to know whether the same pattern holds within individual strides or whether it reflects averaging across variable strides.
5. Are non-paretic-side EMG and kinematic data available for this participant? Since the EMG normalization denominator is side-specific, any between-side amplitude comparison would need explicit methodological handling; side-to-side comparison of interval structure and CCI may still be informative.
6. Would coronal- and transverse-plane kinematics, if recorded, change the interpretation of the hip and knee deviation scores? The present composite reflects sagittal channels only.
7. Which step-length definition best matches the intended clinical question? The +15.1% value is definition-dependent (successive-footfall here), and the instantaneous definition can differ.
8. Are age, sex, time post-stroke and clinical scores retrievable from the release for this participant, to contextualize these measures beyond the pooled comparison used here?
