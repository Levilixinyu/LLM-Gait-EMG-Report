## 1. Objective Findings

**Participant and side.** Sub49_P is a member of the **stroke** cohort; the analyzed limb is the **paretic** side. The released cycle for this limb runs from initial contact of the paretic limb (0%) to the next initial contact of the same limb (100%); these are two successive occurrences of the same event, not one instant. No intermediate gait event (including individual toe-off) and no phase boundary are supplied in this task, so all timing below is expressed strictly as a percentage of the analysis cycle.

**Spatiotemporal values as supplied.**

The alternative instantaneous definition can yield a different value for some subjects; only the successive-footfall value is supplied here.

**Reference comparability — important limitation.** Deviation scores for this subject were computed against **138 pooled healthy subjects** (1001 phase points, source: main_cohort_excel), pooling both body sides and all ages; the reference is neither age- nor speed-matched, and this is not a leave-one-out run. Any difference from the reference therefore conflates slow-walking effects with stroke-related control changes; the two cannot be separated from these data, and no comparison below establishes a disease effect, a clinical diagnosis, or a mechanism.

**What is not available in this task.** Individual toe-off and other gait events, muscle-specific matched normal timing references, stride-to-stride variability, raw-signal quality checks, expert plots, non-paretic-side comparison data, age, sex, time post-stroke, and stroke clinical scores are **not supplied in this input**. Separately, the EMG array audit documents that 86 of 700 subject–muscle combinations lacked valid nonzero paired samples (614 evaluable) — this is a documented dataset-level property, not a statement about this subject's channels, all seven of which are reported below.

## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

**How these numbers must be read.** All values come from **ensemble-averaged envelopes**. For each participant, side, and muscle, normalization used a shared maximum across available strides before averaging (participant-, side-, and muscle-specific denominator; not shared between sides), with no further amplitude normalization in this pipeline. Amplitudes are therefore **relative envelope values** — not neural activation, force, or strength — and muscles must not be ranked as stronger or weaker from them. An averaged curve cannot show stride-to-stride variability, so none of the intervals below may be assumed to occur identically in every stride.

"Above-threshold interval" means the region where the **averaged envelope** exceeds **20% of that same curve's own peak**, with a minimum retained interval of 3% of the cycle. These are properties of the averaged envelope under that rule — not physiological onset, offset, contraction duration, or inactivity outside the intervals. An interval written X%–Y% with X > Y wraps the 100%→0% boundary, i.e. the mean envelope remains above threshold continuously across the cycle-defining initial-contact boundary. An onset written as 0% is the analysis-window edge, not a physiological onset.

**Boundary continuity.** Five channels (VL, BF, ST, TA, and — by their full-cycle report — RF and ERS) show mean-envelope continuity across the initial-contact boundary. GAS is the one channel with a single non-wrapping retained interval, beginning shortly after the 0% boundary (5%) and ending at 58%. Naming numerical proximity to that boundary is permitted; assigning any percentage to stance, swing, loading response, pre-swing or push-off is not, because no toe-off or phase boundary is supplied. Correspondingly, no interval here can be called normal, early, delayed or prolonged, and none can be mapped to a functional role such as clearance or weight acceptance. Anatomical actions may still be named descriptively (e.g. GAS as an ankle plantarflexor, TA as a dorsiflexor, VL/RF as knee extensors, BF/ST as hamstrings, ERS as an erector spinae channel).

**RF and ERS coverage.** Both are reported at 100% coverage with an interval spanning 0%–100%. At the displayed precision this indicates the averaged envelope was above the 20%-of-own-peak threshold throughout the reported cycle; because the value is rounded, this summary does not by itself exclude a brief sub-threshold segment below the reporting resolution. Note also that a 20%-of-own-peak criterion rescales to each curve's own maximum, so high coverage reflects a comparatively flat averaged envelope relative to its own peak rather than any absolute level of activity.

**Max |z| values.** The max |z| is an **extreme-value statistic** — the largest standardized difference across all 1001 phase points against the phase-specific reference mean/SD — not a single-observation z-score, and its location is the point of largest standardized difference, **not** necessarily the peak of the envelope and not a gait event. The conventional 2-SD rule does not apply: in this dataset every one of 109 healthy controls exceeds |z| = 2 on at least one muscle. "Above/below reference" denotes position relative to the phase-specific reference mean at that location only. Supplied per-muscle healthy 95th percentiles for this statistic are given for **ERS (3.65)** and **TA (4.61)** only, with the across-muscle range 3.65–4.61. Against those:
- **ERS 4.7** exceeds the supplied ERS 95th percentile (3.65).
- **TA 2.3** is below the supplied TA 95th percentile (4.61).
- For **GAS (4.5)**, RF, VL, BF and ST, no muscle-specific percentile is supplied here, so these values cannot be classified.

These percentiles are **pooled calibration values**, not matched leave-one-out normal limits (which run higher still); exceeding one does not establish abnormality, pathology, or a stroke-specific effect, particularly given the absent speed match described in Section 1.

**Co-contraction indices.** CCI here is the Falconer–Winter index computed over the **full continuous envelopes** across the whole cycle (2 × common area / summed areas). It is a whole-cycle overlap index of normalized envelopes — **not** simultaneous activation time, not muscular force, not a phase-specific result — and it can be non-zero even when detected burst intervals do not overlap, because sub-threshold activity contributes. Values depend on the normalization and envelope method used and must not be compared with thresholds from other studies or algorithms.
- **TA vs GAS = 0.43**, within the supplied healthy interval 0.24–0.59 and slightly above the supplied median of 0.39.
- **RF vs BF = 0.77**, within the supplied interval 0.48–0.83 and above the supplied median of 0.68, lying in the upper part of that interval, nearer the 97.5th-percentile endpoint than the median.

Falling inside these intervals does not establish physiologically normal co-activation or normal muscle function, and falling outside would not establish pathological co-activation, stiffness, impaired motor control, or any diagnosis.
