## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

**How these numbers were produced.** Each row describes an **ensemble-averaged envelope** normalized to a participant-, side- and muscle-specific maximum shared across strides (individual strides were not rescaled to their own peaks). Amplitudes are therefore **relative envelope values on a within-muscle scale** — they are not neural drive, force or strength, they cannot be used to rank muscles against one another, and they cannot be compared across sides. Because the curves are averages, nothing here describes stride-to-stride variability.

"Above-threshold interval" means the region where the **averaged envelope** exceeded 20% of that same curve's own peak, with a minimum retained length of 3% of the cycle. These are properties of the averaged envelope under that rule, **not** physiological onsets, offsets, burst durations, continuous contraction, or inactivity outside the intervals. An interval written with a wrap (X% > Y%) indicates the mean envelope remained above threshold across the cycle-defining boundary. "No interval detected" would mean only that no region met the threshold-plus-duration criteria. No individual stride is claimed to reproduce the averaged pattern.

**Reading the max |z| column.** Max |z| is the largest absolute standardized difference across all 1001 phase points relative to the phase-specific reference mean and SD. It is an **extreme-value statistic**: in this dataset all 109 healthy controls exceed |z| = 2 somewhere, and per-muscle healthy 95th percentiles for this statistic run from 3.65 (ERS) to 4.61 (TA), higher under leave-one-out. The conventional 2-SD rule does not apply. The location of the maximum is where the standardized difference is largest — **not** necessarily where the envelope peaks, and not a gait event. "Above/below reference" is relative to the phase-specific reference mean at that location, not to a healthy percentile limit.

**Observations at the mean-envelope level.**

- **All seven** recorded envelopes were above threshold across the cycle boundary, i.e. the averaged curve did not drop below its own 20%-of-peak line at the initial-contact boundary. For ERS the single retained interval spans 0%–100% with 100% coverage, so for that muscle the reported evidence does establish that the averaged envelope did not fall below threshold at any sampled phase. For the other six muscles, coverage is below 100% and sub-threshold portions of the averaged curve exist between the listed intervals; the rounded coverage figures alone do not license any finer statement about the hidden unrounded values.
- Coverage of the averaged envelope is high across the panel (52%–100%). Coverage is a property of this threshold-and-duration rule applied to an averaged curve; it is not "time spent contracting" and does not imply the same picture in every stride.
- The **TA** envelope is fragmented into eight retained intervals under the rule, while **VL** is a single wrapping interval covering 87%. These are descriptions of how the averaged curve crosses its own relative threshold, not descriptions of burst structure in any individual stride.
- **GAS** shows by far the largest standardized difference in the panel, |z| = 21.7 above the phase-specific reference mean at 81% of the cycle. This is large relative to the per-muscle healthy 95th percentiles quoted for this statistic (3.65–4.61 range across muscles); even so, given the absence of any speed-matched reference at 0.21 m/s, the value identifies where this averaged curve differs most from a non-matched reference and cannot be attributed to stroke pathology as opposed to very slow walking.
- **VL** (6.4 above, at 51%) and **ST** (6.4 above, at 46%) also exceed the quoted healthy 95th-percentile band for this statistic; **RF** (4.1 below, at 6%), **TA** (3.9 below, at 99%), **ERS** (3.5 above, at 88%) and **BF** (2.6 above, at 50%) sit nearer to or within that band. Note that RF and TA reach their largest standardized difference **below** the reference mean at their respective locations, while the others are above.
- The RF maximum at 6% and the TA maximum at 99% are numerically close to the initial-contact cycle boundary. That proximity is a statement about position in the cycle; no phase, event function or timing judgement (normal, early, delayed, prolonged) can be assigned, because matched muscle-specific timing references and individual events are not supplied.

**Co-contraction indices.** CCI here is the Falconer–Winter index computed on the **full continuous normalized envelopes** across the whole cycle (2 × common area / summed areas). It is a whole-cycle overlap index of envelope shape and relative amplitude. It is **not** simultaneous activation time, not muscular force, not phase-specific, and it can be non-zero even where detected intervals of the pair do not overlap, because sub-threshold envelope area contributes. Values depend on this normalization and envelope pipeline and must not be compared with values or thresholds from other studies or algorithms.

- **TA vs GAS: 0.62.** The supplied healthy reference for this pair is median 0.39 (2.5th–97.5th pct 0.24–0.59). This value lies above the upper endpoint of that interval.
- **RF vs BF: 0.68.** The supplied healthy reference for this pair is median 0.68 (0.48–0.83). This value coincides with the supplied median and lies inside the interval.

Lying inside the reference interval (RF–BF) does not establish physiologically normal co-activation or normal muscle function. Lying above it (TA–GAS) does not establish pathological co-activation, spasticity, stiffness, impaired motor control or any diagnosis — particularly since the reference intervals come from a cohort with no speed overlap with this participant.

**Interpretation of the scores.** The Gait Variable Score and the composite GPS-like score are **RMS summary differences** between this participant's sagittal curves and the reference mean curves. They are magnitude summaries only: they do not indicate the direction of the difference (e.g. more flexed vs more extended), do not localize the difference to any part of the cycle, and do not identify which waveform features contribute. The composite is the RMS of the per-joint GVS values over the sagittal channels provided; it is explicitly **not** the validated 9-variable GPS, so published GPS thresholds and severity bands do not transfer to it.

Against the supplied composite reference (median 5.1, 95th percentile 10.7), this participant's composite score of 16.32 is higher than both figures. Among the individual channels, knee (21.84) carries the largest GVS, followed by hip (17.66) and ankle (15.87), with pelvis (5.00) the smallest. These are ordered magnitudes of difference from a non-matched reference; they do not rank clinical importance, do not identify a cause, and cannot separate slow-walking effects from stroke-related changes.

**On ROM.** The ROM figures are descriptive amplitudes of sagittal excursion over the cycle. No matched normative ROM limits are supplied for this participant's speed, age or side, so ROM alone does not establish a clinical restriction or contracture at any joint. Note also that a small GVS and a given ROM are different quantities: the pelvis shows both the smallest excursion (7.9°) and the smallest deviation score (5.00), and neither value certifies the other as normal.

**Overall.** The kinematic summaries indicate that this participant's sagittal waveforms differ substantially in magnitude from a pooled, unmatched healthy reference, with the largest differences at the knee. Because no appropriate speed-matched comparison exists in this dataset, these differences should be reported as differences from that specific reference and not as evidence of a disease effect, a specific impairment, or a clinical severity grade. A speed inside some overall range would not in itself establish a valid matched reference, and 0.21 m/s is in any case outside the reference range entirely.
