## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

**How these numbers must be read.** The curves are **ensemble-averaged envelopes**, normalized with a shared maximum across available strides per participant/side/muscle before averaging; the released mean curves are used without further amplitude normalization. Amplitudes are therefore **relative envelope values**, not neural activation, force or strength, and muscles must not be ranked as stronger or weaker from them. Detected intervals are properties of *this averaged envelope* under the stated rule: **envelope > 20% of that same curve's own peak, minimum retained interval 3% of the cycle**. They are not physiological onsets, offsets, contraction durations, or proof of inactivity outside the intervals, and they do not establish that the same pattern occurred on every individual stride. An interval written with X > Y wraps across the 100%→0% boundary, meaning the mean envelope remained above its own threshold across the cycle-defining initial-contact boundary. Because **no toe-off or matched muscle timing reference is supplied**, no interval is labelled normal, early, delayed or prolonged, and none is mapped to a functional role such as clearance, push-off or weight acceptance.

**Descriptive observations.**
- All seven mean envelopes are above their own 20% threshold across the initial-contact boundary, i.e. the wrapping interval is continuous through the 100%→0% transition of the averaged curve.
- Retained-interval coverage ranges from 35% (BF) to 81% (ERS) of the cycle. Where two retained intervals are separated by a gap (GAS, RF, BF, ST), the mean envelope fell below its own threshold in that gap, since otherwise the intervals would be merged. Where a single wrapping interval is reported (VL, TA, ERS), the remainder of the cycle contains no region meeting the threshold-plus-duration criteria. In no case does this input support a statement that the muscle was inactive or non-functional outside the retained intervals; sub-threshold envelope activity is not excluded and, by definition, is not represented in interval coverage.
- Anatomically these channels correspond to ankle plantar flexion (GAS), ankle dorsiflexion (TA), knee extension (RF, VL), knee flexion / hip extension (BF, ST) and trunk extension (ERS). Naming these actions does not assign any interval to a gait-phase function.
- Peak normalized amplitudes cluster between 0.55 and 0.81 of each curve's own scale. Because each curve carries its own denominator, these values are **not comparable across muscles as magnitudes of effort**, and an average curve says nothing about stride-to-stride variability.

**Max |z| interpretation.** Max |z| is an **extreme-value statistic** — the largest standardized difference from the phase-specific reference mean across all 1001 phase points — not a single-observation z-score, and the conventional 2-SD rule does not apply. In this dataset every one of 109 healthy controls exceeds |z| = 2 on at least one muscle, and the per-muscle healthy 95th percentiles for this statistic run **3.65 (ERS) to 4.61 (TA)**, higher still under leave-one-out. Against that stated range:
- **GAS (15.2), ERS (8.2), VL (6.4), RF (6.2) and TA (5.9)** lie above the upper end of the quoted 95th-percentile range (4.61), i.e. above every per-muscle 95th percentile listed.
- **ST (3.4) and BF (2.9)** lie below the lower end of that range (3.65), i.e. below every per-muscle 95th percentile listed.
- The **location** of each maximum (e.g. GAS at 57%, ERS at 63%, RF and VL at 36%) marks where the standardized difference from the reference was largest — **not** necessarily where the envelope peaked, and not a gait event. "Above reference" means above the **phase-specific reference mean at that point**, not above a healthy percentile limit. Given the absence of a speed-matched reference below 0.80 m/s, these standardized differences cannot be attributed to stroke pathology as distinct from slow-walking effects.

**Co-contraction indices.** CCI here is the Falconer–Winter index (2 × common area / summed areas) computed on the **full continuous normalized envelopes over the whole cycle**. It can be non-zero even when detected bursts do not overlap, because sub-threshold activity contributes. It is **not** a duration of simultaneous activation, **not** muscular force, and **not** a phase-specific result; it depends on the normalization and envelope pipeline used here, so values and thresholds from other studies or algorithms are not transferable.
- **TA vs GAS = 0.40**, essentially at the supplied healthy median of 0.39 and within the 2.5th–97.5th percentile interval 0.24–0.59.
- **RF vs BF = 0.54**, within the supplied interval 0.48–0.83 but **below** the median 0.68 and relatively close to the lower endpoint.
Lying inside these empirical intervals does not establish physiologically normal co-activation or normal muscle function; lying outside them would not establish pathological co-activation, stiffness, impaired motor control, or a diagnosis. The same reference-matching limits described in Section 1 apply to these comparisons.

**What GVS and the composite do and do not convey.** Both are **summary magnitudes of difference** from the stated pooled reference averaged across the cycle. Neither localizes *when* in the cycle the difference occurred, nor in which direction (e.g. more flexion vs more extension), nor which waveform feature drove it. The pooled 138-subject reference is unmatched for age and, decisively, has **no representation at this subject's 0.75 m/s walking speed**. These comparisons therefore describe how this subject's curves differ from an unmatched empirical reference; they cannot isolate a disease effect or support a clinical diagnosis. The same caution would apply to a healthy-group participant compared against the same reference.
