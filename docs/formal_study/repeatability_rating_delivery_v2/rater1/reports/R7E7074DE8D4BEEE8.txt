All seven mean envelopes are reported above threshold across the initial-contact cycle boundary. GAS and BF have retained intervals spanning the full analysis window. These findings do **not** establish physiological onset or offset, continuous contraction, inactivity outside retained intervals, or the same pattern in every stride. Without intermediate events and matched timing references, the intervals cannot be assigned gait-phase functions or characterized as early, delayed, or prolonged.

Normalization uses a participant-, side-, and muscle-specific maximum shared across available strides before averaging, without further amplitude normalization. The reported amplitudes are relative envelope values—not force, strength, or absolute neural activation—and do not support ranking muscle strength.

At each listed maximum-|z| location, the envelope is **above the phase-specific reference mean**. These locations identify the largest standardized differences, not necessarily peak envelope amplitudes or gait events. Maximum |z| is an extreme-value statistic, so a conventional 2-SD rule is inappropriate. ERS 4.8 exceeds its supplied pooled healthy 95th percentile of 3.65. TA 4.6 is close to its supplied 4.61 percentile; displayed precision does not justify classifying the hidden unrounded value. Corresponding muscle-specific percentile values for the other recorded muscles are not supplied.

**Whole-cycle CCI:**
- **TA–GAS: 0.78**, above the supplied empirical interval of 0.24–0.59 and median of 0.39.
- **RF–BF: 0.65**, within the supplied interval of 0.48–0.83 and below its median of 0.68.

CCI measures whole-cycle overlap of continuous normalized envelopes, including subthreshold contributions. It is not simultaneous activation time, muscular force, or a phase-specific result. These comparisons establish neither pathological nor physiologically normal co-activation and depend on the normalization and envelope methods used.
