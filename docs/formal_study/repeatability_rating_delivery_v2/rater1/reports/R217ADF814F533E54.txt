## 1. Objective Findings

**Participant and side.** Sub07_P is recorded in the stroke cohort; the data supplied describe the **paretic** side. The released cycle for this limb runs from one initial contact of that limb (0%) to the next initial contact (100%); these are two successive occurrences of the same event, not a single instant. No intermediate event — including toe-off — is supplied with this summary, so all timing below is expressed purely as a percentage of the analysis cycle, and no percentage is assigned to a stance/swing subdivision or to any named gait phase.

**Spatiotemporal values as supplied.**
- Gait speed **0.36 m/s**, which falls in the "household ambulator (<0.4 m/s)" speed band. This label denotes a speed range only; it carries no information here about walking independence, safety, assistive-device use, or real-world mobility.
- Cadence **75.8 steps/min**.
- Step length symmetry index **+10.3%**, computed with the **successive-footfall** definition (each heel at its own initial contact). By the stated sign convention, a negative value means a shorter paretic step; this positive value therefore indicates the paretic step measured **longer** than the non-paretic step by this index. A second operational definition (both heels at the same instant) exists and yields a different value in some subjects; only the successive-footfall value was supplied, so the magnitude should not be treated as definition-independent.

**Reference-comparability limit (applies to every comparison in this report).** The deviation reference used for this subject is a **pooled cohort of 138 healthy subjects** (1001 phase points, `main_cohort_excel`), pooling both body sides and all ages, and it is **not matched to this subject on age or walking speed**. The healthy self-selected speeds span 0.80–1.79 m/s; at 0.36 m/s this subject is **below that floor**, so **no speed-matched healthy reference exists in this dataset**. Any difference reported below is a difference from that unmatched reference, in which slow-walking effects and stroke-related control changes are inseparably combined. Group membership in the stroke cohort does not by itself explain any specific number here, and the size of a deviation does not establish a disease effect, a severity grade, or a clinical diagnosis.

**Channels supplied and not supplied.** Supplied: sagittal-plane ankle, knee, hip and pelvis angles with per-joint Gait Variable Scores and ROM; averaged EMG envelopes with detected intervals for GAS, RF, VL, BF, ST, TA, ERS; two co-contraction indices. Not supplied in this task: coronal and transverse kinematics; individual toe-off and other gait events; matched muscle-specific normal timing references; stride-level (stride-to-stride) data; raw-signal quality checks; non-paretic-side EMG or kinematics; age, sex, time post-stroke and stroke clinical scores. These items are **not supplied here**; that is distinct from documented absent recordings in the source release, and their absence from this summary should not be reported as absence from the dataset. ## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

**How these numbers must be read.** Each curve is an **ensemble-averaged envelope** across the available strides, normalized to a shared participant-, side- and muscle-specific maximum before averaging, with no further amplitude normalization. Amplitudes are therefore **relative envelope values** — not neural drive, force or strength — and muscles must not be ranked as stronger or weaker from them. Detected intervals are regions where the *averaged* curve exceeds **20% of its own peak** for at least 3% of the cycle. They are properties of that mean curve under that rule: they are not physiological onsets, offsets, contraction durations, or evidence of inactivity outside the intervals, and they cannot be assumed to recur identically in every individual stride. An interval written X%–Y% with X > Y wraps the 100%→0% boundary; a 0% onset is the analysis-window edge.

All seven averaged envelopes are reported as above threshold across the cycle-defining boundary, i.e. continuity of the mean curve at the initial-contact boundary rather than a discrete event at that point. **BF, ST and ERS** are each reported as a single interval spanning the full analysis window at 100% coverage; at the mean-envelope level this is consistent with the curve remaining above its 20%-of-peak threshold throughout, though a rounded coverage figure on its own would not settle sub-percent behaviour. For **GAS (98%), RF (85%), VL (72%) and TA (64%)**, coverage below 100% means part of the averaged curve fell below its own threshold; the supplied summary does not localize every such segment beyond what the interval boundaries imply, and no statement is made here about whether any individual stride behaved that way. Anatomically these are the ankle plantarflexor (GAS), knee extensors (RF, VL), knee flexors/hip extensors (BF, ST), ankle dorsiflexor (TA) and erector spinae (ERS); no interval is mapped here to a functional role such as clearance, push-off or weight acceptance, and no interval is characterized as early, delayed, prolonged or normal, since matched timing references and events are not supplied.

**Standardized differences (max |z|).** Max |z| is an **extreme-value statistic** — the largest standardized difference across all 1001 phase points — and its location marks where the curve differs most from the **phase-specific reference mean**, which is not necessarily the peak of the envelope and is not a gait event. "Above reference" means above that phase-specific mean, not above a healthy percentile limit. The conventional 2-SD rule does not apply: in this dataset every one of 109 healthy controls exceeds |z| = 2 on at least one muscle, and per-muscle healthy 95th percentiles for this statistic run from **3.65 (ERS)** to **4.61 (TA)**, higher under leave-one-out.
- **TA 3.3** sits below its supplied 95th percentile of 4.61.
- **ERS 5.1** exceeds its supplied 95th percentile of 3.65.
- **GAS 8.6** and **ST 11.0** exceed even the largest of the listed per-muscle percentiles (4.61), so they are large on any of the supplied yardsticks.
- **RF 3.4, VL 3.8 and BF 4.3** fall within the 3.65–4.61 span of the listed endpoints, but their own per-muscle percentiles were not supplied, so they cannot be classified in either direction.
Exceeding a percentile here indicates an unusually large standardized difference from an unmatched, non-speed-matched reference; it does not by itself establish abnormal physiology, pathology or a clinical finding.

**Co-contraction indices.** CCI is the Falconer–Winter index computed over the **full continuous envelopes** across the whole cycle (2 × common area / summed areas). It is a whole-cycle overlap index of normalized envelopes — not simultaneous activation time, not muscular force, not a phase-specific result — and it can be non-zero even where detected bursts do not overlap, because sub-threshold activity contributes. It depends on the normalization and envelope pipeline used here, so values and thresholds from other studies or algorithms are not transferable.
- **TA vs GAS = 0.67**, above the supplied healthy 97.5th-percentile endpoint of 0.59 (median 0.39, interval 0.24–0.59).
- **RF vs BF = 0.75**, inside the supplied healthy interval 0.48–0.83, above the median of 0.68 and closer to the upper endpoint than to the lower.
Lying inside that interval does not establish physiologically normal co-activation or normal muscle function; lying outside it does not establish pathological co-activation, spasticity, stiffness, impaired motor control or any diagnosis — and the same non-matched-reference and slow-speed caveats apply to both indices.

**Not derivable from these data.** Stride-to-stride variability (averaged curves cannot show it), absolute activation or force, non-paretic-side comparison, and any timing statement anchored to toe-off or to a conventional 60/40 stance–swing split.

**Composite (GPS-like) score = 6.29.** This is the RMS of the per-joint GVS values over the supplied sagittal channels — it is a "GPS-like" composite, not the validated 9-variable GPS. Against the supplied composite reference (median 5.1, 95th percentile 10.7) it is **above the median and below the 95th percentile**.

**Interpretive limits.** GVS and the composite are **summary magnitudes of difference** from the stated pooled 138-subject reference waveforms. They do not indicate the direction of the waveform difference (e.g. more flexed vs more extended), do not localize it to any part of the cycle, and do not identify a mechanism. Within this set, the **ankle** carries the largest per-joint difference (9.23) and the **pelvis** the smallest (1.77), with knee and hip intermediate; that ordering describes the summary statistics only.

**ROM.** The ROM figures are descriptive. No healthy ROM reference values were supplied for any joint, so ROM alone — including the small pelvic sagittal excursion of 1.8° — does not establish a restriction, contracture or clinical limitation in the absence of an appropriate matched comparison. Because the reference cohort's speeds start at 0.80 m/s and this subject walked at 0.36 m/s, every kinematic comparison above conflates slow-walking effects with stroke-related changes, and these data cannot separate them or attribute the differences wholly to stroke pathology. Pooled calibration percentiles such as the composite 95th percentile are **not** matched leave-one-out normal limits for this individual.

## 4. Questions for Further Clinical Assessment

These are open questions raised by the limits of the supplied summary. None presupposes that the mechanism or measurement issue named has already been observed, and none implies a management plan.

1. **Gait events.** Are individual toe-off and other event markers available in the source release for this trial? If so, can the envelope intervals and max-|z| locations be re-expressed against this subject's own events rather than as raw cycle percentages?
2. **Speed-appropriate comparison.** Is any slow-speed healthy or matched reference dataset accessible, or could a speed-controlled comparison condition be recorded, so that deviations at 0.36 m/s can be examined separately from slow-walking effects?
3. **Per-muscle percentile values.** Can the per-muscle healthy 95th percentiles (and leave-one-out values) for RF, VL, BF, GAS and ST be retrieved, so that the max-|z| results currently uninterpretable against a specific limit (RF 3.4, VL 3.8, BF 4.3) can be placed?
4. **Signal-quality review.** Has a raw-signal and baseline review been performed for the channels with near-total coverage (BF, ST, ERS at 100%; GAS at 98%)? Specifically, could baseline offset, motion artefact, crosstalk, or a low peak relative to noise floor influence the 20%-of-own-peak threshold, and is that question answerable from the stored raw data?
5. **Stride-level data.** Are individual stride envelopes retained? Consistency of the reported coverage and interval pattern across strides cannot be examined from an ensemble average.
6. **Contralateral data.** Are non-paretic-side EMG and kinematics available for this participant, given that the EMG normalization denominator is side-specific and cross-side amplitude comparison is therefore not valid without explicit handling?
7. **Step-length definition.** Does the instantaneous (same-instant) step-length definition yield a different symmetry value for this subject, and which definition is intended for any downstream comparison?
8. **Clinical and demographic context.** Are age, sex, time post-stroke, stroke clinical scores, assistive-device and orthosis use, and observed walking independence documented in the release for this participant? None of these were supplied here, and the speed-band label does not substitute for them.
9. **Out-of-plane motion.** Is a coronal/transverse dataset available, since the present kinematic picture is sagittal-only?
10. **Correspondence with clinical examination.** Do the examination findings correspond with, or diverge from, the summary differences reported here — recognizing that none of these metrics can establish a mechanism on its own?
