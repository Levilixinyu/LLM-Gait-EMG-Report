RF and ERS have reported full-window retained intervals. RF, VL, BF, ST, TA and ERS are explicitly reported as remaining above threshold across the cycle-defining initial-contact boundary **at the mean-envelope level**. These results do not establish physiological onset or offset, continuous contraction, inactivity outside retained intervals, or the same pattern in every stride. Without intermediate events and matched timing references, the intervals cannot be assigned gait-phase functions or characterized as early, delayed or prolonged.

The supplied normalization shares a maximum across available strides before averaging; the described stroke denominator is participant-, side- and muscle-specific. The released means undergo no further amplitude normalization. Thus, these amplitudes do not measure strength, force or absolute neural activation and cannot rank muscles by strength.

The maximum absolute z locations identify the **largest standardized differences from the phase-specific reference mean**, not necessarily peak envelope amplitude or a gait event. “Above” and “below” describe direction relative to that mean, not a percentile boundary. These are extreme-value statistics, so the conventional 2-SD rule is inappropriate. For the explicitly supplied muscle-specific pooled calibration values, TA **3.5** is below its healthy 95th percentile of **4.61**, whereas ERS **4.8** is above **3.65**; these comparisons are not diagnostic classifications.

**Whole-cycle CCI:**
- **TA–GAS: 0.62**, above the supplied empirical interval **0.24–0.59** and median **0.39**.
- **RF–BF: 0.78**, within **0.48–0.83** and above the median **0.68**.

CCI measures whole-cycle overlap of the continuous normalized envelopes, including subthreshold contributions. It is not simultaneous activation time, force or a phase-specific result. Neither comparison establishes normal muscle function, pathological co-activation, stiffness or impaired control; values depend on the normalization and envelope methods used.
