### 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings
EMG threshold metrics denote regions where the participant’s averaged and normalized envelope exceeded 20% of its own peak for at least 3% of the cycle. These metrics are properties of the averaged envelope and do not establish physiological onset, offset, absolute neural activation, muscular force, or stride-to-stride continuous activity. 

All seven evaluated muscles demonstrated above-threshold activity across the cycle boundary (initial contact at 0%/100%).

- **GAS:** Envelope coverage is 70% of the cycle, with detected intervals at 73%–82% and 97%–57% (wrapping the cycle boundary). Peak normalized amplitude is 0.55. The maximum |z| deviation from the healthy reference is 8.1 (above the phase-specific reference mean, located at 2% of the cycle).
- **RF:** Envelope coverage is 97% of the cycle, with a detected interval at 97%–91% (wrapping the cycle boundary). Peak normalized amplitude is 0.42. The maximum |z| deviation is 2.6 (above reference, at 29% of the cycle).

**Co-Contraction Indices (CCI):**
CCI represents a whole-cycle overlap index of the normalized EMG envelopes. Proximity to or deviation from reference intervals does not by itself establish physiologically normal function or pathological stiffness.
- **TA vs. GAS:** CCI is 0.72. This exceeds the empirical healthy-cohort median of 0.39 (2.5th–97.5th percentile interval: 0.24–0.59).
- **RF vs. BF:** CCI is 0.83. This value is higher than the empirical healthy-cohort median of 0.68 and rests at the upper boundary of the reference interval (0.48–0.83).

### 3. Kinematic and Reference Comparisons
The kinematic deviations below are compared against a pooled reference of 138 healthy participants (unmatched for age). The self-selected speeds of this healthy cohort range from 0.80 to 1.79 m/s. Because this participant’s gait speed (0.26 m/s) is well below this floor, no speed-matched reference exists in the dataset. Consequently, kinematic deviations conflate the effects of very slow walking with stroke-specific impairments, and the two cannot be distinguished here. A limited range of motion does not independently establish a clinical joint restriction.

### 4. Questions for Further Clinical Assessment
- Are there structural restrictions, underlying weakness, or compensatory gait strategies contributing to the extremely limited sagittal range of motion observed at the knee and hip?
- Does clinical examination reveal spasticity, impaired selective motor control, or heightened postural demands that might explain the near-continuous (95%-100%) above-threshold envelope coverage in the RF, TA, ST, BF, and ERS muscles?
- To what degree do the elevated whole-cycle co-contraction indices (particularly for the TA/GAS pair) reflect functional joint stiffening to compensate for instability versus an underlying loss of independent agonist/antagonist control?
- Is the step-length asymmetry (paretic step longer than the non-paretic step) a result of difficulty advancing the non-paretic limb, or is it due to reduced single-limb stance time and weight-bearing capability on the paretic limb?
