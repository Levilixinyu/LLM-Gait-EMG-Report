The wrapped intervals establish above-threshold continuity across the initial-contact cycle boundary **at the mean-envelope level** for RF, VL, BF, ST, TA, and ERS. They do not establish the same pattern in every stride, continuous physiological contraction, or inactivity outside retained intervals. Matched muscle-specific timing references are unavailable, so timing cannot be classified as normal, early, delayed, or prolonged.

Maximum |z| identifies the largest standardized reference difference across 1001 cycle points, **not necessarily peak envelope amplitude**. “Above” and “below” refer to the phase-specific reference mean. ERS has the largest supplied value, **6.8**, exceeding the supplied pooled ERS 95th-percentile calibration value of **3.65**. TA’s **2.1** is below its supplied calibration value of **4.61**. These extreme-value statistics should not be classified using a conventional |z| > 2 rule.

**Whole-cycle envelope overlap:**

- **TA–GAS CCI: 0.38**, compared with median **0.39** and empirical interval **0.24–0.59**.
- **RF–BF CCI: 0.71**, compared with median **0.68** and empirical interval **0.48–0.83**.

Both CCIs lie within the supplied intervals. CCI measures whole-cycle overlap of normalized continuous envelopes, including subthreshold contributions; it is not simultaneous activation time, force, or a phase-specific result. These values do not establish normal muscle function or pathological co-activation and depend on the normalization and envelope methods used.
