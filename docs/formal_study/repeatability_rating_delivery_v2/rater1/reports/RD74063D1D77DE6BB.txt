## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

These findings describe **ensemble-averaged envelopes**, using a threshold of >20% of each mean curve’s own peak and a minimum retained interval of 3% of the cycle by sample count. They are not physiological onset/offset measurements and do not establish the pattern in every stride or inactivity outside retained intervals.

Normalization uses a participant-, side-, and muscle-specific maximum shared across available strides before averaging, with no further amplitude normalization in this analysis. Peak values therefore represent relative envelope amplitude—not strength, force, or absolute neural activation.

The maximum \|z\| locations mark the **largest standardized differences from the phase-specific reference mean**, not necessarily envelope peaks or gait events. “Above reference” refers to that mean, not to a percentile limit. These extreme-value statistics should not be interpreted using a conventional 2-SD cutoff.

**Whole-cycle CCI:**
- **TA–GAS: 0.59**, above the reference median of 0.39 and equal to the displayed upper endpoint of the empirical 0.24–0.59 interval. Rounding does not establish the position of the hidden unrounded value relative to that endpoint.

CCI quantifies whole-cycle overlap of normalized continuous envelopes, including sub-threshold contributions. It is not simultaneous activation time, muscular force, or a phase-specific result. Its interpretation depends on normalization and envelope processing; these comparisons do not establish normal or pathological muscle function.
