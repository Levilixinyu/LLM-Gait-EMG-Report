**Spatiotemporal values (as supplied).**

- Not supplied in this input: individual toe-off and any other intra-cycle event; matched muscle-specific normal timing references; stride-to-stride variability; raw-signal quality checks; expert visual plots; coronal- and transverse-plane kinematics (the pipeline is sagittal-only by definition); non-paretic-side EMG and kinematic curves; age, sex, time post-stroke and stroke clinical scores. ## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

**How to read these numbers.** Each curve is an **ensemble-averaged envelope** built with a shared per-participant/side/muscle maximum across available strides (the denominator is not shared between sides), used without further amplitude normalization. Amplitudes are therefore **relative envelope values** — not neural drive, force, or strength — and muscles must not be ranked as stronger or weaker from them. An average curve cannot show stride-to-stride variability. "Above-threshold interval" means the **averaged envelope exceeded 20% of its own peak for at least 3% of the cycle**; it is a property of that averaged curve under that rule, not a physiological onset, offset, contraction duration, or proof of inactivity elsewhere. Intervals written X%–Y% with X > Y wrap the 100%→0% boundary, i.e. the mean envelope remained above threshold across the cycle-defining initial-contact boundary.

**Coverage and continuity (mean-envelope level).** All seven averaged envelopes remained above their own 20% threshold across the cycle boundary. **RF and ST are reported at 100% coverage**, so for these two curves the supplied evidence does establish that no segment of the averaged envelope fell below threshold. For the remaining muscles, coverage is high but below 100% (BF 98%, GAS 93%, VL 81%, TA 65%), meaning the complement of the listed intervals did not meet the threshold-plus-duration criteria in the **averaged** curve. GAS is fragmented into several retained intervals separated by short non-retained gaps (around 31–33, 36–38, 44–45, 72–74, 85–86 and 96–98). TA shows two retained regions with a non-retained block between roughly 42% and 72%. None of this can be projected onto any individual stride, and none of these percentages can be assigned to stance, swing, loading response, mid-stance or pre-swing, because no intermediate event reference is supplied; a conventional 60% split is not applied. Anatomically, GAS and TA act at the ankle, RF/VL as knee extensors (RF also crossing the hip), BF/ST as hamstrings, and ERS as trunk extensor musculature — but no interval here can be mapped to a functional role such as clearance, push-off or weight acceptance, and no timing can be called normal, early, delayed or prolonged without matched timing references.

**Max |z| interpretation.** Max |z| is an **extreme-value statistic** — the largest standardized difference across all 1001 phase points against the phase-specific reference mean/SD — so the conventional 2-SD rule does not apply (every one of 109 healthy controls exceeds |z| = 2 on at least one muscle). The dataset's per-muscle healthy 95th percentiles for this statistic run **3.65 (ERS) to 4.61 (TA)**, higher still under leave-one-out. Against that:
- **GAS 16.8** is far above the entire 3.65–4.61 bracket and is by a wide margin the largest standardized difference in this subject's set; **VL 6.5**, **ST 6.1** and **RF 5.1** also exceed the upper end of that bracket.
- **ERS 4.2** exceeds its own stated 95th percentile of 3.65; **TA 4.2** lies below its own stated 4.61.
- **BF 2.4** is below the whole bracket.
"Above reference" in each row means the curve sat above the **phase-specific reference mean at that location** — not above a healthy percentile limit. The stated location (e.g. 57% for GAS) is where the standardized difference was largest; it is **not** necessarily the time of peak envelope amplitude and does not identify a gait event. These pooled percentiles are calibration values, not matched leave-one-out normal limits for this subject, and with no speed-matched reference available they cannot establish pathological muscle behaviour.

**Co-contraction indices.** CCI here is the Falconer–Winter index (2 × common area / summed areas) computed on the **full continuous normalized envelopes over the whole cycle**. It is not simultaneous-activation time, not muscular force, not phase-specific, and it can be non-zero even when detected bursts do not overlap, because sub-threshold activity contributes.
- **TA vs GAS = 0.60**, marginally above the upper endpoint of the healthy empirical interval 0.24–0.59 (median 0.39).
- **RF vs BF = 0.74**, inside the healthy interval 0.48–0.83 (median 0.68), toward the upper half.

Lying inside that interval does not establish physiologically normal co-activation or normal muscle function; lying just outside it does not establish pathological co-activation, spasticity, stiffness, impaired motor control, or any diagnosis — particularly given the rounded value's proximity to the endpoint and the absence of a speed-matched reference. CCI values depend on the normalization and envelope pipeline used here and must not be compared with values or thresholds from other studies or algorithms.

**Composite (GPS-like) = 13.39** — the RMS of the four sagittal Gait Variable Scores above. This is *not* the validated 9-variable GPS, so it should not be read against published GPS norms. Against this dataset's own composite reference (median 5.1, 95th percentile 10.7), 13.39 sits above the 95th-percentile calibration value.

**What these scores do and do not say.** GVS and the GPS-like composite are **whole-cycle summary magnitudes of difference from the stated pooled reference**. They do not indicate the direction of the waveform difference (e.g. more flexion vs more extension), do not localize the difference to any part of the cycle, and do not identify which sub-portion of the curve drives the score. The relative ordering — knee carrying the largest per-joint deviation (19.10), ankle and hip intermediate (12.53, 12.02), pelvis smallest (7.14) — describes magnitude of difference from that reference only.

ROM figures are descriptive. An ROM value alone does not establish a clinical restriction or a contracture without an appropriate matched comparison, and no matched ROM reference is supplied here.

**Comparability.** The reference is a pooled 138-subject healthy sample, not matched to this participant, and — as noted in Section 1 — this subject's 0.34 m/s speed lies below the reference cohort's speed floor, so no speed-matched comparison exists. The elevated composite and joint scores therefore quantify **how far these curves sit from an unmatched empirical reference**, with slow-walking effects and stroke-related changes inseparably combined. They do not by themselves establish a disease effect, a clinical impairment, a severity grade, or statistical significance, and the pooled percentiles must not be treated as matched leave-one-out normal limits. These same limits would apply equally to a control participant compared against this reference.
