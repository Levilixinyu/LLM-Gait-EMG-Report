## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

**How these numbers must be read.** The curves are **ensemble-averaged envelopes**. Normalization used a shared maximum across available strides before averaging, participant-, side- and muscle-specific in the checked stroke release; the released mean curves were used without further amplitude normalization. Consequently: amplitudes are **relative envelope values**, not neural drive, force or strength; muscles cannot be ranked as stronger or weaker from them; and the averaged curve cannot show stride-to-stride variability. Detected intervals are regions where the **mean envelope exceeds 20% of that same curve's own peak** for at least 3% of the cycle. They are properties of that averaged envelope under that rule — not physiological onset, offset, contraction duration, continuous contraction, or proven inactivity outside the interval. An interval written with X > Y wraps the 100%→0% boundary, i.e. the mean envelope remains above threshold across the cycle-defining initial-contact boundary. I do not assign any percentage to stance, swing, loading response, pre-swing or push-off, and I do not label any timing as normal, early, delayed or prolonged, because the required matched timing references and individual events are not supplied.

- "Above/below reference" means relative to the **phase-specific reference mean at that location**, not relative to a healthy percentile limit. The location of max |z| (e.g. 70% for GAS, 91% for ERS, 80% for TA) is where the standardized difference is largest — not necessarily where the envelope peaks, and not a gait event.

**Co-contraction indices.** CCI here is the Falconer–Winter index over the **full continuous normalized envelopes across the whole cycle** (2·common area / summed areas). These values depend on the normalization and envelope pipeline used here and must not be compared with CCI values or thresholds from other studies or algorithms.

## 4. Questions for Further Clinical Assessment

These are open questions for the treating clinician to consider; none presupposes that the mechanism or measurement issue named has already been observed in this dataset.

1. **Event timing.** Are this participant's individual gait events (particularly paretic toe-off) available in the release? If so, re-expressing the intervals in the table against his own events — rather than as bare cycle percentages — would be the prerequisite for any phase-referenced interpretation.
2. **Matched reference.** Is a speed-matched and age-matched healthy subset (or a leave-one-out comparison) constructible from the release? That would clarify how much of the composite score of 11.33 and of the GAS/ERS standardized differences persists under a better-matched comparison.
3. **Trunk channel.** ERS shows both the highest coverage (90%) and a large standardized difference (5.5, exceeding its stated 3.65 percentile). Is there a documented recording, electrode-placement or cross-talk consideration for the erector spinae channel in this session, and is trunk motion (outside this sagittal lower-limb set) documented clinically?
4. **Contralateral data.** Are non-paretic-side EMG and kinematic curves available? Note that EMG normalization denominators are side-specific in this release, so between-side amplitude comparison would not be valid without addressing that; timing and coverage comparisons would be interpreted separately.
5. **Step-length definition.** Does the release also provide the instantaneous-heel definition of step length for this subject? Confirming whether the −6.6% asymmetry is stable across both operational definitions would indicate how robust that value is.
6. **Stride-level behaviour.** Are per-stride envelopes or stride counts retrievable? The averaged curves used here cannot speak to consistency across strides, which is a separate question from the mean-envelope pattern described in Section 2.
7. **Clinical context.** Are time post-stroke, clinical scores, and observational gait findings available to place these summary metrics alongside examination data? No such fields were supplied with this summary.
