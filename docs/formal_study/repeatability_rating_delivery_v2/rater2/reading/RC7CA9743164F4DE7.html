## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

**How these numbers must be read.** The curves are **ensemble-averaged envelopes**, normalized using a shared maximum across available strides before averaging (in the checked stroke release the denominator is participant-, side-, and muscle-specific, and is not shared between sides). Amplitudes are therefore **relative envelope values** — not neural activation, force, or strength — and muscles must not be ranked as stronger or weaker from them. Detected intervals are regions where the **averaged** envelope exceeds **20% of that same curve's own peak**, with a minimum retained length of 3% of the cycle. They are properties of the mean curve under this rule, **not physiological onsets, offsets, contraction durations, or evidence of inactivity outside the intervals**, and they should not be assumed to recur identically in every stride. An interval written with X > Y wraps the 100%→0% boundary, meaning the mean envelope remained above threshold across the cycle-defining initial-contact boundary. Gaps between retained intervals may reflect sub-threshold envelope values **or** above-threshold excursions shorter than the 3% retention rule; the supplied evidence does not distinguish these.

**Boundary continuity.** RF, VL, BF, ST and TA each show one retained interval spanning the initial-contact boundary — at the mean-envelope level the envelope did not drop below its 20% threshold across that boundary. GAS and ERS have no boundary-spanning interval; GAS's earliest interval begins at 1%, numerically close to the initial-contact boundary. The GAS onset at 1% and all other interval edges are threshold crossings of an averaged curve, not physiological events.

**Coverage.** Reported above-threshold coverage ranges from 23% (ERS) to 79% (ST). Coverage is the proportion of the averaged cycle above each curve's own relative threshold; because the threshold is self-referenced to each muscle's own peak, coverage values are **not comparable as amplitude or effort across muscles**. No statement is made here that any muscle had "no below-threshold segment," as the supplied summary does not establish that for any channel.

**Standardized differences (max |z|).** Max |z| is an **extreme-value statistic** over 1001 phase points, not a single-observation z-score; in this dataset every one of 109 healthy controls exceeds |z| = 2 on at least one muscle, so the conventional 2-SD rule does not apply. Per-muscle healthy 95th percentiles for this statistic run from **3.65 (ERS) to 4.61 (TA)**, and are higher under leave-one-out. Against the two muscle-specific limits that are supplied: **ERS 2.8 is below its 3.65 limit**, and **TA 2.2 is below its 4.61 limit**. For GAS (3.4), RF (3.1), VL (1.5), BF (2.8) and ST (4.6), the muscle-specific 95th percentile is **not supplied**; all fall within or near the 3.65–4.61 span of per-muscle limits, with ST's 4.6 the largest standardized difference in this subject and the one closest to the upper end of that span. No value here can be labelled abnormal, and none establishes pathology. The location of each max |z| marks the phase point of the **largest standardized difference from the phase-specific reference mean** — it is not necessarily the time of peak envelope amplitude, and it is not a gait event. "Above/below reference" means relative to that phase-specific reference mean, not relative to a healthy percentile limit.

**Co-contraction indices.** CCI is the Falconer–Winter index (2 × common area / summed areas) computed on the **full continuous envelopes over the whole cycle**. It can be non-zero even when detected bursts of the pair do not overlap, because sub-threshold envelope area contributes. It is **not** simultaneous activation time, muscular force, or a phase-specific result, and it depends on the normalization and envelope pipeline used here — values and thresholds from other studies or algorithms are not transferable.
- **TA vs GAS = 0.38**, essentially at the healthy-cohort median of 0.39 and within the 2.5th–97.5th percentile interval 0.24–0.59.
- **RF vs BF = 0.73**, above the healthy median of 0.68 and within the interval 0.48–0.83, in the upper part of that interval but not at its endpoint.

Falling inside these empirical intervals does **not** establish physiologically normal co-activation or normal muscle function, just as a value outside them would not establish pathological co-activation, stiffness, impaired motor control, or a diagnosis.
