## 1. Objective Findings

**Participant and side.** Sub16_P is recorded in the **stroke** group, and the analyzed limb is the **paretic** side. The released cycle for this limb runs from one initial contact of that limb (0%) to the next initial contact (100%); these are two successive occurrences of the same event, not one instant. **No intermediate gait event (including individual toe-off) is supplied**, so all timing below is expressed as a percentage of the analysis cycle only. Percentages are not translated into stance/swing or any named sub-phase, and no conventional 60% split is applied.

**Spatiotemporal values as supplied.**
- Gait speed **1.36 m/s**, labelled "full community ambulator (>0.8 m/s)". This label denotes a **speed band only** — it does not measure walking independence, safety, or real-world community mobility.
- Cadence **116.2 steps/min**.
- Step length symmetry index **−5.1%** (definition: **successive_footfall**, i.e. each heel's position at its own initial contact). The negative sign indicates the **paretic step is the shorter** of the two by this index. An alternative instantaneous-heel-position definition exists and can yield a different value for some subjects; only the successive-footfall value is supplied here.

**Reference used.** Kinematic deviation is computed against **138 pooled healthy subjects** (1001 phase points, source: main_cohort_excel). This reference pools both body sides and all ages and is **not age- or speed-matched** to this participant. This subject's speed (1.36 m/s) falls inside the healthy cohort's overall self-selected speed span (0.80–1.79 m/s), which removes the "no speed-matched reference exists" problem that applies to most stroke subjects in this dataset — but **a speed inside an overall range does not establish that a properly matched reference subgroup exists**. Any difference from this reference is therefore a difference from a pooled empirical comparison group; it cannot by itself establish a disease effect, a stroke-specific control change, or a clinical diagnosis. The same limitation would apply to a control participant.

**Available vs. not supplied.** All seven EMG channels (GAS, RF, VL, BF, ST, TA, ERS) and four sagittal kinematic channels (ankle, knee, hip, pelvis) are present. **Only sagittal-plane** angles are included; coronal and transverse data are not part of this input. Not supplied in this task: individual toe-off and any matched muscle-specific timing reference, stride-level (stride-to-stride) data, raw-signal quality checks, expert plots, non-paretic-side data, age, sex, time post-stroke, and stroke clinical scores. These are "not supplied to this interpretation" — not documented as absent from the source release.

## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

**How these numbers must be read.** The curves are **ensemble-averaged envelopes**, normalized using a shared maximum across available strides before averaging (in the checked stroke release the denominator is participant-, side-, and muscle-specific, and is not shared between sides). Amplitudes are therefore **relative envelope values** — not neural activation, force, or strength — and muscles must not be ranked as stronger or weaker from them. Detected intervals are regions where the **averaged** envelope exceeds **20% of that same curve's own peak**, with a minimum retained length of 3% of the cycle. They are properties of the mean curve under this rule, **not physiological onsets, offsets, contraction durations, or evidence of inactivity outside the intervals**, and they should not be assumed to recur identically in every stride. An interval written with X > Y wraps the 100%→0% boundary, meaning the mean envelope remained above threshold across the cycle-defining initial-contact boundary. Gaps between retained intervals may reflect sub-threshold envelope values **or** above-threshold excursions shorter than the 3% retention rule; the supplied evidence does not distinguish these.

**Boundary continuity.** RF, VL, BF, ST and TA each show one retained interval spanning the initial-contact boundary — at the mean-envelope level the envelope did not drop below its 20% threshold across that boundary. GAS and ERS have no boundary-spanning interval; GAS's earliest interval begins at 1%, numerically close to the initial-contact boundary. The GAS onset at 1% and all other interval edges are threshold crossings of an averaged curve, not physiological events.

**Coverage.** Reported above-threshold coverage ranges from 23% (ERS) to 79% (ST). Coverage is the proportion of the averaged cycle above each curve's own relative threshold; because the threshold is self-referenced to each muscle's own peak, coverage values are **not comparable as amplitude or effort across muscles**. No statement is made here that any muscle had "no below-threshold segment," as the supplied summary does not establish that for any channel.

**Standardized differences (max |z|).** Max |z| is an **extreme-value statistic** over 1001 phase points, not a single-observation z-score; in this dataset every one of 109 healthy controls exceeds |z| = 2 on at least one muscle, so the conventional 2-SD rule does not apply. Per-muscle healthy 95th percentiles for this statistic run from **3.65 (ERS) to 4.61 (TA)**, and are higher under leave-one-out. Against the two muscle-specific limits that are supplied: **ERS 2.8 is below its 3.65 limit**, and **TA 2.2 is below its 4.61 limit**. For GAS (3.4), RF (3.1), VL (1.5), BF (2.8) and ST (4.6), the muscle-specific 95th percentile is **not supplied**; all fall within or near the 3.65–4.61 span of per-muscle limits, with ST's 4.6 the largest standardized difference in this subject and the one closest to the upper end of that span. No value here can be labelled abnormal, and none establishes pathology. The location of each max |z| marks the phase point of the **largest standardized difference from the phase-specific reference mean** — it is not necessarily the time of peak envelope amplitude, and it is not a gait event. "Above/below reference" means relative to that phase-specific reference mean, not relative to a healthy percentile limit.

**Co-contraction indices.** CCI is the Falconer–Winter index (2 × common area / summed areas) computed on the **full continuous envelopes over the whole cycle**. It can be non-zero even when detected bursts of the pair do not overlap, because sub-threshold envelope area contributes. It is **not** simultaneous activation time, muscular force, or a phase-specific result, and it depends on the normalization and envelope pipeline used here — values and thresholds from other studies or algorithms are not transferable.
- **TA vs GAS = 0.38**, essentially at the healthy-cohort median of 0.39 and within the 2.5th–97.5th percentile interval 0.24–0.59.
- **RF vs BF = 0.73**, above the healthy median of 0.68 and within the interval 0.48–0.83, in the upper part of that interval but not at its endpoint.

Falling inside these empirical intervals does **not** establish physiologically normal co-activation or normal muscle function, just as a value outside them would not establish pathological co-activation, stiffness, impaired motor control, or a diagnosis.

**Composite (GPS-like) = 7.39** — the RMS of the four sagittal GVS values above. This is **not** the validated 9-variable GPS. Relative to the supplied composite reference (median 5.1, 95th percentile 10.7), 7.39 lies **above the median and below the 95th percentile**.

**Interpretation limits.** GVS and the composite are **summary magnitudes of difference** from the stated pooled 138-subject reference at each phase point. They do **not** localize the direction of the difference (e.g., more flexion vs. more extension), do not identify which part of the cycle contributes, and do not indicate a mechanism. Within this subject, the **knee (10.83)** and **pelvis (9.38)** channels carry the largest deviations from the reference and dominate the composite, while **ankle (1.84)** and **hip (3.14)** are the smaller contributors; no per-joint reference distribution is supplied, so these individual GVS values cannot be placed against percentile limits.

**ROM.** No ROM reference values are supplied for any joint. ROM alone therefore does not establish a clinical restriction or excess — including the small sagittal pelvic ROM of 2.1°, which cannot be classified from this input and should not be conflated with the elevated pelvic GVS (a difference-magnitude statistic, not a range measure). The pooled calibration percentiles quoted for the composite are **not** matched leave-one-out normal limits for this participant.

**Overall comparability.** Because the reference is pooled across sides and ages and is not matched to this participant on age or walking speed, all differences described here are differences from that specific empirical reference. They cannot be attributed to stroke pathology, nor separated from speed, age, anthropometric or methodological effects, and they do not establish a clinical diagnosis or severity grade. Group membership in the stroke cohort likewise does not, by itself, explain any particular deviation; and healthy-group membership would not prove normal physiology.

## 4. Questions for Further Clinical Assessment

These are open questions raised by the limits of this dataset. None presupposes that the mechanism or measurement issue in question has been observed, and none implies a treatment.

1. Are individual gait events (toe-off, opposite foot contact) available in the release for this participant, so that the interval boundaries reported here could be placed relative to this limb's own events rather than to cycle percentage alone?
2. Can a matched reference subgroup (age, walking speed, cadence) be assembled from the 138-subject cohort, and would the composite of 7.39 and the per-joint GVS pattern change against such a subgroup rather than the pooled reference?
3. Are stride-level EMG envelopes and stride counts retrievable, so it can be examined whether the mean-curve intervals — particularly the boundary-spanning ones in RF, VL, BF, ST and TA — reflect a consistent pattern across strides or averaging of variable strides?
4. Were raw-signal quality checks (baseline noise, ECG or movement artefact, crosstalk, electrode placement) performed for the ST and ERS channels, given that these carry the highest coverage and one of the largest standardized differences, and could such a check be reviewed?
5. Would applying an alternative envelope threshold or minimum-duration rule alter the retained intervals — in particular, do the short gaps in the GAS (6–9%, 15–21%) and VL (27–28%, 34–36%) traces correspond to sub-threshold segments or to excursions below the 3% retention length?
6. Is the muscle-specific healthy 95th percentile for the max |z| statistic available for GAS, RF, VL, BF and ST (and a leave-one-out version for all seven), so that ST's 4.6 could be placed against its own limit rather than the 3.65–4.61 across-muscle span?
7. Are non-paretic-side kinematic and EMG data available? If so, note that the amplitude denominator is side-specific in this release, so any cross-side comparison would need a stated method for handling that.
8. Does the release contain the second (instantaneous-heel) step-length definition for this participant, and does the −5.1% asymmetry hold under that definition?
9. Are coronal- and transverse-plane kinematics available, given that the knee and pelvis sagittal deviations are the largest here and out-of-plane data are entirely outside this input?
10. Are clinical descriptors that apply to the stroke cohort (time post-stroke, clinical scores, assistive device or orthosis use during capture) documented for this participant, and could they be reviewed alongside these measures?
