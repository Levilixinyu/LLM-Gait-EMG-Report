## 1. Objective Findings

**Participant and analysis frame.** This participant belongs to the stroke cohort, and the released data describe the **paretic** limb. The analysis cycle runs from one initial contact of that limb (0%) to the next initial contact (100%); these are two successive occurrences of the same event, not a single instant. No intermediate gait events (toe-off, phase transitions) are supplied, so all timing below is reported as a percentage of the analysis cycle only, and no percentage is assigned to stance, swing, loading response, pre-swing or any other phase.

**Spatiotemporal values as supplied.**
- Gait speed 0.81 m/s, labelled "full community ambulator (>0.8 m/s)." This label denotes a speed band only; it does not measure walking independence, safety or real-world mobility.
- Cadence 99.6 steps/min.
- Step length symmetry index −13.1% (successive-footfall definition: each heel at its own initial contact). The negative sign indicates the **paretic step is the shorter** of the two. The alternative instantaneous definition can yield a different value in some subjects and was not the one used here.
- Composite kinematic deviation score (GPS-like) 3.92, computed as the RMS of the sagittal Gait Variable Scores supplied. This is not the validated 9-variable GPS.

**Reference used.** Deviations were computed against 138 pooled healthy subjects (1001 phase points, main_cohort_excel), pooling both body sides and all ages. This reference is not age-matched or speed-matched to this participant. The subject's 0.81 m/s falls inside the healthy cohort's overall self-selected speed span (0.80–1.79 m/s), but lying inside an overall range does not establish that an appropriately matched reference subgroup exists; comparability remains limited. Consequently, any difference from the reference described below is a difference from that pooled empirical reference and cannot, on its own, establish a disease effect, a stroke-specific control change, or a clinical diagnosis.

**Channels available and not available.** Sagittal-plane kinematics are supplied for ankle, knee, hip and pelvis; coronal and transverse data are not included in this input. Seven EMG envelope channels are supplied (GAS, RF, VL, BF, ST, TA, ERS). Not supplied in this task: individual toe-off and matched muscle-specific timing references, non-paretic-side values, stride-level variability, raw-signal quality checks, expert plots, and demographic/clinical fields (age, sex, time post-stroke, clinical scores). "Not supplied here" is not the same as "absent from the source release"; these fields may exist in the release but are outside this input and cannot be described or inferred.

## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

**How these numbers must be read.** The curves are ensemble-averaged envelopes. Normalization used a shared maximum across available strides per participant, side and muscle (in the checked stroke release the denominator is participant-, side- and muscle-specific and is not shared between sides); individual strides were not rescaled to their own peaks, and no further amplitude normalization was applied. Amplitudes are therefore **relative envelope values** — not neural drive, force or strength — and muscles must not be ranked as stronger or weaker from them. Detected intervals are regions where the *averaged* envelope exceeds 20% of its own peak for at least 3% of the cycle. They are properties of that averaged curve under that rule, not physiological onsets, offsets, contraction durations or proof of inactivity elsewhere; they also do not imply that every individual stride followed the same pattern, and an average curve cannot show stride-to-stride variability.

| RF | 50–60; 63–76; 78–82; 87–45 (wraps) | 86% | 0.67 | 3.5 above reference, at 28% |

**Boundary continuity.** For RF, VL, BF, ST, TA and ERS the averaged envelope remained above its 20% threshold across the 0%/100% cycle-defining boundary; this is a continuity property of the mean envelope at that boundary and is the only event-related statement the data support. GAS is the one channel whose detected interval (9%–53%) lies wholly within the cycle without wrapping; its onset value is a detection result under the stated rule, not a physiological onset.

**Coverage.** Coverage values range from 30% (VL) to 95% (ERS). High coverage means a large fraction of the averaged curve sat above that curve's own 20% peak-relative threshold; it does not establish continuous contraction. Equally, the complement of coverage is not established as sub-threshold in every stride, and the supplied evidence does not establish that any channel had *no* below-threshold segment. RF shows four retained intervals, of which one wraps the boundary; TA shows two. Muscle actions may be named anatomically (e.g., TA as an ankle dorsiflexor, GAS as a plantarflexor, RF/VL as knee extensors, BF/ST as knee flexors/hip extensors, ERS as an erector spinae channel), but no interval here can be mapped to a functional role such as clearance, push-off or weight acceptance, and no interval can be called normal, early, delayed or prolonged, because matched muscle-specific timing references and individual events are not supplied.

**Max |z| values.** Each max |z| is an extreme-value statistic — the largest standardized difference across all 1001 phase points — not a single-observation z-score, and the conventional 2-SD rule does not apply. In this dataset every one of 109 healthy controls exceeds |z| = 2 on at least one muscle, and per-muscle healthy 95th percentiles for this statistic run 3.65 (ERS) to 4.61 (TA), higher still under leave-one-out. Against that background, the values of 2.0–2.3 for GAS, VL, BF, ST and TA, and 3.5 for RF, are below the lowest per-muscle 95th percentile supplied (3.65) and are not distinguishable from the healthy calibration range on this statistic. **ERS at 6.8 exceeds the ERS healthy 95th percentile of 3.65**; this is the largest standardized difference from the pooled reference in this subject's data, located at 90% of the cycle. That location marks where the standardized difference was greatest — not necessarily where the envelope peaked and not a gait event. "Above reference" and "below reference" here mean relative to the phase-specific reference mean at that location, not relative to a healthy percentile limit. For TA, the largest standardized difference was *below* the reference mean at 72%. None of these values, individually or together, establishes pathology, and the ERS value in particular cannot be separated from the limited comparability of a pooled, unmatched reference (including possible electrode-placement and normalization differences for a trunk channel).

**Co-contraction indices.** CCI is the Falconer–Winter index computed on the full continuous normalized envelopes (2 × common area / summed areas) over the whole cycle. It is not simultaneous activation time, not muscular force, and not phase-specific; it can be non-zero even when detected bursts do not overlap, because sub-threshold envelope area contributes. It also depends on the normalization and envelope pipeline used here, so values and thresholds from other studies or algorithms are not transferable.
- **TA vs GAS = 0.38**, essentially at the healthy median of 0.39 and within the supplied 2.5th–97.5th percentile interval 0.24–0.59.
- **RF vs BF = 0.71**, above the healthy median of 0.68 and within the supplied interval 0.48–0.83.

Falling inside these empirical intervals does not establish physiologically normal co-activation or normal muscle function; falling outside would not establish pathological co-activation, stiffness or impaired motor control.

**Composite (GPS-like) = 3.92**, the RMS of the four GVS values above, compared with the supplied healthy composite reference (median 5.1, 95th percentile 10.7). This subject's composite is **below the healthy median** and well below the 95th percentile — i.e., the overall summary difference from the pooled reference is smaller than that of the typical healthy reference subject on this metric. Within the composite, the ankle (5.39) and knee (5.35) channels contribute the largest per-joint summary differences, while hip (1.70) and pelvis (0.99) contribute least.

**What GVS and the GPS-like score do and do not convey.** Both are summary magnitudes of difference from the stated reference across the cycle. Neither localizes the difference to a phase of the cycle, and neither indicates the direction of waveform deviation (e.g., more flexion vs more extension). They also do not identify which portion of the cycle drove the value.

**ROM.** The ROM figures are descriptive ranges for this subject's sagittal curves. No matched normative ROM limits are supplied in this input, so ROM alone does not establish a restriction, contracture or clinical limitation — that would require an appropriate comparison that is not available here. The small pelvic sagittal ROM (2.5 deg) is reported as a value only.

**Comparability caveat (applies to Sections 2 and 3 alike).** The reference is a pooled, non-age-matched, non-side-specific healthy sample, and the pooled calibration percentiles quoted for the max-|z| statistic are not matched leave-one-out normal limits for this subject. Differences from that reference are descriptive; they conflate any slow- or altered-walking effects, measurement and normalization differences, and stroke-related control changes, and these cannot be separated from these data. No statement here establishes a disease effect or a clinical diagnosis, and the same limits would apply if this participant were a control.

## 4. Questions for Further Clinical Assessment

These are open questions about whether a mechanism or measurement factor exists — none is presupposed to have been observed, and no treatment is suggested.

1. Are the individual gait events (particularly toe-off) and matched, muscle-specific timing references retrievable from the release for this subject? Without them, the detected intervals cannot be related to stance/swing or to any expected timing window.
2. Is a non-paretic-side dataset available for this participant? Since the EMG denominator is side-specific, a between-side envelope-amplitude comparison would not be valid, but side-to-side interval structure, coverage, CCI and kinematics could be compared descriptively.
3. For the ERS channel, which produced the only max |z| above its supplied healthy 95th percentile and the highest coverage (95%): can raw-signal quality, electrode placement, trunk-marker/electrode setup, and the possibility of ECG or movement artefact be reviewed? Raw-signal quality checks were not part of this input.
4. Can stride-level (non-averaged) EMG and kinematic data be examined, to address whether the averaged-envelope pattern — including the boundary-crossing continuity seen in six channels — is representative across strides, which an ensemble average cannot show?
5. Is an age-, side- and speed-matched healthy comparison group (or a leave-one-out reference) obtainable, so that the ankle and knee GVS contributions and the ERS finding can be re-evaluated under a more comparable reference?
6. Are demographic and stroke-specific fields (age, sex, time post-stroke, clinical scores, formal non-paretic designation) available in the release for this subject, to contextualize the observed −13.1% step-length asymmetry and the spatiotemporal profile?
7. Would recomputing step-length symmetry under the instantaneous definition change the value materially for this subject, given that the two operational definitions can diverge?
8. Is there a clinical question for which coronal- and transverse-plane kinematics would be informative, since only sagittal channels were provided here?
