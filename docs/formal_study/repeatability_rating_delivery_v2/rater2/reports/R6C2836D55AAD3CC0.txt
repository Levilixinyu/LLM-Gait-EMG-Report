# Gait Report Interpretation — Sub27_P
**Interpretation version:** limited-evidence-v3.1-candidate
**Scope:** Research-level interpretation of a supplied quantitative summary. Not a diagnosis, not a treatment recommendation.

---

## 1. Objective Findings

**Participant and side.** Sub27_P is recorded in the stroke cohort; the analysed limb is the **paretic** side. The analysis cycle for this limb runs from initial contact of the paretic limb at 0% to the next initial contact of the same limb at 100%. These two endpoints are successive occurrences of the same event, not one instant. **No intermediate gait event is supplied** — individual toe-off is not available in this input, so all timing below is expressed purely as a percentage of the analysis cycle. Percentages are not converted to stance, swing, loading response, mid-stance or pre-swing, and no conventional 60% split is applied.

**Spatiotemporal values as supplied.**
- Gait speed **0.75 m/s**, labelled "limited community ambulator (0.4–0.8 m/s)". This label denotes a speed band only; it is not a measurement of walking independence, safety, or real-world community mobility.
- Cadence **82.5 steps/min**.
- Step length symmetry index **−15.8%** (definition: **successive-footfall**, i.e. each heel's position at its own initial contact). By the stated sign convention, the negative value means the **paretic step is the shorter** of the two. An instantaneous-heel-position definition can yield a different value for some subjects; that alternative value is not supplied here.

**Reference used and its limits.** The kinematic deviation reference for this subject is **138 pooled healthy subjects, 1001 phase points (main_cohort_excel)**. This pooling combines both body sides and all ages and is **not age- or speed-matched** to this participant. Critically, the healthy reference cohort's self-selected speeds span **0.80–1.79 m/s**, and this subject walks at **0.75 m/s — below that floor**, so **no speed-matched healthy reference exists in this dataset for him**. Any difference from the reference therefore conflates slow-walking effects with stroke-related control changes, and these two contributions cannot be separated from the data supplied. Differences from the reference are described as differences from that empirical reference only; they do not by themselves establish a disease effect, a mechanism, or a clinical diagnosis. The percentiles quoted below are pooled calibration percentiles, **not matched leave-one-out normal limits**.

**Channels and fields available / not available.**
- Joint kinematics: **sagittal plane only** (ankle, knee, hip, pelvis). Coronal and transverse-plane data are not included in this analysis.
- EMG: seven averaged envelope channels on the analysed side (GAS, RF, VL, BF, ST, TA, ERS). **Non-paretic-side EMG is not supplied here**, so no side-to-side envelope comparison is possible; note also that the normalization denominator is participant-, side- and muscle-specific and is **not shared between sides** in this release.
- Not supplied in this input: individual toe-off and other gait events, matched muscle-specific normal timing references, stride-level (stride-to-stride) data, raw-signal quality checks, expert plots, age, sex, time post-stroke, and stroke clinical scores. "Not supplied here" is not the same as "absent from the source dataset" — several of these fields may exist in the release but are outside this input. Separately, the pipeline documentation notes that a local array audit found 86 of 700 combinations lacking valid nonzero paired samples; nothing in this input states whether any of this subject's channels were affected.

## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

**How these numbers must be read.** The curves are **ensemble-averaged envelopes**, normalized with a shared maximum across available strides per participant/side/muscle before averaging; the released mean curves are used without further amplitude normalization. Amplitudes are therefore **relative envelope values**, not neural activation, force or strength, and muscles must not be ranked as stronger or weaker from them. Detected intervals are properties of *this averaged envelope* under the stated rule: **envelope > 20% of that same curve's own peak, minimum retained interval 3% of the cycle**. They are not physiological onsets, offsets, contraction durations, or proof of inactivity outside the intervals, and they do not establish that the same pattern occurred on every individual stride. An interval written with X > Y wraps across the 100%→0% boundary, meaning the mean envelope remained above its own threshold across the cycle-defining initial-contact boundary. Because **no toe-off or matched muscle timing reference is supplied**, no interval is labelled normal, early, delayed or prolonged, and none is mapped to a functional role such as clearance, push-off or weight acceptance.

**Descriptive observations.**
- All seven mean envelopes are above their own 20% threshold across the initial-contact boundary, i.e. the wrapping interval is continuous through the 100%→0% transition of the averaged curve.
- Retained-interval coverage ranges from 35% (BF) to 81% (ERS) of the cycle. Where two retained intervals are separated by a gap (GAS, RF, BF, ST), the mean envelope fell below its own threshold in that gap, since otherwise the intervals would be merged. Where a single wrapping interval is reported (VL, TA, ERS), the remainder of the cycle contains no region meeting the threshold-plus-duration criteria. In no case does this input support a statement that the muscle was inactive or non-functional outside the retained intervals; sub-threshold envelope activity is not excluded and, by definition, is not represented in interval coverage.
- Anatomically these channels correspond to ankle plantar flexion (GAS), ankle dorsiflexion (TA), knee extension (RF, VL), knee flexion / hip extension (BF, ST) and trunk extension (ERS). Naming these actions does not assign any interval to a gait-phase function.
- Peak normalized amplitudes cluster between 0.55 and 0.81 of each curve's own scale. Because each curve carries its own denominator, these values are **not comparable across muscles as magnitudes of effort**, and an average curve says nothing about stride-to-stride variability.

**Max |z| interpretation.** Max |z| is an **extreme-value statistic** — the largest standardized difference from the phase-specific reference mean across all 1001 phase points — not a single-observation z-score, and the conventional 2-SD rule does not apply. In this dataset every one of 109 healthy controls exceeds |z| = 2 on at least one muscle, and the per-muscle healthy 95th percentiles for this statistic run **3.65 (ERS) to 4.61 (TA)**, higher still under leave-one-out. Against that stated range:
- **GAS (15.2), ERS (8.2), VL (6.4), RF (6.2) and TA (5.9)** lie above the upper end of the quoted 95th-percentile range (4.61), i.e. above every per-muscle 95th percentile listed.
- **ST (3.4) and BF (2.9)** lie below the lower end of that range (3.65), i.e. below every per-muscle 95th percentile listed.
- The **location** of each maximum (e.g. GAS at 57%, ERS at 63%, RF and VL at 36%) marks where the standardized difference from the reference was largest — **not** necessarily where the envelope peaked, and not a gait event. "Above reference" means above the **phase-specific reference mean at that point**, not above a healthy percentile limit. Given the absence of a speed-matched reference below 0.80 m/s, these standardized differences cannot be attributed to stroke pathology as distinct from slow-walking effects.

**Co-contraction indices.** CCI here is the Falconer–Winter index (2 × common area / summed areas) computed on the **full continuous normalized envelopes over the whole cycle**. It can be non-zero even when detected bursts do not overlap, because sub-threshold activity contributes. It is **not** a duration of simultaneous activation, **not** muscular force, and **not** a phase-specific result; it depends on the normalization and envelope pipeline used here, so values and thresholds from other studies or algorithms are not transferable.
- **TA vs GAS = 0.40**, essentially at the supplied healthy median of 0.39 and within the 2.5th–97.5th percentile interval 0.24–0.59.
- **RF vs BF = 0.54**, within the supplied interval 0.48–0.83 but **below** the median 0.68 and relatively close to the lower endpoint.
Lying inside these empirical intervals does not establish physiologically normal co-activation or normal muscle function; lying outside them would not establish pathological co-activation, stiffness, impaired motor control, or a diagnosis. The same reference-matching limits described in Section 1 apply to these comparisons.

## 3. Kinematic and Reference Comparisons

All values below are **sagittal plane only**; no coronal or transverse channel was analysed, so any deviation confined to those planes would not appear in these scores.

|---|---|---|
| Ankle | 34.8 | 7.82 |
| Knee | 59.2 | 9.04 |
| Hip | 41.6 | 5.03 |
| Pelvis | 7.8 | 4.16 |

**Composite ("GPS-like") = 6.81**, the RMS of the four sagittal GVS values. This is *not* the validated 9-variable GPS. Against the supplied composite reference (median 5.1, 95th percentile 10.7), 6.81 sits **above the median and below the 95th percentile** of the pooled calibration distribution. No per-joint GVS reference values are supplied, so the individual GVS figures can be ranked among themselves (knee highest, then ankle, hip, pelvis) but cannot be placed against a normal limit.

**What GVS and the composite do and do not convey.** Both are **summary magnitudes of difference** from the stated pooled reference averaged across the cycle. Neither localizes *when* in the cycle the difference occurred, nor in which direction (e.g. more flexion vs more extension), nor which waveform feature drove it. ROM values on their own describe the excursion of the averaged sagittal curve; without an appropriate matched comparison they do not establish a clinical restriction or an excessive excursion — and no matched comparison is available here, for the reasons in Section 1. The pooled 138-subject reference is unmatched for age and, decisively, has **no representation at this subject's 0.75 m/s walking speed**. These comparisons therefore describe how this subject's curves differ from an unmatched empirical reference; they cannot isolate a disease effect or support a clinical diagnosis. The same caution would apply to a healthy-group participant compared against the same reference.

## 4. Questions for Further Clinical Assessment

These are open questions raised by the limits of the supplied data. None presupposes that the mechanism or measurement issue in question has been observed, and none implies a treatment direction.

1. Are individual gait events — particularly paretic toe-off and the contralateral events — retrievable for this subject? Without them, no envelope interval can be related to stance/swing subdivisions or evaluated for timing appropriateness.
2. Is a **speed-matched or slow-walking healthy reference** (or a matched leave-one-out run) obtainable? This is the single largest constraint on interpreting the max |z| values, the GVS values and the composite score at 0.75 m/s.
3. Can **stride-level EMG data** be examined, rather than the ensemble average? The current curves cannot show whether the reported boundary-crossing continuity and coverage were consistent across strides or driven by a subset.
4. Are **raw-signal quality records** (electrode placement, crosstalk, movement artefact, baseline, saturation) available for the analysed channels — especially GAS, whose max |z| of 15.2 is far above the other channels — so that a measurement explanation can be distinguished from a physiological one?
5. Is **non-paretic-side EMG** available? Given that the normalization denominator is side-specific in this release, what side-to-side comparison, if any, would be methodologically defensible?
6. Is the **instantaneous-heel** step-length symmetry value available alongside the successive-footfall value of −15.8%, to check whether the asymmetry estimate is definition-dependent for this subject?
7. Would **coronal- and transverse-plane kinematics** add information here, given that the composite score summarizes sagittal channels only?
8. Are **age, sex, time post-stroke and clinical assessment scores** retrievable from the release for this participant, to contextualize the comparisons that were possible?
9. Do the clinical examination findings and observed walking pattern correspond to the sagittal ROM and deviation magnitudes reported, and does the "limited community ambulator" speed band match observed functional walking status — which this speed label does not itself measure?
