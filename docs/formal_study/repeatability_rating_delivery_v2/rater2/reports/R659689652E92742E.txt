## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

These are **ensemble-averaged envelope** findings. Normalization uses a participant-, side-, and muscle-specific maximum shared across available strides before averaging, without further amplitude normalization. Reported amplitudes are relative envelope values—not force, strength, or absolute neural activation.

Retained intervals exceed 20% of each mean curve’s own peak and meet the minimum 3%-of-cycle duration rule by sample count.

All seven mean envelopes are explicitly reported above threshold across the initial-contact cycle boundary. Wrapping intervals indicate continuity across the 100%→0% boundary; ERS has a retained interval spanning the full analysis window. These findings do **not** establish physiological onset or offset, continuous contraction, inactivity outside retained intervals, or the same pattern in every stride. Intermediate gait events are unavailable, so intervals are described only by analysis-cycle percentages.

Maximum |z| identifies the largest standardized difference from the phase-specific reference mean—not necessarily peak envelope amplitude or a gait event. “Above” and “below” refer to that mean at the stated location, not to a percentile limit. These extreme-value statistics should not be classified using a conventional |z| > 2 rule.

The knee has the largest supplied GVS, followed by the ankle, hip, and pelvis. These scores summarize waveform differences from the reference; they do not identify the direction or cycle location of those differences. ROM values alone do not establish clinical restriction.

The **GPS-like score is 6.29**, above the supplied calibration median of 5.1 and below its 95th percentile of 10.7. This is an RMS composite of the supplied sagittal GVS channels, not the validated nine-variable GPS.

The deviation reference actually used was **138 pooled healthy participants**, evaluated at 1001 cycle points, from `main_cohort_excel`. Comparability is limited: the pooled reference is not individually matched, and this participant’s speed of 0.43 m/s is below the healthy speed range of 0.80–1.79 m/s. No speed-matched healthy reference exists within this dataset at that speed. Consequently, the EMG and kinematic comparisons cannot separate slow-walking effects from stroke-related effects or establish a disease effect or clinical diagnosis. Supplied pooled calibration percentiles are not matched leave-one-out normal limits.
