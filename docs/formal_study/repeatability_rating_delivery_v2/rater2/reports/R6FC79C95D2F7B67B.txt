All seven envelopes are explicitly reported as above threshold across the initial-contact cycle boundary. ERS has a reported retained interval spanning the full analysis cycle. These findings describe mean-envelope threshold coverage—not physiological onset, offset, continuous contraction or inactivity outside retained intervals. They do not establish the same pattern in every stride, and no gait-phase function can be assigned from these percentages alone.

Normalization used a shared maximum across available strides for each participant-side-muscle combination before averaging, without further amplitude normalization. These relative amplitudes cannot establish strength, force, absolute neural activation or stride-to-stride variability.

Maximum |z| identifies the largest standardized difference from the phase-specific reference mean, not necessarily the envelope’s amplitude peak or a gait event. It is an extreme-value statistic, so the conventional 2-SD rule is inappropriate. TA’s 3.9 and ERS’s 2.5 are below their supplied pooled healthy 95th-percentile values of 4.61 and 3.65, respectively; exact muscle-specific percentile values for the other muscles are not supplied.
