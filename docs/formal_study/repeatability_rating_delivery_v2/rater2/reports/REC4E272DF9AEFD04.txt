## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

All statements below are properties of the **ensemble-averaged envelope** for each muscle under the stated rule (threshold = 20% of that curve's own peak; minimum retained interval = 3% of the cycle by sample count). They are not physiological onsets, offsets, contraction durations, or evidence of inactivity outside the intervals, and they do not establish that any individual stride followed the same pattern. Amplitudes are relative envelope values under a shared-maximum-across-strides normalization; they are not neural drive, force, or strength, and muscles must not be ranked as stronger or weaker from them.

| Muscle | Retained interval(s) (% cycle) | Reported coverage | Peak norm. amplitude | Max \|z\| (location) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| GAS | 3%–2% (wraps boundary) | 99% | 0.37 | 12.1 above reference, at 77% |
| RF | 97%–77% (wraps) | 86% | 0.65 | 6.9 above reference, at 38% |
| VL | 98%–76% (wraps) | 77% | 0.53 | 5.8 above reference, at 52% |
| BF | 80%–84%; 90%–65% (wraps) | 81% | 0.48 | 3.8 above reference, at 47% |
| ST | 76%–75% (wraps) | 99% | 0.37 | 4.9 above reference, at 42% |
| TA | 84%–95%; 95%–70% (wraps) | 89% | 0.60 | 9.9 above reference, at 33% |
| ERS | 0%–100% | 100% | 0.46 | 4.5 above reference, at 28% |

**Coverage and boundary continuity.** Every recorded channel has at least one retained interval that remains above its own 20% threshold across the cycle-defining initial-contact boundary, and reported coverage is high in all seven channels (77%–100% of the cycle). For ERS, the single retained interval spans the full analysis window (0%–100%) with 100% reported coverage; the 0% onset is the analysis-window boundary rather than a physiological onset, and because coverage is reported as a rounded percentage, this summary is consistent with — but does not by itself prove — the absence of any brief sub-threshold segment. The same caution applies to the 99% values for GAS and ST. BF and TA each retain two separate intervals, so the mean envelope crossed below its own threshold within the cycle for those two channels; the remaining channels' coverage values below 100% likewise imply at least one below-threshold region, but its exact extent is not resolvable from the reported figures alone.

**Max |z| interpretation.** Max |z| is an extreme-value statistic across all 1001 phase points, not a single-observation z-score, and its location marks the **largest standardized difference from the phase-specific reference mean**, not necessarily the time of peak envelope amplitude or any gait event. "Above reference" means above the reference mean at that phase point, not above a healthy percentile limit. The conventional 2-SD rule does not apply: in this dataset every one of 109 healthy controls exceeds |z| = 2 on at least one muscle, and per-muscle healthy 95th percentiles for this statistic run 3.65 (ERS) to 4.61 (TA), higher still under leave-one-out. Against those supplied anchors, GAS (12.1), TA (9.9), RF (6.9), VL (5.8) and ST (4.9) exceed the upper end of that percentile span (4.61), and ERS (4.5) exceeds the ERS-specific 95th percentile of 3.65; BF (3.8) falls inside the 3.65–4.61 span, and no BF-specific percentile is supplied, so its position relative to a BF limit cannot be determined here. Because no speed-matched reference exists for this subject, these standardized differences cannot be attributed to stroke pathology as opposed to walking at 0.11 m/s.

**Co-contraction indices.** CCI is the Falconer–Winter whole-cycle overlap index of the normalized envelopes (2 × common area / summed areas). It is not simultaneous activation time, not muscular force, and not a phase-specific result; it can be non-zero even when detected bursts do not overlap, since sub-threshold envelope area contributes. Values depend on the normalization and envelope pipeline used here and cannot be compared with thresholds from other studies or algorithms.
- **TA vs GAS = 0.71**, above the upper endpoint of the supplied healthy interval (median 0.39; 2.5th–97.5th pct 0.24–0.59).
- **RF vs BF = 0.76**, inside the supplied healthy interval (median 0.68; 0.48–0.83) and above that median, positioned in the upper portion of the interval.

Being inside that interval does not establish physiologically normal co-activation or normal muscle function, and being outside it does not establish pathological co-activation, spasticity, stiffness, impaired motor control, or any diagnosis — particularly with no speed-matched reference available.

## 4. Questions for Further Clinical Assessment

Posed as open questions; none presupposes that the mechanism or measurement issue has been observed, and none implies a treatment recommendation.

1. Are individual gait events (particularly paretic toe-off) and matched muscle-specific timing references available elsewhere in the release, so that the reported envelope intervals could be re-expressed against events rather than cycle percentages?
2. Is a stride-level (non-ensemble) EMG dataset available that would permit assessment of whether the near-continuous above-threshold coverage seen in the mean envelopes is present in individual strides, or whether it reflects averaging across strides?
3. Have raw-signal quality checks been performed for these seven channels — baseline noise, movement or ECG artifact, and cross-talk between adjacent muscles — any of which could influence the 20%-of-own-peak threshold, the coverage values, and the whole-cycle CCI?
4. Given that a low-amplitude envelope with an elevated noise floor can raise both coverage and overlap indices, is a signal-to-noise or baseline estimate retrievable for the channels with 99–100% coverage (GAS, ST, ERS)?
5. At 0.11 m/s, is any slow-speed healthy or within-cohort comparison dataset available? Without one, deviations here cannot be separated into speed-related and stroke-related components.
6. Are clinical descriptors for this participant (age, sex, time post-stroke, clinical scores, assistive-device or orthosis use during the recording) available in the release to contextualize this summary?
7. Would coronal and transverse plane kinematics, and the contralateral side, be obtainable for this session, recognizing that the EMG normalization denominator is side-specific and would not support direct cross-side amplitude comparison?
8. Is a leave-one-out reference run available for this subject's kinematic comparison, given that the supplied percentiles are pooled calibration values?
9. Do the two step-length definitions (successive-footfall vs. instantaneous) yield concordant symmetry values for this subject, since only the successive-footfall value is reported here?
