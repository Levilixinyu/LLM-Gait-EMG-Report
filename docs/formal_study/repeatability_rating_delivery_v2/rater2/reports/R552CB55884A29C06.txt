All seven envelopes have explicitly reported above-threshold continuity across the initial-contact cycle boundary. ERS has a retained interval spanning the entire analysis window. These are mean-envelope findings and do not demonstrate continuous physiological contraction or the same pattern in every stride.

Amplitudes follow the supplied normalization to a shared maximum across available strides before averaging, without further amplitude normalization. They are relative envelope values, not force or strength, and do not support ranking muscles as stronger or weaker.

The maximum \|z\| location identifies the **largest standardized difference**, not necessarily the envelope peak or a gait event. “Above” and “below” refer to the phase-specific reference mean at that location, not a healthy percentile limit. These extreme-value statistics should not be classified using a conventional 2-SD rule.

**Whole-cycle CCI:**
- **TA–GAS: 0.71**, above the supplied empirical interval of 0.24–0.59 and median of 0.39.
- **RF–BF: 0.72**, within the supplied empirical interval of 0.48–0.83 and above its median of 0.68.

CCI measures whole-cycle overlap of the normalized continuous envelopes, including subthreshold contributions. It is not simultaneous activation time, muscular force, or a phase-specific result. Its values depend on normalization and envelope processing; neither comparison establishes normal or pathological muscle function.
