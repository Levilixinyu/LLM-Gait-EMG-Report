They do not identify physiological activation onset, offset, contraction duration or inactivity outside retained intervals, and do not establish the pattern in every stride.

Above-threshold continuity across the initial-contact cycle boundary is explicitly reported for GAS, RF, VL, ST, TA and ERS. RF and ERS have full-window retained intervals. These are mean-envelope threshold findings, not evidence of continuous physiological contraction. A retained interval beginning at 0%, including BF’s first interval, does not establish physiological onset.

Normalization used a participant-, side- and muscle-specific maximum shared across available strides before averaging, without further amplitude normalization here. The amplitudes therefore cannot be interpreted as force, strength or cross-muscle strength rankings.

Maximum |z| identifies the largest standardized reference difference across 1001 cycle points, **not necessarily peak envelope amplitude or a gait event**. “Above” and “below” refer to the phase-specific reference mean at that location. TA’s 3.3 is below its supplied pooled calibration 95th percentile of 4.61; ERS’s 3.9 is above its corresponding value of 3.65. Exact muscle-specific percentiles are not supplied for the other rows. These calibration percentiles are not matched leave-one-out normal limits; a conventional |z| > 2 classification is inappropriate.

**Whole-cycle CCI:**
- **TA–GAS: 0.60**, just above the displayed empirical interval of 0.24–0.59; supplied median 0.39.
- **RF–BF: 0.46**, below the displayed empirical interval of 0.48–0.83; supplied median 0.68.

CCI measures whole-cycle overlap of normalized continuous envelopes, including sub-threshold contributions. It is not simultaneous activation time, force or a phase-specific result. These comparisons do not establish pathological co-activation, stiffness or impaired control; values depend on the normalization and envelope methods.
