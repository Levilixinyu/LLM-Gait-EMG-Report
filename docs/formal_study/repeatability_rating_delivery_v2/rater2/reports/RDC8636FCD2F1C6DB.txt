## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

These are **ensemble-averaged envelopes**, normalized using a participant-, side- and muscle-specific maximum shared across available strides before averaging, without further amplitude normalization. The amplitudes are relative envelope values, not force or strength, and do not establish stride-to-stride variability.

RF, VL and TA have explicitly reported mean-envelope continuity above threshold across the initial-contact cycle boundary. BF, ST and ERS have full-window retained intervals with boundary continuity. These findings describe threshold behavior of the averaged curves—not physiological onset, offset, continuous contraction or a pattern established in every stride. Other intervals do not establish inactivity outside them. Matched muscle-specific timing references are unavailable, so timing cannot be classified as normal, early, delayed or prolonged.

All listed maximum-|z| locations are **above the phase-specific reference mean**. They identify the largest standardized differences, not necessarily envelope-amplitude peaks or gait events. TA’s 5.1 exceeds the supplied pooled healthy 95th percentile of 4.61, and ERS’s 3.9 exceeds 3.65. Exact corresponding percentiles for the other muscles are not supplied. These extreme-value statistics should not be interpreted using a conventional |z| > 2 rule.

**Whole-cycle CCI:**
- **TA–GAS: 0.59**, above the supplied median of 0.39 and numerically equal to the displayed upper endpoint of the 0.24–0.59 empirical interval. Rounded values do not resolve its position relative to an unrounded endpoint.
- **RF–BF: 0.76**, above the supplied median of 0.68 and within the 0.48–0.83 empirical interval.

CCI measures whole-cycle overlap of normalized continuous envelopes, including sub-threshold contributions. It is not simultaneous activation time, force or a phase-specific result. Its magnitude depends on normalization and envelope methods and does not establish normal muscle function, pathological co-activation or stiffness.
