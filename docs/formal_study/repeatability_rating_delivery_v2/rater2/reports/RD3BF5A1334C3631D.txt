## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

Retained intervals describe where the **ensemble-averaged envelope** exceeds 20% of its own peak, subject to a minimum interval of 3% of the cycle by sample count. They are not physiological onset/offset measurements and do not establish continuous contraction, inactivity elsewhere or the same pattern in every stride.

All seven mean envelopes are explicitly reported as remaining above threshold across the initial-contact cycle boundary. ERS is reported above threshold throughout 0%–100%, not merely as a rounded coverage value. These remain envelope-level observations.

Amplitudes use a shared maximum across available strides for each participant, side and muscle; strides were not independently rescaled to their own peaks. These relative amplitudes do not measure strength, force or absolute neural activation, and the average curves cannot establish stride-to-stride variability.

At each listed maximum-|z| location, the envelope is **above the phase-specific healthy-reference mean**. These locations identify the largest standardized reference differences, not necessarily envelope peaks or gait events. Maximum |z| is an extreme statistic across 1001 points, so a conventional 2-SD rule is inappropriate. TA’s 4.1 is below the supplied pooled healthy TA 95th percentile of 4.61; ERS’s 7.7 is above the supplied ERS value of 3.65. **Whole-cycle CCI comparisons:**

- **TA–GAS: 0.67**, above the supplied empirical interval of 0.24–0.59 and median of 0.39.
- **RF–BF: 0.72**, within the supplied interval of 0.48–0.83 and above its median of 0.68.

CCI measures whole-cycle overlap of continuous normalized EMG envelopes, including sub-threshold contributions. It is not simultaneous activation time, force or a phase-specific result. These comparisons do not establish pathological or physiologically normal co-activation; values depend on the normalization and envelope methods.
