RF, VL, BF, ST, and TA have retained intervals that remain above threshold across the initial-contact cycle boundary. ERS is reported above threshold throughout the analysis cycle and across that boundary. These are **mean-envelope threshold properties**, not physiological onset, offset, contraction duration, or evidence of inactivity outside retained intervals. They do not establish the same pattern in each stride.

Normalization uses a participant-, side-, and muscle-specific shared maximum across available strides before averaging, without further amplitude normalization here. These relative amplitudes do not measure force or strength and cannot rank muscles as stronger or weaker.

At every listed maximum-|z| location, the envelope is **above the phase-specific reference mean**. These locations identify the largest standardized reference differences, not necessarily envelope peaks or gait events. Maximum |z| is an across-cycle extreme statistic; the conventional 2-SD rule is inappropriate. TA’s 3.8 is below the supplied pooled healthy 95th percentile of 4.61, whereas ERS’s 7.0 is above its supplied percentile of 3.65. Exact muscle-specific percentiles are not supplied for the other muscles.

**Whole-cycle CCI:**
- **TA–GAS: 0.49**, above the supplied median of 0.39 and within the empirical 0.24–0.59 interval.
- **RF–BF: 0.78**, above the supplied median of 0.68 and within the empirical 0.48–0.83 interval, toward its upper endpoint.

CCI measures whole-cycle overlap of normalized continuous envelopes, including subthreshold contributions. It is not simultaneous activation time, force, or a phase-specific result. These values do not establish physiologically normal co-activation or normal muscle function, and their interpretation depends on the normalization and envelope methods.
