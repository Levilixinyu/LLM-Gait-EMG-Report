## 1. Objective Findings

**Participant and side.** Sub38_P is recorded in the stroke group; the analyzed limb is the **paretic** side. The released cycle runs from initial contact of that limb (0%) to its next initial contact (100%); these are two successive occurrences of the same event, not a single instant. No intermediate gait event (including individual toe-off) is supplied, so all timing below is expressed as a percentage of the analysis cycle only.

**Supplied spatiotemporal values.**
- Gait speed **0.81 m/s**, carrying the label "full community ambulator (>0.8 m/s)". This label denotes a speed band only; it does not measure walking independence, safety, or real-world community mobility.
- Cadence **99.6 steps/min**.
- Step length symmetry index **−13.1%**, computed with the **successive-footfall** definition (each heel at its own initial contact). By the stated sign convention, the negative value indicates the **paretic step is shorter** than the non-paretic step. The alternative instantaneous definition can yield a different value in some subjects; only the successive-footfall value is supplied here.

**Reference used.** Deviation scores compare this subject with **138 pooled healthy subjects** (1001 phase points, source: main_cohort_excel). That cohort pools both body sides and all ages and is not age- or speed-matched to this participant. This subject's speed (0.81 m/s) falls inside the healthy cohort's overall self-selected speed span (0.80–1.79 m/s), but sitting inside an overall range does not establish that a genuinely matched reference subset exists, and it is at the low end of that span. Any difference from the reference therefore reflects the combined influence of walking speed, age, side pooling, and stroke-related control changes, which these data cannot separate. Differences from an empirical reference do not by themselves establish disease, a lesion effect, or a clinical diagnosis.

**Channels available.** Sagittal-plane kinematics for ankle, knee, hip and pelvis; ensemble-averaged EMG envelopes for GAS, RF, VL, BF, ST, TA and ERS; two co-contraction indices (TA–GAS, RF–BF).

**Not supplied in this input.** Individual toe-off and any other intra-cycle event; muscle-specific matched normal timing references; stride-level (stride-to-stride) variability; raw-signal quality checks or expert plots; non-paretic-side EMG or kinematics; coronal- and transverse-plane joint data (explicitly outside the released metric set); per-joint GVS reference values. Age, sex, gait events, time post-stroke and clinical stroke scores may exist elsewhere in the release but are not part of this input — they should be treated as **not supplied here**, not as documented absent recordings.

## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

**How these numbers must be read.** All EMG values are properties of an **ensemble-averaged envelope**, normalized with a shared maximum across available strides before averaging (in this stroke release the denominator is participant-, side- and muscle-specific and is *not* shared across sides). Amplitudes are therefore **relative envelope values**, not neural drive, force or strength, and muscles cannot be ranked as stronger or weaker from them. "Above-threshold intervals" are regions where the averaged envelope exceeds 20% of that same curve's own peak, with a minimum retained length of 3% of the cycle. These are algorithmic properties of the mean curve — not physiological onset, offset, contraction duration, or proof of inactivity outside the intervals — and they do not establish that the same pattern occurs in every individual stride. An interval written X%–Y% with X > Y wraps the 100%→0% boundary, i.e. the mean envelope remains above threshold across the cycle-defining initial-contact boundary.

| RF | 50–60; 63–76; 78–82; 87–45 (wraps) | 86% | 0.67 | 3.5 above reference, at 28% |

| ERS | 44–38 (wraps) | 95% | 0.75 | 6.8 above reference, at 90% |

**Interval structure (mean-envelope level).** VL, BF, ST, ERS, and the long RF and TA intervals show **boundary continuity**: the averaged envelope remains above its own 20% threshold across the 100%/0% initial-contact boundary. GAS is the one muscle whose retained interval lies entirely within the cycle (9%–53%). RF's detection is fragmented into four retained intervals totalling 86% coverage, with regions between retained intervals around 45–50%, 60–63%, 76–78% and 82–87%. TA has two retained intervals (67% coverage) with a region between them near 26–54% and near 79–84%. ERS has the highest coverage (95%), with the region between the wrapped interval's endpoints lying between roughly 38% and 44%. These gap regions simply did not meet the threshold-plus-duration criteria on the mean curve; they should not be described as periods of muscle inactivity, and conversely the covered spans should not be described as continuous contraction. Anatomically, GAS is a plantar flexor, TA a dorsiflexor, RF/VL knee extensors (RF also a hip flexor), BF/ST hamstrings, and ERS a trunk extensor — but no interval here can be assigned a gait-phase function (weight acceptance, push-off, clearance) or labelled early, delayed, prolonged or normal, because the required event and matched-timing references are not supplied.

**Max |z| values.** These are **extreme-value statistics** — the largest absolute standardized difference across all 1001 phase points — and the conventional 2-SD rule does not apply. In this dataset every one of 109 healthy controls exceeds |z| = 2 on at least one muscle, and per-muscle healthy 95th percentiles for this statistic run from 3.65 (ERS) to 4.61 (TA), higher still under leave-one-out. On that basis, the values for GAS (2.3), VL (2.3), BF (2.0), ST (2.2), TA (2.1) and RF (3.5) all fall **below the lowest supplied per-muscle 95th percentile**, so they are not distinguishable from the healthy reference distribution of this statistic. **ERS at 6.8 exceeds every supplied per-muscle 95th percentile**, making it the clearly largest standardized difference in this dataset. The location of a max |z| (e.g. 90% for ERS, 28% for RF) marks where the standardized difference from the phase-specific reference mean is greatest — it is not necessarily the time of peak envelope amplitude and identifies no gait event. "Above reference" means above the reference **mean** at that phase point, not above a healthy percentile limit; TA's single "below reference" entry at 72% is likewise relative to the phase-specific mean.

**Co-contraction indices.** CCI here is the Falconer–Winter whole-cycle overlap index of the normalized envelopes (2 × common area / summed areas). It is not simultaneous activation time, not muscular force, and not phase-specific; it can be non-zero even where detected bursts do not overlap, because sub-threshold envelope activity contributes. It is also method-dependent — values and thresholds from other studies or processing pipelines cannot be transferred.
- **TA vs GAS = 0.38**, essentially at the supplied healthy median of 0.39 and within the 2.5th–97.5th percentile interval 0.24–0.59.
- **RF vs BF = 0.71**, slightly above the supplied healthy median of 0.68 and within the interval 0.48–0.83.

Lying inside these empirical intervals does not establish physiologically normal co-activation or normal muscle function; lying outside would not establish pathological co-activation, stiffness or impaired motor control.

## 3. Kinematic and Reference Comparisons

All joint values are **sagittal plane only**; no coronal or transverse data are included, so nothing can be said about frontal- or transverse-plane mechanics.

| Hip | 40.6° | 1.70 |
| Pelvis | 2.5° | 0.99 |

**Composite (GPS-like) score = 3.92** — the RMS of the four sagittal GVS values, not the validated 9-variable GPS. Against the supplied composite reference (median 5.1, 95th percentile 10.7), this subject's composite lies **below the healthy median**, i.e. the overall sagittal waveform difference from the pooled reference is smaller than that of the typical healthy reference subject on this index.

Within the composite, the ankle (5.39) and knee (5.35) contribute the largest per-joint differences and the hip (1.70) and pelvis (0.99) the smallest. GVS and the GPS-like composite are **summary magnitudes of difference from the stated reference across the cycle**: they do not indicate the direction of the waveform difference (e.g. more flexed vs more extended), do not localize the difference to a phase, and do not identify a mechanism. ROM values on their own likewise do not establish a clinical restriction — no matched ROM reference is supplied for these joints, and the pelvic ROM of 2.5° in particular cannot be judged without an appropriate comparison and knowledge of the marker/model conventions.

The reference here is a **pooled calibration cohort of 138 subjects**, not a matched leave-one-out normal limit. Its percentiles should not be read as individualized normal limits for this participant. Because the reference is neither age- nor speed-matched and this subject walks near the lower edge of the reference speed span, slow-walking effects and stroke-related control changes remain confounded; the comparison can characterize *difference from this reference*, but cannot attribute that difference to stroke pathology or establish any clinical diagnosis. The same limits would apply to a control participant compared against this reference.

## 4. Questions for Further Clinical Assessment

The following are open questions raised by the limits of this summary; none presupposes that the issue has been observed, and none implies a management decision.

1. **Event timing.** Can individual toe-off (and other intra-cycle events) for this limb be retrieved from the release so that envelope intervals and max-|z| locations can be placed relative to this participant's own stance/swing transition rather than reported as raw cycle percentages?
2. **ERS channel.** The ERS max |z| of 6.8 (at 90%) and 95% coverage stand apart from all other channels. Is there any independent information — raw-signal quality review, electrode placement notes, cross-talk or movement-artifact screening — that would help determine whether this reflects a recorded trunk-extensor envelope pattern, a normalization artifact (a low participant/side/muscle-specific denominator compresses the dynamic range and raises relative coverage), or a signal-quality issue? None of these has been assessed here.
3. **Stride-level behaviour.** Are single-stride envelopes available? The supplied averages cannot show whether the retained intervals and boundary continuity are consistent across strides or driven by a subset of them.
4. **Contralateral comparison.** Is a non-paretic-side dataset available for this participant? Note that EMG amplitudes could not be compared directly across sides, since the normalization denominator is side-specific.
5. **Reference matching.** Is a speed- and age-matched (ideally leave-one-out) healthy subset available? At 0.81 m/s the participant sits at the lower margin of the reference speed span, and a matched comparison would clarify how much of the observed deviation is attributable to walking speed.
6. **Step-length definition.** Would the instantaneous step-length definition yield a materially different symmetry index for this subject? The two definitions diverge in some participants, and only the successive-footfall value (−13.1%) is supplied.
7. **Out-of-plane and clinical context.** Are coronal/transverse kinematics, and the stroke-cohort fields not included here (time post-stroke, clinical scores, age/sex), available in the source release for a fuller interpretation?
