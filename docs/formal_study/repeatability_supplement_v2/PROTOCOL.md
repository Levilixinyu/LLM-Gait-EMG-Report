## Amended analysis

The principal repeatability comparison uses the two newly generated responses. For each participant, model and reviewer, C is the number of case requirements Covered divided by the fixed case denominator K. Partially covered has no fractional credit; unknown review states are not omissions. Report G03 separately. The participant-level repeatability statistic is V = abs(C_new1 - C_new2). Average V equally across participants and express it in percentage points; present both coverage levels alongside the difference. A low difference does not establish high quality.

For each reviewer, any-error is positive if at least one in-scope Error is identified, negative only after complete issue review without Error or Pending, and otherwise unknown. Show the two-response status pattern and pair discordance. Do not pool the 450 ratings as independent observations, convert Pending into Error, or substitute the original response for a missing new response.

Principal variation estimates require two normally completed, fully rated new captures. Administrative interruptions and other incomplete captures remain in the delivery accounting and available-content ratings, but their case/model pairs are excluded from the principal complete-capture variability estimate and reported explicitly. Include a secondary available-text estimate using both captured texts where rating is possible, flagging operational interruptions; present its denominator separately. This amendment identifies the known interruption before clinical scoring and does not remove it from the record or replace it.

Report results separately for each reviewer. For each model, show every planned participant and the reasons for incomplete pairs. Use 20,000 participant-cluster percentile bootstrap draws, seed 20260925, retaining all models, repeats and reviewer labels within each sampled participant. Give descriptive 95% intervals; no interval with fewer than two complete participant pairs. Label constant intervals. These intervals are conditional on two observed new responses and cannot precisely characterize the full stochastic output distribution. Between-model descriptive contrasts use participants with complete pairs for both models.

For all-planned accounting, retain all 50 new slots per model. Unknown coverage gives bounds [known Covered/K, (known Covered + unknown)/K]; average across the two repeats and then participants. For any-error rates, unknown states contribute zero or one to bounds. Known unequal binary pair states contribute discordance 1, known equal states 0, and any unknown member gives pair bounds [0,1]; average over all 25 participants. Also show a conservative operational-missing sensitivity treating an incomplete capture as unknown instead of interpreting its missing text as model omission. Bounds are not confidence intervals, and coverage variability is unavailable for incomplete pairs rather than inferred from mean-coverage bounds.

The three-report series consists of the original report and two new reports. Present this as a separate historical comparison, using comparable original independent ratings and disclosing later rubric clarifications and possible provider version/time drift. Do not mix old consensus ratings with new first-pass ratings or describe all three as simultaneous identically evaluated draws. Record returned versions; a shared model alias is not proof of an unchanged backend.

Report report-length distributions descriptively. No per-word normalization is claimed to eliminate length confounding. Human agreement (cross-tabs, raw agreement, pairwise nominal kappa and supplementary AC1) remains distinct from correctness. No new severity weights, best-response selection or confirmatory model ranking is introduced.
