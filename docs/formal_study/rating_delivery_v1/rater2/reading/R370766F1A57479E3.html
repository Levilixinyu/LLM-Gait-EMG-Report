## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

All values below are properties of an **ensemble-averaged envelope** per muscle, normalized to a shared maximum across available strides (not per-stride rescaling), with intervals defined as regions exceeding **20% of that curve's own peak** and retained only if ≥3% of the cycle. These are threshold-crossing properties of a mean curve — they are **not** physiological onset, offset, contraction duration, or evidence of inactivity outside the intervals, and they do not imply the same pattern occurred in every individual stride. Amplitudes are relative envelope values and cannot be read as strength, force, or neural drive, and cannot be used to rank muscles against each other. Percentages refer to position in the analysis cycle only; no stance/swing, loading-response, pre-swing or push-off assignment is possible because no intermediate event boundaries are supplied.

**Boundary continuity.** Six of seven envelopes (RF, VL, BF, ST, TA, ERS) remain above their own 20% threshold across the 100%→0% cycle boundary, i.e. across the initial-contact boundary of contributing strides. GAS is the one muscle whose retained interval lies wholly within the cycle, beginning at 6% and ending at 48%. The RF interval is written as 0%–100%: the 0% onset is the analysis-window boundary and not a physiological onset, and the reported 100% coverage is a rounded integer value — it is consistent with the envelope remaining above threshold throughout, but a rounded coverage figure alone does not establish that no below-threshold segment exists at finer resolution. The same caution applies to reading ERS's 90% coverage as a single uninterrupted region; only the retained interval listed is established.

**Amplitude.** Peak normalized amplitudes range from 0.55 (RF) to 0.71 (GAS) on the shared-maximum scale. These are relative envelope heights within each curve's own normalization and carry no force or strength meaning, nor any implication about stride-to-stride consistency.

**Standardized differences.** The "max abs. z" is an **extreme-value statistic** — the largest standardized difference across all 1001 phase points — and the conventional 2-SD rule does not apply to it. In this dataset every one of the 109 healthy controls exceeds |z| = 2 on at least one muscle, and per-muscle healthy 95th percentiles for this statistic run from 3.65 (ERS) to 4.61 (TA), higher still under leave-one-out. All values here (1.1–2.2) fall below those percentile figures. The largest single standardized difference is RF at 2.2 (above the phase-specific reference mean at 66% of cycle), followed by BF at 2.1 (below the phase-specific reference mean at 98%). "Above"/"below" here means relative to the reference **mean at that phase point**, not relative to any healthy percentile limit. The location of maximum |z| marks the largest standardized difference, not necessarily the point of peak envelope amplitude and not a gait event.

**Co-contraction indices.** CCI is the Falconer–Winter whole-cycle overlap index of normalized envelopes (2 × common area / summed areas). It is a whole-cycle index, not a duration of simultaneous activation, not a force measure, and not phase-specific; it can be non-zero even where detected bursts do not overlap, because sub-threshold envelope area contributes. It depends on the normalization and envelope pipeline used here, so values and thresholds from other studies or algorithms are not transferable.

- **TA vs GAS: 0.32.** This lies inside the supplied healthy 2.5th–97.5th percentile interval (0.24–0.59) and below the supplied healthy median of 0.39, positioned in the lower part of that interval.
- **RF vs BF: 0.56.** This lies inside the supplied healthy interval (0.48–0.83) and below the supplied median of 0.68, relatively closer to the lower endpoint.

Falling within these empirical intervals does not establish physiologically normal co-activation or normal muscle function, just as falling outside would not establish pathological co-activation, stiffness, or impaired motor control.
