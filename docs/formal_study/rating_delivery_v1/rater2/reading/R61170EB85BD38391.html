## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

**How to read these numbers.** Each interval is a region where that muscle's **ensemble-averaged** envelope exceeds 20% of its **own** peak, with a minimum retained duration of 3% of the cycle. Normalization uses a shared maximum across available strides before averaging, so amplitudes are relative envelope values — not neural drive, force or strength — and muscles cannot be ranked against each other. These are properties of the mean curve: they do not mark physiological onset or offset, do not indicate continuous contraction inside an interval, and do not indicate inactivity outside one. The same pattern should not be assumed in every individual stride, and an averaged curve carries no information about stride-to-stride variability.

| ERS | 0%–100% | 100% | 0.59 | 1.6 above reference @ 9% |

**Boundary continuity.** RF, VL, BF, ST and TA each retain an interval that wraps the 100%→0% boundary, i.e. the averaged envelope remains above its 20% threshold across the cycle-defining initial-contact boundary. GAS is the one muscle whose single retained interval lies wholly within the cycle (5%–47%). RF and TA each additionally retain a second, separate interval (62%–66% and 52%–80% respectively). Intervals may be described by their numerical proximity to the initial-contact boundary, but no interval can be assigned a gait-phase function such as clearance, push-off or weight acceptance, and none can be called normal, early, delayed or prolonged without matched timing references.

**ERS.** The reported single interval spans the entire analysis window (0%–100%) with 100% coverage; at the mean-envelope level this indicates no retained segment below the 20% threshold anywhere in the averaged cycle. This is a threshold property of an averaged, self-normalized curve and does not establish sustained or continuous erector spinae contraction, nor does it establish that any individual stride behaved this way. Note also that an onset of 0% is an analysis-window boundary, not a physiological onset.

**Anatomical naming only.** The recorded muscles include an ankle plantar flexor (GAS), an ankle dorsiflexor (TA), knee extensors (RF, VL), knee flexors/hip extensors (BF, ST) and a trunk extensor (ERS). Naming these actions is descriptive anatomy and does not assign a functional role to any interval in this cycle.

**Maximum |z| interpretation.** The reported max |z| is an extreme-value statistic — the largest standardized difference across all 1001 phase points versus the pooled healthy phase-specific mean/SD — not a single-observation z-score, and its location is the point of largest standardized difference, not necessarily the point of peak envelope amplitude and not a gait event. "Above" or "below reference" means relative to the phase-specific reference mean at that location, not relative to a healthy percentile limit. In this dataset every one of 109 healthy controls exceeds |z| = 2 on at least one muscle, and per-muscle healthy 95th percentiles for this statistic run 3.65 (ERS) to 4.61 (TA), higher under leave-one-out. Sub09's largest values (RF 2.3, ST 2.2, GAS 2.0) therefore fall well below the applicable per-muscle healthy 95th percentiles and the conventional 2-SD rule does not apply here.

**Co-contraction indices.** CCI is the Falconer–Winter whole-cycle overlap index of the two normalized envelopes (2 × common area / summed areas). It is not simultaneous activation time, not muscular force, not phase-specific, and it can be non-zero even when detected intervals do not overlap, because sub-threshold envelope area contributes. Values depend on this pipeline's normalization and envelope methods and must not be compared with thresholds from other studies or algorithms.

- **TA vs GAS = 0.35** — inside the supplied healthy interval 0.24–0.59, somewhat below the supplied median of 0.39.
- **RF vs BF = 0.58** — inside the supplied healthy interval 0.48–0.83, below the supplied median of 0.68 and nearer the lower endpoint of that interval.

Lying inside these empirical intervals does not establish physiologically normal co-activation or normal muscle function; lying outside them would not establish pathological co-activation, stiffness or impaired motor control.

7. Are coronal- and transverse-plane kinematics available? The present deviation scores are sagittal-only, so any non-sagittal contribution to overall gait deviation is outside these numbers.
8. Is contextual information on the testing session (footwear, walkway length, assistive devices, number of contributing strides) documented, since these can influence averaged envelopes and cycle-normalized kinematics?
