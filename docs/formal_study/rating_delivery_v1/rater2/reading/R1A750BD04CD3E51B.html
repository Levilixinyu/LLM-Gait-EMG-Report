## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

**How these numbers must be read.** The curves are **ensemble-averaged envelopes**. Normalization uses a shared maximum across available strides before averaging, participant-, side- and muscle-specific; the released mean curves are used without further amplitude normalization. Consequently:
- Amplitudes are **relative envelope values**, not neural drive, force or strength. Muscles cannot be ranked as stronger or weaker from these numbers.
- Intervals are properties of the **averaged** curve under the stated rule (envelope exceeds 20% of that curve's own peak, minimum retained length 3% of the cycle). They are **not physiological onsets, offsets, contraction durations, or evidence of inactivity outside the interval**, and they do not establish that the same pattern occurred in every stride.
- An average curve carries **no information about stride-to-stride variability**.
- Because no toe-off or matched muscle-specific normal timing is supplied, no interval below can be called early, delayed, prolonged or normal, and none can be mapped to a functional role such as clearance, push-off or weight acceptance.

Coverage values of 59–87% mean that this proportion of the averaged cycle sits above each curve's own 20%-of-peak line. Because the threshold is curve-relative, coverage is not comparable across muscles as an amplitude statement. Where two or more intervals are listed (GAS, BF, ST, TA), the gaps between retained intervals are regions that did not meet the threshold-plus-duration criteria in the mean curve; ST and TA show the most fragmented retained pattern (three and four retained intervals respectively). For the single-wrapping channels (RF, VL, ERS), the supplied evidence does not establish that the complement of the retained interval contains no below-threshold segment beyond what the interval boundaries themselves imply — and in no case does a retained interval imply continuous contraction within it.

**Max |z| interpretation.** Each max |z| is an **extreme-value statistic** taken as the largest absolute standardized difference across all 1001 phase points versus the pooled healthy mean/SD at each phase. It marks the location of the **largest standardized difference**, not the time of peak envelope amplitude and not a gait event. The conventional 2-SD rule does not apply: in this dataset every one of 109 healthy controls exceeds |z| = 2 on at least one muscle, and per-muscle healthy 95th percentiles for this statistic run 3.65 (ERS) to 4.61 (TA), higher still under leave-one-out. Against those supplied per-muscle healthy percentiles, **TA (2.3) and ERS (2.7) sit below the corresponding healthy 95th-percentile figures**, while GAS (8.1), VL (6.4) and BF (6.9) are numerically well above the range spanned by those percentiles, and RF (4.4) and ST (3.3) are intermediate. "Above reference" and "below reference" here mean relative to the **phase-specific reference mean at that location** — not relative to a healthy percentile limit. TA is the only channel whose largest standardized difference is in the below-reference direction. Given the absence of a speed-matched reference, none of these values can be attributed to stroke pathology as opposed to walking at 0.42 m/s, and none establishes abnormality of the underlying muscle.

**Co-contraction indices.** CCI is the Falconer–Winter index over the **full continuous envelopes** (2×common area / summed areas), a **whole-cycle overlap index of normalized envelopes**. It is not a duration of simultaneous activation, not force, and not phase-specific; it can be non-zero even when detected bursts do not overlap, because sub-threshold activity contributes. It is dependent on this normalization and envelope pipeline, so values and thresholds from other studies or algorithms do not transfer.
- **TA vs GAS = 0.47**, inside the supplied healthy interval 0.24–0.59 and above the supplied median of 0.39.
- **RF vs BF = 0.62**, inside the supplied healthy interval 0.48–0.83 and below the supplied median of 0.68.

Both indices falling inside the supplied empirical intervals does **not** establish physiologically normal co-activation or normal muscle function; conversely, values outside such an interval would not establish pathological co-activation, stiffness or impaired control. The same limited-comparability caveat applies as for the kinematics, since the CCI reference is drawn from the same unmatched pooled cohort.

**What GVS and the composite do and do not convey.** Each GVS is a summary RMS difference between this subject's curve and the stated reference across the cycle. Among the four channels, the knee carries the largest summary difference (12.28) and the pelvis the smallest (1.48); this ranking describes the reference comparison, not a mechanism.

**ROM.** The listed ROM values are excursions within the sagittal plane. **ROM alone does not establish a clinical restriction** in the absence of an appropriate matched comparison, and no ROM reference interval is supplied here. In particular, the ankle ROM of 14.5° and pelvic ROM of 4.5° should not be graded as reduced or normal from this input.

**Comparability limit (applies to this whole section).** The reference is 138 pooled healthy subjects, unmatched for age and walking speed, and this subject's 0.42 m/s lies below the reference cohort's 0.80 m/s floor. Deviations therefore describe distance from an empirical reference population; they do not isolate a stroke-specific effect, do not establish a clinical diagnosis or severity level, and cannot be separated from the influence of markedly slow walking. The pooled percentiles supplied here are calibration values and are **not** matched leave-one-out normal limits. These limits apply symmetrically: membership in a healthy group would not prove normal physiology, and deviation from this reference does not by itself establish disease.

## 4. Questions for Further Clinical Assessment

3. **Contralateral EMG.** Are non-paretic-side envelopes recorded for this participant? Note that the normalization denominator is side-specific, so any side-to-side amplitude comparison would need explicit handling.
4. **Stride-level data.** Are individual stride envelopes retrievable? The averaged curve cannot show whether the multi-interval patterns in ST and TA, or the boundary continuity seen in all seven channels, recur stride to stride or reflect averaging across dissimilar strides.
5. **Signal quality.** Is there documentation of raw-signal quality, electrode placement, or crosstalk screening for these seven channels — particularly for ST, whose peak normalized amplitude of 0.22 sets a low curve-relative threshold, and for the wrapping single-interval channels?
6. **Step-length definition.** Would the instantaneous definition yield a different symmetry value for this subject? The +37.8% figure is tied to the successive-footfall definition, and the sign and magnitude may not be stable across definitions.
7. **Out-of-plane kinematics.** Are coronal and transverse channels available? The composite here rests on four sagittal variables only and is not the validated GPS.
8. **Clinical and demographic context.** Are age, sex, time post-stroke and clinical scores available in the release for this participant? None were supplied to this interpretation, and their absence here should not be read as absence from the source dataset.
