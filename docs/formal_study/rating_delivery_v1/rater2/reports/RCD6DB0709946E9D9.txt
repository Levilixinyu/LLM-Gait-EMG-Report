## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

The retained intervals identify where each **averaged envelope exceeds 20% of its own peak**, subject to a minimum interval of 3% of the cycle by sample count. They are not physiological onset/offset measurements, continuous contraction durations or evidence of inactivity outside the intervals. They also do not establish that every contributing stride had the same pattern.

Normalization used a shared muscle-specific maximum across available strides before averaging, without further amplitude normalization in this pipeline. Peak values are relative envelope amplitudes, not strength, force or absolute neural activation.

RF, VL, BF, ST, TA and ERS explicitly remain above threshold across the cycle boundary at the mean-envelope level. Documentation identifies this as **initial contact of the contributing strides**, not an observed event trace for one named limb. Healthy pooling, normalization and this event anchor are documentary; healthy MAT aggregation and side-specific denominators were not numerically reverified by the supplied audit. No intermediate event boundary is available, so the intervals cannot be assigned to stance, swing or phase-specific functions.

Maximum \|z\| identifies the largest standardized reference difference, not necessarily peak envelope amplitude. “Above” or “below” refers to the phase-specific reference mean at that location. These are extreme-value statistics; exceeding 2 does not justify an abnormality label.

**Whole-cycle envelope overlap:**

- **TA–GAS CCI: 0.40**, within the supplied empirical interval **0.24–0.59** and close to its median **0.39**.
- **RF–BF CCI: 0.55**, within **0.48–0.83**, below the median **0.68** and nearer the lower endpoint.

These Falconer–Winter indices quantify overlap of the full normalized envelopes, including subthreshold contributions. They are not simultaneous activation time, muscular force or phase-specific measurements. Their values depend on normalization and envelope processing; interval membership does not establish physiologically normal co-activation.

The **GPS-like score is 4.02**, below the supplied pooled calibration median of **5.1** and 95th percentile of **10.7**. It is the RMS of the four supplied sagittal GVS values, not the validated nine-variable GPS. The hip has the largest GVS (**4.77**) and the knee the smallest (**3.54**) among these channels.

GVS and GPS-like summarize differences from the reference; they do not identify waveform direction or the cycle location of those differences. ROM values alone, including pelvic ROM of **2.0°**, do not establish clinical restriction without an appropriate comparison.

Leave-one-out exclusion does not make the reference individually matched. The pooled calibration percentiles and CCI intervals are not matched leave-one-out normal limits, and unavailable gait speed prevents assessment of speed comparability. These limitations apply across the kinematic and EMG comparisons: control-group membership does not prove normal physiology, and reference differences alone cannot establish a disease effect or clinical diagnosis.
