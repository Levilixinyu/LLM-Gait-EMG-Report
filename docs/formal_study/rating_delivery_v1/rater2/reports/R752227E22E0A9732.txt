## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

Retained intervals identify portions of the **averaged envelope** exceeding 20% of that curve’s own peak, with a minimum retained duration of 3% of the cycle by sample count. They are not physiological onsets, offsets or contraction durations, and do not establish inactivity outside the retained intervals.

Normalization uses a participant-, side- and muscle-specific maximum shared across available strides before averaging, with no further amplitude normalization here. Peak values therefore describe relative envelope amplitude—not strength, force or absolute neural activation.

This establishes mean-envelope boundary continuity, not continuous physiological contraction or the same pattern in every stride. ERS has the greatest reported coverage, at 87%.

Maximum |z| locates the largest standardized reference difference, **not necessarily peak envelope amplitude or a gait event**. “Above” and “below” refer to the phase-specific reference mean. These whole-curve extreme statistics should not be classified using a conventional |z| = 2 cutoff. TA 2.3 and ERS 2.7 are below their supplied pooled healthy 95th-percentile values of 4.61 and 3.65, respectively; this does not establish normal muscle function.

**Whole-cycle envelope overlap:**
- **TA–GAS CCI:** 0.47, above the supplied median of 0.39 and within the empirical 2.5th–97.5th percentile interval of 0.24–0.59.
- **RF–BF CCI:** 0.62, below the supplied median of 0.68 and within the corresponding interval of 0.48–0.83.

These Falconer–Winter CCIs quantify whole-cycle overlap of normalized continuous envelopes, including sub-threshold contributions. They are not simultaneous activation time, force or phase-specific findings. Their interpretation depends on the normalization and envelope methods; being within these intervals does not establish physiologically normal co-activation.
