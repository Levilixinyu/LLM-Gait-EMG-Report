## 1. Objective Findings

**Participant and side.** Sub49_P is recorded in the stroke cohort. All findings below describe the **paretic** side; no non-paretic-side data were supplied for this task, so no within-subject side comparison is possible.

**Analysis cycle.** The released paretic cycle runs from initial contact of that limb (0%) to the next initial contact of the same limb (100%). These are two successive occurrences of the same event, not one instant. **No intermediate gait events (including toe-off) are supplied**, so all timing below is expressed as a percentage of the analysis cycle only. Percentages are not translated into stance, swing, loading response, mid-stance or pre-swing, and no conventional 60/40 split is assumed.

**Spatiotemporal values (as supplied).**

- Cadence 97.8 steps/min.
- Step length symmetry index −6.6% (successive-footfall definition). The negative sign indicates the **paretic step is the shorter** of the two by this index. Note that an instantaneous-heel-position definition can yield a different value for some subjects; the value here should be read as definition-specific.

**Reference used.** Kinematic/EMG deviations are computed against a pooled reference of **138 healthy subjects, 1001 phase points** (source: main_cohort_excel), pooling both body sides and all ages. This reference is **not age- or speed-matched** to this participant. The healthy reference speeds span 0.80–1.79 m/s; at 0.79 m/s this subject falls **just below that floor**, so no speed-matched healthy comparator exists in this dataset. Deviations therefore conflate slow-walking effects with stroke-related control changes, and these two contributions cannot be separated from the data supplied. Proximity to the 0.80 m/s boundary does not create a matched reference, and the rounded value does not license any assumption about the hidden unrounded speed.

**Channels available.** Sagittal-plane kinematics for ankle, knee, hip and pelvis; seven EMG envelopes (GAS, RF, VL, BF, ST, TA, ERS); two co-contraction indices.

**Channels not supplied in this task.** Coronal and transverse plane kinematics; individual toe-off and other gait events; muscle-specific matched normal timing references; stride-level (individual-stride) data and variability; raw-signal quality checks; expert plots; non-paretic-side data; age, sex, time post-stroke and stroke clinical scores. These are **not supplied for this interpretation** — that is distinct from being documented as absent recordings in the source release.

## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

**How these numbers must be read.** All EMG values derive from **ensemble-averaged envelopes**. Normalization uses a shared maximum across available strides per participant, side and muscle before averaging (in the checked stroke release the denominator is participant-, side- and muscle-specific and is not shared between sides); the released mean curves are used without further amplitude normalization. Consequently:

- Amplitudes are **relative envelope values**, not neural drive, force or strength. Muscles must not be ranked as stronger or weaker from these peaks.
- Detected intervals are properties of the **averaged envelope** under the stated rule (envelope exceeds 20% of that curve's own peak; minimum retained interval 3% of the cycle). They are **not** physiological onset, offset, contraction duration, or continuous contraction, and they do not establish inactivity outside the interval.
- An averaged pattern **cannot be assumed to occur in every individual stride**, and an average curve carries no information about stride-to-stride variability.
- Timing is not labelled normal, early, delayed or prolonged, because matched muscle-specific timing references and individual events are not supplied.

**Boundary continuity.** VL, BF, ST, TA, RF and ERS are each reported as above threshold **across the cycle-defining boundary** — i.e. the averaged envelope does not drop below the 20% criterion at the 100%→0% transition. For RF and ERS the reported interval spans 0%–100% with 100% coverage; the 0% onset is the **analysis-window boundary**, not an identified physiological onset. For GAS, the 5% onset is numerically close to the initial-contact boundary; this is a numerical statement about proximity, not an event assignment or a phase-function attribution.

**Sub-threshold segments.** Coverage values below 100% (GAS 53%, VL 42%, BF 62%, ST 71%, TA 67%) indicate portions of the averaged cycle where that envelope did not meet the retained-interval criterion. For RF and ERS, coverage is reported as 100%; the supplied summary does not itself resolve whether any momentary below-threshold sample exists behind that rounded figure, so no claim of "no below-threshold segment at any point" is made here.

**Max |z| interpretation.** Each max |z| is an **extreme-value statistic** — the largest standardized difference across all 1001 phase points relative to the phase-specific healthy mean/SD — not a single-observation z-score. The conventional 2-SD rule does not apply: in this dataset every one of 109 healthy controls exceeds |z| = 2 on at least one muscle, and per-muscle healthy 95th percentiles for this statistic run 3.65 (ERS) to 4.61 (TA), higher still under leave-one-out. Against that framing:

- **ERS 4.7** exceeds the ERS healthy 95th percentile of 3.65 quoted here; **GAS 4.5** and all remaining values fall within or below the quoted per-muscle percentile band range. These are descriptive comparisons with the supplied percentiles only; they do not constitute statistical significance testing and do not establish disease.
- "Above reference" / "below reference" means relative to the **phase-specific reference mean** at that location — not relative to a healthy percentile limit.
- The location of max |z| (e.g. 56% for GAS, 99% for VL, 65% for ERS) marks the **largest standardized difference**, not necessarily the timing of the envelope's own peak amplitude and not a gait event.
- The pooled, unmatched, speed-unsupported reference applies here as it does to the kinematics; these EMG deviations cannot be attributed wholly to stroke pathology.

**Co-contraction indices.** CCI is the Falconer–Winter index (2 × common area / summed areas) computed on the **full continuous normalized envelopes over the whole cycle**. It is not simultaneous-activation time, not muscular force, not phase-specific, and it can be non-zero even where detected bursts do not overlap, because sub-threshold envelope area contributes. It is method-dependent (normalization and envelope processing); values and thresholds from other studies or algorithms cannot be transferred.

- **TA vs GAS = 0.43** — inside the supplied healthy interval 0.24–0.59, and above the supplied median of 0.39.
- **RF vs BF = 0.77** — inside the supplied healthy interval 0.48–0.83, above the supplied median of 0.68, and positioned toward the upper endpoint of that interval.

Lying inside these empirical intervals does **not** establish physiologically normal co-activation or normal muscle function; lying outside them would not establish pathological co-activation, stiffness, impaired motor control or any diagnosis. The intervals are calibration percentiles from a pooled cohort, not matched leave-one-out limits for this participant.

**Composite (GPS-like) = 5.33.** This is the RMS of the per-joint GVS values across the supplied sagittal channels — it is **not** the validated 9-variable GPS. Against the supplied composite reference (median 5.1, 95th percentile 10.7), 5.33 sits **slightly above the median and well below the 95th percentile**. This is a descriptive comparison with a pooled, non-matched calibration distribution.

**What GVS/GPS-like can and cannot say.** These are **summary magnitude differences** from the stated reference across the cycle. They do **not** localize the deviation to a phase of the cycle and do **not** indicate the direction of the waveform difference (e.g. more flexion vs more extension). Among the per-joint scores, the knee (8.78) carries the largest summary difference from the reference and the pelvis (0.73) the smallest; this ordering describes deviation magnitude only.

**ROM values in isolation.** The supplied ROM figures (including the small 3.1° sagittal pelvic excursion) are reported without a matched ROM comparator in this input. **ROM alone does not establish a clinical restriction** in the absence of an appropriate comparison, and the pooled cohort here is neither age- nor speed-matched.

**Comparability limit (applies to all comparisons above and in Section 2).** The reference is 138 pooled healthy subjects across both sides and all ages, and this participant's speed sits below the reference cohort's speed floor. Any deviation described here therefore blends slow-walking effects with stroke-related differences, and the data supplied cannot separate them or establish a disease effect, mechanism or clinical diagnosis. The same limitation would apply symmetrically if this participant were a control.

## 4. Questions for Further Clinical Assessment

These are open questions raised by the limits of this dataset. None presupposes that the mechanism or measurement issue in question has been observed, and none implies a treatment recommendation.

1. **Gait events.** Can individual toe-off and other event timings for this paretic cycle be retrieved from the release, so that envelope intervals could be positioned relative to the participant's own events rather than to cycle percentage alone?
2. **Contralateral data.** Is a non-paretic-side record available for this participant? Because the EMG denominator is side-specific, any cross-side amplitude comparison would need explicit handling of that normalization.
3. **Stride-level data.** Are individual stride envelopes retrievable? The reported patterns are averaged curves; whether the boundary-spanning above-threshold behaviour (RF, ERS, VL, BF, ST, TA) recurs stride to stride, and how variable the strides are, cannot be determined from means.
4. **Signal quality.** Have raw-signal quality checks (baseline offset, movement artefact, crosstalk, electrode placement — particularly for ERS, which shows 100% coverage and the largest max |z| here) been performed and documented for this record? Whether any such issue is present is not addressed by the supplied summary.
5. **Reference matching.** Is a speed- and age-matched healthy subset, or a leave-one-out reference, available? Given the 0.79 m/s speed and the 0.80 m/s reference floor, this would substantially change the interpretability of the GVS, composite and max |z| comparisons.
6. **Clinical context.** Are time post-stroke, stroke clinical scores, age and sex available in the release for this participant? None were supplied here, and their status (available vs not applicable vs documented absent) should be confirmed before any clinical reading.
7. **Symmetry definition.** Would the instantaneous-heel-position step-length definition yield a different symmetry value for this participant, and which definition is preferred for the intended clinical question?
8. **Non-sagittal planes.** Are coronal/transverse kinematics recorded for this session? The present interpretation is sagittal-only by construction.
