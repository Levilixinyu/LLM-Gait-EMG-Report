**2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings**
*Note: EMG metrics describe an ensemble-averaged envelope relative to its own peak across available strides. Detected intervals denote where the mean envelope met a 20% amplitude threshold and 3% duration rule. They do not represent absolute muscular force, stride-to-stride variability, or physiological onset/offset times, nor does the absence of a detected interval prove complete muscular inactivity.*

*   **Continuous Above-Threshold Activity:** The rectus femoris (RF), vastus lateralis (VL), biceps femoris (BF), semitendinosus (ST), and erector spinae (ERS) maintained above-threshold activity for 100% of the gait cycle, remaining above threshold continuously across the cycle boundary (initial contact). Their peak normalized amplitudes were 0.67 (RF), 0.59 (VL), 0.48 (BF), 0.62 (ST), and 0.55 (ERS).
*   **Gastrocnemius (GAS):** Displayed two above-threshold intervals at 3%-8% and 13%-68% of the cycle, yielding 61% overall coverage. Peak normalized amplitude was 0.51.
*   **Tibialis Anterior (TA):** Displayed above-threshold intervals at 69%-88% and 89%-24% of the cycle (the latter wrapping across the cycle boundary), totaling 59% coverage. Peak normalized amplitude was 0.58.
*   **Co-Contraction Indices (CCI):** Calculated using the Falconer-Winter method, this index represents whole-cycle continuous envelope overlap, dependent on normalization protocols, rather than simultaneous physiological activation time or force.

    *   *RF vs. BF:* CCI was 0.83, which sits precisely at the upper boundary of the healthy reference interval (median 0.68; 2.5th–97.5th percentile 0.48–0.83).
