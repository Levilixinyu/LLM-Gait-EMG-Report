## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

The released envelopes use a shared maximum across available strides for each participant and muscle before averaging, without further amplitude normalization here. Amplitudes are relative envelope values—not strength, force, or absolute neural activation.

Retained intervals exceed **20% of each mean curve’s own peak**, with a minimum retained duration of **3% of the analysis cycle**.

“Wrapping” denotes mean-envelope continuity above threshold across the 100%→0% initial-contact boundary. ERS also has explicitly reported boundary continuity; its retained 0%–100% interval supports above-threshold coverage throughout the sampled mean-envelope cycle. GAS beginning at 0% identifies the analysis-window boundary, not a physiological onset.

These findings do not establish physiological onset, offset, contraction duration, or inactivity outside retained intervals, and need not occur identically in every stride. Intermediate gait events and matched muscle-specific timing references are unavailable, so no phase-function or early/delayed timing interpretation is supported.

VL has the largest reported maximum standardized difference, **|z| = 7.4 at 39%**, above the phase-specific reference mean. Maximum |z| identifies the largest standardized reference difference, not necessarily peak envelope amplitude or a gait event. It is an extreme-value statistic; the conventional single-observation 2-SD rule is inappropriate. A VL-specific leave-one-out percentile limit is not supplied.

**Whole-cycle CCI:**
- **TA–GAS: 0.53**, above the supplied median **0.39**, within **0.24–0.59**.
- **RF–BF: 0.75**, above the supplied median **0.68**, within **0.48–0.83**.

These are Falconer–Winter overlap indices of the full normalized envelopes, including subthreshold contributions. They are not simultaneous activation time, muscular force, or phase-specific findings, and depend on the normalization and envelope methods.
