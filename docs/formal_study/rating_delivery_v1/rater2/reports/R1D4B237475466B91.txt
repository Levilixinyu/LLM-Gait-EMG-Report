## 1. Objective Findings

**Participant and side.** Sub27_P is recorded in the stroke group, and the data supplied are for the **paretic** side. The released cycle for this limb runs from one initial contact of that limb (0%) to the next initial contact (100%); these are two successive occurrences of the same boundary event, not a single instant. **No intermediate gait event (including toe-off) and no phase boundary are supplied in this input.** All timing below is therefore expressed as a percentage of the analysis cycle only, and no percentage here should be read as stance, swing, loading response, mid-stance or pre-swing.

**Spatiotemporal values as supplied:**

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Gait speed | 0.75 m/s (band label: "limited community ambulator", 0.4–0.8 m/s) |
| Cadence | 82.5 steps/min |
| Step length symmetry index | −15.8% (successive-footfall definition) |
| Composite kinematic deviation (GPS-like) | 6.81 |

The ambulation label denotes a **speed band only**. It does not represent measured walking independence, safety, assistive-device use or real-world community mobility, none of which are supplied here.

The symmetry index is negative, which under the stated formula means the **paretic step is shorter** than the non-paretic step by 15.8% of the mean step length. This value was computed with the successive-footfall definition (each heel at its own initial contact); the alternative instantaneous definition can yield a different value in some subjects, and that alternative value is not supplied.

**Reference and comparability limit (applies to every comparison in this report).** The deviation reference actually used for this subject is a **pooled cohort of 138 healthy subjects** (1001 phase points, source: main_cohort_excel), pooling both body sides and all ages; it is not age-, sex- or speed-matched to this participant. This subject's speed of **0.75 m/s falls below the healthy cohort's self-selected speed floor of 0.80 m/s**, so no speed-matched healthy reference exists in this dataset for him. Any deviation reported below therefore **conflates slow-walking effects with stroke-related control changes, and these two cannot be separated from these data.** Deviation from an empirical reference is a statistical statement about distance from a non-matched comparison group; on its own it does not establish a disease effect, a specific impairment mechanism, or a clinical diagnosis.

**Channels available and not available.**

- **Not supplied in this input** (not the same as documented absent from the source release): coronal- and transverse-plane kinematics, individual toe-off and other gait events, matched muscle-specific normal timing references, non-paretic-side data, stride-level variability, raw-signal quality checks, expert plots, age, sex, time post-stroke, and stroke clinical scores. ## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

**How these numbers must be read.** The curves are **ensemble-averaged envelopes**, normalized to a participant-, side- and muscle-specific maximum shared across the available strides before averaging (in the checked stroke release the denominator is not shared between sides). Amplitudes are therefore **relative envelope values**, not absolute activation, force or strength; muscles must not be ranked as stronger or weaker from them. "Above-threshold intervals" are regions where the **averaged** envelope exceeds 20% of that same curve's own peak, with a minimum retained duration of 3% of the cycle. These are properties of the mean curve under that rule — they are **not** physiological onsets, offsets, contraction durations, or evidence of inactivity outside the retained intervals. They also do not demonstrate that the same pattern occurs in every individual stride.

| Muscle | Retained interval(s) (% cycle) | Coverage | Peak norm. amp. | Max abs. z (location) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| GAS | 22–28; 36–66; 99–21 (wraps) | 58% | 0.55 | 15.2 above reference, at 57% |
| RF | 11–61; 69–76; 90–95; 99–11 (wraps) | 76% | 0.58 | 6.2 above reference, at 36% |
| VL | 87–56 (wraps) | 69% | 0.58 | 6.4 above reference, at 36% |
| BF | 16–20; 57–60; 89–15 (wraps) | 35% | 0.79 | 2.9 above reference, at 97% |
| ST | 45–62; 86–28 (wraps) | 58% | 0.68 | 3.4 above reference, at 51% |
| TA | 68–37 (wraps) | 69% | 0.63 | 5.9 above reference, at 32% |
| ERS | 49–28 (wraps) | 81% | 0.81 | 8.2 above reference, at 63% |

**Boundary continuity.** For all seven muscles the mean envelope remains above the 20%-of-own-peak threshold **across the 0%/100% cycle boundary**, i.e. across the initial-contact boundary of the released cycle. This is a continuity property of the averaged curve at that boundary; it does not by itself indicate sustained or continuous physiological contraction, and it is not assigned here to any gait-phase function such as weight acceptance, push-off or clearance.

**Coverage.** Mean-envelope coverage ranges from 35% (BF) to 81% (ERS). Where coverage is below 100% and multiple intervals are retained, there are regions of the **averaged** curve that did not satisfy the threshold-plus-duration criteria — for example, the gaps between GAS's retained intervals. These regions are not established as electrically silent, and no claim of muscle inactivity is made. Conversely, for the single-wrapping-interval muscles (VL, TA, ERS) the supplied evidence does not establish the absence of any below-threshold segment beyond what the coverage figure itself implies.

Anatomically, the recorded muscles include ankle plantarflexors (GAS), ankle dorsiflexors (TA), knee extensors (RF, VL), knee flexors/hip extensors (BF, ST) and trunk erector spinae (ERS). Naming these actions is permitted; mapping any specific interval to a functional gait task is not supported without the missing event and matched-timing data. Because no matched muscle-specific normal timing reference is supplied, **no interval here can be called normal, early, delayed or prolonged.**

**Maximum |z| values.** Max |z| is the **largest standardized difference across all 1001 phase points**, i.e. an extreme-value statistic, not a single-observation z-score. In this dataset every one of 109 healthy controls exceeds |z| = 2 on at least one muscle, so the conventional 2-SD rule must not be applied. The supplied per-muscle healthy 95th percentiles for this statistic span **3.65 (ERS) to 4.61 (TA)**, and are higher still under leave-one-out; per-muscle percentiles for GAS, RF, VL, BF and ST are not supplied and are not invented here.

Against the values that are supplied: GAS (15.2), ERS (8.2 vs its own 3.65), VL (6.4), RF (6.2) and TA (5.9 vs its own 4.61) all exceed the upper end of that stated percentile range; BF (2.9) and ST (3.4) fall below its lower end. "Above reference" in each row means the curve lies above the **phase-specific reference mean at that phase point**, not above a healthy percentile limit. The listed location is where the standardized difference is largest — it is **not** necessarily where the envelope reaches its peak amplitude, and it is not a gait event. These comparisons inherit the full comparability limit stated in Section 1 (pooled, unmatched, and below the reference speed floor), so they do not establish pathological muscle activity.

**Co-contraction indices.** CCI is the Falconer–Winter index (2 × common area / summed areas) computed on the **full continuous normalized envelopes across the whole cycle**. It is not simultaneous activation time, not muscular force, and not a phase-specific result; it can be non-zero even where detected bursts do not overlap, because sub-threshold envelope area contributes.

- **TA vs GAS = 0.40** — inside the supplied healthy interval 0.24–0.59 and close to the supplied median of 0.39.
- **RF vs BF = 0.54** — inside the supplied healthy interval 0.48–0.83, and nearer its **lower endpoint (0.48)** than the supplied median of 0.68.

Falling inside these intervals does **not** establish physiologically normal co-activation or normal muscle function; falling outside them would not establish pathological co-activation, spasticity, stiffness or impaired motor control. These values depend on the normalization and envelope-processing pipeline used here, and must not be compared with CCI values or thresholds from other studies or algorithms.

## 3. Kinematic and Reference Comparisons

All kinematic values are **sagittal plane only**; coronal and transverse motion is not included in this input and cannot be commented on.

|---|---|---|
| Ankle | 34.8 | 7.82 |
| Knee | 59.2 | 9.04 |
| Hip | 41.6 | 5.03 |
| Pelvis | 7.8 | 4.16 |

**Composite (GPS-like) = 6.81.** This is the RMS of the per-joint GVS values over the sagittal channels provided — it is **not** the validated 9-variable GPS. Relative to the supplied composite reference (median 5.1, 95th percentile 10.7), 6.81 lies **above the median and below the 95th percentile**.

**Per-joint interpretation limits.** GVS is a summary root-mean-square difference from the reference mean waveform across the cycle. Within this subject's set, the knee GVS (9.04) is the largest and the pelvis GVS (4.16) the smallest, but **no per-joint healthy reference values are supplied**, so no individual GVS can be classified as within or outside normal limits. GVS and the composite score are magnitude summaries only: neither localizes the deviation to a phase of the cycle, nor indicates the direction of the waveform difference (for example, more flexion versus more extension).

**ROM.** The ROM figures are descriptive. Without an appropriate matched comparison, ROM alone does not establish a clinical restriction, a contracture, or excessive motion at any joint. No such joint-level comparison is supplied here.

**Comparability.** These kinematic comparisons use the pooled 138-subject reference described in Section 1 and inherit the same limits: the reference is not age-, sex- or side-matched, and this subject walks below its speed floor, so slow-walking effects and stroke-related changes cannot be disentangled. The pooled calibration percentiles quoted in this report are **not** matched leave-one-out normal limits, and a speed falling inside some overall range would not by itself prove that an appropriate matched reference exists. These same limits would apply if this participant were a control: neither group membership nor deviation magnitude, on its own, establishes normal physiology or disease.

## 4. Questions for Further Clinical Assessment

These are open questions raised by the limits of the supplied summary. None presupposes that the mechanism or measurement issue in question has been observed, and none implies a management plan.

1. **Event timing.** Can individual toe-off and other gait events for this paretic cycle be retrieved from the release? Without them, no interval in Section 2 can be assigned to stance or swing, and the conventional 60% split must not be substituted.
2. **Matched reference.** Is a speed-matched (or speed-adjusted) and age-matched healthy reference obtainable, given that 0.75 m/s lies below the 0.80 m/s cohort floor? Would a leave-one-out or slow-walking comparison change the apparent magnitude of the EMG and kinematic deviations?
3. **Signal-level review.** Has raw-signal quality for the paretic channels — particularly GAS, whose max |z| of 15.2 is far above the other channels — been reviewed for artifact, electrode placement, cross-talk or baseline offset? This is a question, not an assertion that any such issue is present.
4. **Normalization dependence.** How sensitive are the retained intervals, coverage values and both CCIs to the 20%-of-own-peak threshold, the 3% duration rule, and the participant/side/muscle-specific normalization denominator?
5. **Stride-level behaviour.** Are individual stride envelopes available? The averaged curves supplied cannot support any statement about stride-to-stride consistency or variability of the observed pattern.
6. **Contralateral and out-of-plane data.** Are non-paretic-side EMG and kinematics, and coronal/transverse-plane kinematics, available for this participant? Both would materially change what the step-length asymmetry (−15.8%, paretic step shorter) and the sagittal-only composite score can be taken to mean.
7. **Step-length definition.** Would the instantaneous-heel definition yield a different symmetry value for this subject, and which definition is preferred for the intended comparison?
8. **Clinical context.** Are age, sex, time post-stroke and clinical scores available in the release for this participant, so that the quantitative summary can be placed alongside a clinical examination that this report does not substitute for?
