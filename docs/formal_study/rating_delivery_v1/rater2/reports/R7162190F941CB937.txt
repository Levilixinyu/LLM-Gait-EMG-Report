**2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings**
EMG parameters are derived from ensemble-averaged envelopes normalized to their own peak amplitudes during available strides. The relative amplitudes do not represent absolute muscle force, strength, or stride-to-stride variability. Under the applied algorithm (threshold of 20% of peak, minimum duration of 3% of the cycle), all seven evaluated muscles exhibited above-threshold activity that crossed the analysis-cycle boundary (0%/100%, corresponding to consecutive initial contacts). 

Total above-threshold envelope coverage across the cycle was: ERS 81%, RF 76%, VL 69%, TA 69%, GAS 58%, ST 58%, and BF 35%. 
- Multiple discrete above-threshold intervals within the average curve were detected for RF (11%-61%, 69%-76%, 90%-95%, and 99%-11%), GAS (22%-28%, 36%-66%, and 99%-21%), ST (45%-62% and 86%-28%), and BF (16%-20%, 57%-60%, and 89%-15%).
- Single continuous above-threshold intervals wrapping the cycle boundary were retained for VL (87%-56%), TA (68%-37%), and ERS (49%-28%).
- Peak normalized amplitudes ranged from 0.55 (GAS) to 0.81 (ERS).

Whole-cycle co-contraction indices (CCI) calculated from the overlapping normalized envelopes were 0.40 for TA vs GAS and 0.54 for RF vs BF. Both indices fall within the dataset's healthy empirical intervals (0.24-0.59 and 0.48-0.83, respectively), with TA vs GAS near the reference median of 0.39 and RF vs BF below the reference median of 0.68. This normalization-dependent overlap metric does not establish either physiologically normal muscle function or pathological co-activation.
