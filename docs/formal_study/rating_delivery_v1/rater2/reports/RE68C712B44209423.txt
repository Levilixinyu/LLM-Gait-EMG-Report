# Gait Report Interpretation — Sub18_P

## 1. Objective Findings

**Participant and side.** Sub18_P is recorded in the stroke group, and all data summarized here are from the **paretic** side. The analysis cycle runs from one initial contact of that limb (0%) to the next initial contact of the same limb (100%); these are two successive occurrences of the same event, not a single instant. **No intermediate gait event (including toe-off) is supplied**, so all timing below is expressed purely as a percentage of the analysis cycle. Percentages cannot be translated into stance/swing, loading response, mid-stance or pre-swing, and no conventional 60% stance/swing split has been applied.

**Spatiotemporal values as supplied.**
- Gait speed **0.34 m/s**, which falls in the "household ambulator (<0.4 m/s)" speed band. This label denotes a speed band only; it is not a measurement of walking independence, safety, supervision needs or real-world community mobility.
- Cadence **62.2 steps/min**.
- Step length symmetry index **−3.9%** using the **successive-footfall** definition (each heel at its own initial contact). By the stated sign convention, the negative value indicates the **paretic step is the shorter of the two** by this index. An instantaneous-heel-position definition can yield a different value in some subjects; only the successive-footfall value was supplied here.

**Reference used and its limits.** The kinematic deviation reference for this subject is **138 pooled healthy subjects, 1001 phase points (main_cohort_excel)**. This reference pools both body sides and all ages and is **not age- or speed-matched** to this participant. Critically, the healthy cohort's self-selected speeds span **0.80–1.79 m/s**, and this subject walks at **0.34 m/s** — well below that floor — so **no speed-matched healthy reference exists in this dataset** for him. Every deviation reported in Sections 2 and 3 therefore conflates slow-walking effects with any stroke-related control change, and the two **cannot be separated from these data**. Comparisons below are descriptive differences from an imperfectly comparable empirical reference; they do not by themselves establish a disease effect, a severity grade or a clinical diagnosis. Percentile values quoted from the pooled calibration cohort are **not matched leave-one-out normal limits**.

**What is available vs. not supplied here.**
- Available: sagittal-plane joint angle ROM and Gait Variable Scores for ankle, knee, hip and pelvis; a composite (GPS-like, RMS of the supplied sagittal GVS channels — not the validated 9-variable GPS); seven paretic-side averaged EMG envelopes with detected intervals; two co-contraction indices.
- **Not included by definition:** coronal- and transverse-plane kinematics.
- **Not supplied in this input:** individual toe-off and other gait events; muscle-specific matched normal timing references; stride-level (stride-to-stride) data; raw-signal quality checks; expert plots; non-paretic-side EMG or kinematics; age, sex, time post-stroke and stroke clinical scores. These are "not supplied to this interpretation" — they must not be described as absent from the source release, which may contain some of them.
- Dataset-level note: the local array audit evaluated **614 of 700** subject/muscle combinations, with 86 lacking valid nonzero paired samples. This is a property of the dataset audit and is reported here for context, not as a statement about this subject's channels.

## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

**How these numbers must be read.** All EMG values are from **ensemble-averaged envelopes**. Normalization uses a shared maximum across available strides for that participant, side and muscle before averaging; in this stroke release the denominator is participant-, side- and muscle-specific and is **not shared between sides**. Consequently amplitudes are **relative envelope values only** — not absolute activation, force or strength — and muscles **cannot be ranked as stronger or weaker**, nor compared across sides or subjects on amplitude. Detected intervals are regions where the **mean** envelope exceeds 20% of **its own peak**, with a minimum retained length of 3% of the cycle. They are properties of that averaged curve under that rule, **not** physiological onset, offset, contraction duration or proven inactivity outside the intervals, and they must **not** be assumed to recur identically in every stride. An interval written X%–Y% with X > Y wraps the 100%→0% boundary, meaning the mean envelope remained above threshold across the cycle-defining initial contact. An onset listed at 0% is the analysis-window edge, not a physiological onset. Because no matched timing reference or gait events are supplied, **no interval is characterized here as early, delayed, prolonged or normal**, and no interval is mapped to a functional role such as clearance, push-off or weight acceptance.

**Reading of the envelope pattern.** At the mean-envelope level, all seven recorded channels show retained above-threshold regions that are continuous across the cycle boundary, and reported coverage is high in six of seven channels (81–100%), with TA the lowest at 65% and GAS fragmented into several retained segments plus a boundary-wrapping segment. For RF, ST and ERS the reported coverage is 100% and the retained interval spans the full analysis window; because these are rounded summary values and no sample-level listing is supplied, I **do not** assert that no below-threshold sample exists anywhere in those curves — the supplied evidence establishes the reported coverage and the retained interval, not the absence of sub-threshold samples. Likewise, the gaps between GAS and TA intervals indicate only that the averaged envelope did not meet the 20%-of-own-peak plus 3%-duration criterion there; they do not establish muscle silence, and they do not establish that individual strides behaved the same way.

**Max |z| values.** Max |z| is an **extreme-value statistic** — the largest standardized difference across all 1001 phase points versus the phase-specific pooled reference mean/SD — not a single-observation z-score. In this dataset *every* one of 109 healthy controls exceeds |z| = 2 on at least one muscle, and per-muscle healthy 95th percentiles for this statistic run **3.65 (ERS) to 4.61 (TA)**, higher again under leave-one-out. The conventional 2-SD rule therefore does not apply. Against the supplied values: **BF (2.4)** sits below the whole span of listed per-muscle 95th percentiles; **TA (4.2)** is below its own supplied TA 95th percentile of 4.61; **ERS (4.2)** is above its own supplied ERS 95th percentile of 3.65. For **GAS (16.8), VL (6.5), ST (6.1) and RF (5.1)** no muscle-specific 95th percentile was supplied, but each exceeds the upper end (4.61) of the range of listed per-muscle percentiles, GAS by a very large margin. "Above reference" in each row means the curve lay above the **phase-specific reference mean** at that location — not above a healthy percentile limit. The quoted location (e.g. 57% for GAS) is where the **largest standardized difference** occurred; it is not necessarily where the envelope peaked and it does not identify a gait event or phase. All of these comparisons inherit the pooled, unmatched, sub-speed-floor limitation described in Section 1 and cannot, on their own, establish pathological muscle behaviour.

**Co-contraction indices.** CCI here is the Falconer–Winter index computed on the **full continuous normalized envelopes** over the whole cycle (2 × common area / summed areas). It is a whole-cycle overlap index of envelope shapes — **not** simultaneous activation time, not muscular force, not a phase-specific result — and it can be non-zero even where detected bursts do not overlap, because sub-threshold activity contributes. It is dependent on this normalization and envelope pipeline, so values and cut-offs from other studies or algorithms are not transferable.
- **TA vs GAS = 0.60**, against a healthy-cohort median of 0.39 (2.5th–97.5th percentile 0.24–0.59). This lies just **outside the upper endpoint** of that interval, by 0.01 at the displayed precision — a near-boundary value that should not be over-interpreted, and one that does not by itself establish pathological co-activation, spasticity, stiffness or impaired motor control.
- **RF vs BF = 0.74**, against a healthy median of 0.68 (0.48–0.83). This lies **inside** the supplied interval, above the median and below the upper endpoint. Falling inside the interval does not establish physiologically normal co-activation or normal muscle function.

All joint data are **sagittal plane only**; coronal and transverse motion are not included and cannot be commented on.

Among the supplied channels, the **knee** carries the largest Gait Variable Score (19.10), followed by ankle (12.53) and hip (12.02), with pelvis lowest (7.14). The composite of **13.39** exceeds the supplied composite reference median of **5.1** and also lies above the pooled calibration **95th percentile of 10.7**. That percentile is a pooled-cohort calibration figure, not a matched leave-one-out normal limit for this participant, and exceeding it does not constitute a severity grade, a statistical test result or evidence of a specific pathological mechanism.

GVS and the GPS-like composite are **summary magnitudes of difference** from the stated reference waveforms. They are scalar RMS-type quantities: they do **not** indicate the direction of the waveform difference (e.g. more flexed vs more extended), do **not** localize the deviation to any part of the cycle, and do not identify which joint behaviour drives the value. The ROM figures are descriptive ranges only; **ROM alone does not establish a clinical restriction** in the absence of an appropriate matched comparison, and none is available here. Because this participant walks at 0.34 m/s — below the reference cohort's 0.80 m/s floor — and because the reference pools sides and ages, these elevated deviation scores reflect a combination of slow-walking effects and any individual-specific control differences that cannot be disentangled from these data. They do not by themselves establish a disease effect or support any clinical diagnosis. The same interpretive limits would apply to a control participant compared against this reference; healthy-group membership would not prove normal physiology, just as stroke-group membership does not, on its own, explain any particular number above.

## 4. Questions for Further Clinical Assessment

These are open questions about what evidence could be obtained; none presupposes that the mechanism or measurement issue in question has been observed, and none implies a treatment plan.

1. Are individual gait events — particularly paretic toe-off and contralateral events — available in the source release for this subject, so that intervals could be expressed relative to actual phase boundaries rather than cycle percentages?
2. Are stride-level (non-ensembled) EMG envelopes available, to assess whether the mean-envelope pattern (including the boundary-continuous, near-full-coverage channels) is representative of individual strides or is partly an averaging effect?
3. Are raw-signal quality records available — electrode placement notes, baseline noise, movement artefact, possible crosstalk (e.g. between adjacent shank or thigh channels) — that would bear on the very high GAS max |z| and on the high coverage figures? Has any such issue been examined for this recording?
4. Given that the 20%-of-own-peak threshold is referenced to each curve's own peak, would a supplementary analysis using an alternative threshold or normalization show a different interval structure, particularly for GAS and TA?
5. Is a subset of the healthy cohort (or an external dataset) available that is closer in gait speed, age and side, so that the deviation scores and max |z| values could be compared against a more comparable reference? Absent that, can a slow-walking control condition be recorded?
6. Are non-paretic-side kinematics and EMG available? (Note that, with side-specific normalization denominators in this release, between-side EMG amplitude comparison would not be valid without a common reference.)
7. Are coronal- and transverse-plane kinematics available, given that the present composite reflects sagittal channels only and is not the validated 9-variable GPS?
8. Are the additional stroke-cohort source fields — time post-stroke, clinical scores, non-paretic-side designation, age and sex — retrievable for this subject to contextualize these summary metrics?
9. What were the walking conditions during capture (footwear, orthosis, assistive device, handrail or supervision, overground vs treadmill, familiarization)? These bear directly on how the 0.34 m/s speed and the resulting reference mismatch should be interpreted.
10. Would a repeat session establish the within-subject reproducibility of these summary values, which cannot be judged from a single set of averaged curves?

*All statements above are limited to the supplied quantitative summary for Sub18_P. No diagnosis, severity grading, statistical significance, prognosis or management recommendation is offered or implied.*
