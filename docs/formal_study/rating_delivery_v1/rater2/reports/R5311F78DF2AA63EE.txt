RF, VL, BF, ST, TA, and ERS have reported above-threshold continuity across the cycle boundary. ERS has the greatest reported coverage, **90%**. These findings describe threshold behavior of mean envelopes—not physiological onset, offset, contraction duration, inactivity outside retained intervals, or a pattern demonstrated in every stride. No intermediate gait events or phase functions can be assigned to these percentages.

Normalization uses a muscle-specific shared maximum across available strides before averaging, without further amplitude normalization here. The amplitudes are relative envelope values, not strength, force, or absolute neural activation.

The maximum \|z\| location is the **largest standardized reference difference**, not necessarily peak envelope amplitude or a gait event. Above/below refers to the phase-specific reference mean. ERS **4.1** exceeds the supplied pooled ERS 95th percentile of **3.65**; TA **2.3** is below the supplied pooled TA 95th percentile of **4.61**. These pooled thresholds are not matched leave-one-out limits. This extreme-value statistic should not be classified using a conventional 2-SD rule.

Values within these intervals do not establish normal muscle function or physiological co-activation; interpretation remains dependent on this normalization and envelope method.

The **GPS-like score is 5.91**, above the supplied pooled median of **5.1** and below its 95th percentile of **10.7**. It is the RMS of the supplied sagittal-channel GVS values, not the validated nine-variable GPS.

All reference comparisons in this report are descriptive and have limited comparability. The reference cohort is not participant-matched, gait speed is unavailable, and pooled calibration percentiles are not matched leave-one-out normal limits. Neither these deviations nor positions within empirical intervals establish a disease effect, clinical diagnosis, or normal physiology.
