Their reported boundary continuity, and the wrapped intervals for VL, BF, ST, and TA, indicate above-threshold continuity across the initial-contact boundary **at the mean-envelope level**. These findings do not establish continuous physiological contraction. Segments outside retained intervals should not be interpreted as inactivity. Without individual toe-off and matched muscle-specific timing references, the intervals cannot be assigned gait-phase functions or characterized as normal, early, delayed, or prolonged.

Amplitude normalization uses a shared maximum across available strides for each participant, side, and muscle—not independent normalization of each stride—with no further amplitude normalization of the released mean curves. These relative values do not measure force or strength or support stronger/weaker rankings between muscles.

Maximum |z| identifies the largest standardized reference difference across 1001 cycle points, **not the envelope-amplitude peak**. Above/below denotes direction relative to the reference mean at that location. ERS 4.7 exceeds its supplied healthy calibration 95th percentile of 3.65; TA 2.3 is below its corresponding value of 4.61. The conventional |z| = 2 rule is inappropriate for these extreme-value statistics.

**Whole-cycle CCI:**

- **RF–BF:** 0.77, above the supplied median of 0.68 and within the empirical interval 0.48–0.83.

CCI quantifies whole-cycle overlap of normalized continuous envelopes, including subthreshold contributions. It is method-dependent and is not simultaneous activation time, force, or a phase-specific result. Being within these intervals does not establish normal physiological co-activation.
