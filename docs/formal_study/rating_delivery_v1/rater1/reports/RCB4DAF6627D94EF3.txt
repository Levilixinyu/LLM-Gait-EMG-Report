2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings
EMG metrics are based on ensemble-averaged envelopes, normalized to each muscle's respective maximum over available strides. Above-threshold intervals represent regions where the average envelope exceeded 20% of its own peak for at least 3% of the cycle. These metrics reflect average properties under this defined rule; they do not establish physiological onset, offset, duration, continuous contraction, or inactivity outside the intervals. "Wrapping" indicates the mean envelope remains above threshold across the 100% to 0% boundary, designated as the initial contact of contributing strides.

- GAS: Retained interval at 0%-52%; 53% of the cycle is above threshold. Peak normalized amplitude is 0.59.
- RF: Retained interval at 80%-70% (wrapping across the initial-contact boundary); 90% of the cycle is above threshold. Peak normalized amplitude is 0.50.
- VL: Retained intervals at 70%-76% and 79%-60% (wrapping across the initial-contact boundary); 87% of the cycle is above threshold. Peak normalized amplitude is 0.62.
- BF: Retained intervals at 27%-49%, 59%-68%, and 70%-9% (wrapping across the initial-contact boundary); 70% of the cycle is above threshold. Peak normalized amplitude is 0.61.
- ST: Retained intervals at 36%-44% and 85%-10% (wrapping across the initial-contact boundary); 33% of the cycle is above threshold. Peak normalized amplitude is 0.66.
- TA: Retained intervals at 37%-42%, 61%-79%, 81%-91%, and 92%-34% (wrapping across the initial-contact boundary); 76% of the cycle is above threshold. Peak normalized amplitude is 0.52.
- ERS: Retained interval at 0%-100% (wrapping across the initial-contact boundary); 100% of the cycle is above threshold. Peak normalized amplitude is 0.53.

Co-contraction indices (CCI) compute the whole-cycle overlap of these continuous normalized envelopes. The TA-vs-GAS CCI is 0.53, which is greater than the supplied reference median of 0.39 but within the empirical reference interval of 0.24 to 0.59. The RF-vs-BF CCI is 0.75, which is greater than the reference median of 0.68 and within the empirical interval of 0.48 to 0.83. Being within these reference intervals does not establish physiologically normal co-activation or specific muscular function, as these values depend heavily on the normalization method and capture algorithm.
