## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

These are **ensemble-averaged envelope** findings. Normalization uses a participant-, side-, and muscle-specific maximum shared across available strides before averaging, without further amplitude normalization here. Values represent relative envelope amplitude, not strength, force, or absolute neural activation.

RF, VL, BF, ST, TA, and ERS have documented above-threshold continuity across the initial-contact cycle boundary **at the mean-envelope level**. Coverage and retained intervals do not establish physiological onset or offset, continuous contraction, inactivity outside intervals, or the same pattern in every stride. Without intermediate events and matched timing references, no gait-phase function or normal/early/delayed timing classification is supported.

Maximum |z| identifies the largest standardized reference difference, **not necessarily peak envelope amplitude or a gait event**. “Above” and “below” refer to the phase-specific reference mean. ERS 5.5 exceeds its supplied pooled healthy 95th-percentile calibration value of 3.65; TA 1.4 is below its corresponding value of 4.61. Exact muscle-specific cutoffs are not supplied for the other muscles. These cycle-wide extreme statistics should not be classified using a conventional |z| > 2 rule.

**Whole-cycle CCI:**
- **TA–GAS: 0.53**, within the empirical interval **0.24–0.59** and above the median **0.39**.
- **RF–BF: 0.67**, within **0.48–0.83** and close to the median **0.68**.

CCI quantifies whole-cycle overlap of normalized continuous envelopes, including subthreshold contributions. It is method-dependent and is not simultaneous activation time, muscular force, or a phase-specific result. Being within these intervals does not establish physiologically normal co-activation or muscle function.
