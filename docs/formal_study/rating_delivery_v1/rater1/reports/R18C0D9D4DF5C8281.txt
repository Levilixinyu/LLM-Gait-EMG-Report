## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

All statements below are properties of the **ensemble-averaged envelope** under the stated rule: above-threshold = exceeding 20% of that same curve's own peak, minimum retained interval 3% of the cycle. Detected intervals are not physiological onsets, offsets, contraction durations, or evidence of inactivity outside the intervals, and a mean-curve pattern does not imply the same pattern in every individual stride. Amplitudes are relative envelope values normalized within participant/side/muscle; they are **not** force, strength, or neural drive, and muscles must not be ranked against one another on this basis.

**Coverage and boundary continuity.** Every recorded muscle's averaged envelope is reported as above threshold across the cycle-defining boundary (continuity from the 100% end into the 0% end at the mean-curve level). GAS, BF and ERS are reported at 100% coverage with a single 0%–100% interval; RF, VL, ST and TA show high but sub-total coverage (79%, 59%, 89%, 90%), meaning below-threshold portions of the averaged curve exist for those four. For GAS, BF and ERS, the coverage value as displayed is rounded, so I do not assert that *no* below-threshold sample exists — only that none was retained under the threshold-plus-duration rule as reported. An onset written as 0% is the analysis-window boundary, not a physiological onset. RF's listing contains adjacent/overlapping segments (68–72, 72–81) within the broader wrapping interval (95–61); this is a property of the segmentation output as supplied, not a separate physiological event.

**Standardized differences.** Max |z| is an extreme-value statistic over 1001 phase points, so the conventional 2-SD rule does not apply: in this dataset every one of 109 healthy controls exceeds |z| = 2 on at least one muscle. Per-muscle healthy 95th percentiles for this statistic span 3.65 (ERS) to 4.61 (TA), higher still under leave-one-out, and only these two endpoint values are individually identified.
- GAS (10.6), RF (8.2) and VL (5.7) exceed the upper end of that stated range (4.61), so they exceed the healthy 95th percentile whichever muscle-specific value applies.
- ERS (6.5) exceeds its own stated 95th percentile (3.65).
- TA (3.8) is **below** its stated 95th percentile (4.61).
- BF (3.0) and ST (1.5) fall below the lower end of the range (3.65), so they are below whichever muscle-specific percentile applies.
Exceeding a pooled calibration percentile is not the same as exceeding a matched leave-one-out normal limit, and does not establish abnormality, pathology, or a disease effect — particularly with no speed-matched reference available.

**Location of maximum |z|.** Each stated percentage marks the largest standardized difference from the phase-specific reference mean, not the time of peak envelope amplitude and not a gait event. "Above reference" means above the reference mean at that phase point; "below reference" (ST at 96%) means below the reference mean at that phase point. Neither statement is relative to a healthy percentile limit.

**Co-contraction indices.** CCI here is the Falconer–Winter whole-cycle overlap index of the two normalized envelopes (2×common area / summed areas). It is not simultaneous activation time, not force, and not phase-specific; it can be non-zero even when detected bursts do not overlap, since sub-threshold envelope area contributes. It is method-dependent, so values and thresholds from other studies or algorithms do not transfer.

## 4. Questions for Further Clinical Assessment

These are open questions raised by the supplied summary. None presupposes that the mechanism or measurement issue in question has been observed, and none implies a treatment plan.

1. **Event data.** Can individual toe-off and other gait events for this subject's paretic cycles be retrieved from the release, so that envelope intervals can be related to stance/swing subdivisions rather than to cycle percentages alone?
2. **Speed-comparability strategy.** Given that no healthy reference at 0.24 m/s exists in this dataset, is there an alternative slow-speed reference set, or a within-subject repeated-measures design, that could separate slow-walking effects from stroke-related control differences?
3. **Stride-level behaviour.** Are per-stride envelopes and stride counts available? The supplied averaged curves cannot indicate stride-to-stride consistency, and the continuous above-threshold coverage seen on GAS, BF and ERS at the mean level may or may not be present in each stride.
4. **Signal quality.** Have raw-signal checks (baseline offset, movement artefact, ECG contamination for ERS, crosstalk between adjacent channels) been performed for this recording? High fractional coverage combined with a within-curve percentage-of-own-peak threshold can arise from either broad envelope activity or an elevated baseline, and these data cannot distinguish them.
5. **Pelvic kinematics.** Is the 0.7° sagittal pelvic ROM consistent with the marker set, model definition and static calibration used for this session, and is the same value reproducible across trials?
6. **Step-length definition.** Would the instantaneous-heel definition change the +16.3% symmetry value for this subject, and which definition is preferred for the intended comparison?
7. **Contralateral and clinical context.** Are non-paretic-side kinematics and EMG, plus age, sex, time post-stroke and clinical scores, available in the release for this participant? Note that any cross-side EMG amplitude comparison would be limited by the side-specific normalization denominator.
8. **Analysis-pipeline context.** For reproducibility, should this subject's channels be checked against the 614 evaluable / 86 non-evaluable combination audit to confirm all seven muscles here were among the evaluable set?

*All interpretations above are constrained to the supplied mean-curve metrics, detected intervals and reference summaries. No statement here establishes normal or abnormal physiology, a disease effect, or a clinical diagnosis.*
