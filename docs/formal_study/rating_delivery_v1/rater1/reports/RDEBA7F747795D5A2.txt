# Gait Report Interpretation — Sub33_P

**Interpretation version:** limited-evidence-v3.1-candidate
**Scope:** Research interpretation of a supplied quantitative summary. This is not a diagnosis, not a clinical assessment of walking ability, and not a basis for treatment selection.

## 1. Objective Findings

**Participant and side.** Sub33_P is a member of the stroke cohort. All data below come from the **paretic** side. The analysis cycle is defined from initial contact of the paretic limb (0%) to the next initial contact of the same limb (100%); these are two successive occurrences of the same event, not a single instant. No intermediate gait event (toe-off, or any phase boundary) is supplied for this participant, so all timing below is reported as a percentage of the analysis cycle only.

**Spatiotemporal values as supplied.**
- Gait speed 0.22 m/s, which falls in the "household ambulator (<0.4 m/s)" speed band. This label denotes a speed band only; it does not measure walking independence, safety, or real-world mobility, and none of those were assessed here.
- Cadence 62.7 steps/min.
- Step length symmetry index +8.6%, using the **successive-footfall** definition. By the stated sign convention, a positive value indicates the paretic step is the *longer* of the two. Note that the alternative (instantaneous) step-length definition yields a different value in some subjects, so this figure is definition-dependent and should not be compared with symmetry values computed under the other convention.
- Composite kinematic deviation score (GPS-like) 10.56.

**Reference used.** Kinematic deviations are computed against 138 pooled healthy subjects (1001 phase points, source: main_cohort_excel). This reference pools both body sides and all ages and is **not** age- or speed-matched to this participant.

**A central comparability limit for this report.** This participant's gait speed of 0.22 m/s lies well below the healthy reference cohort's self-selected speed range of 0.80–1.79 m/s. **No speed-matched healthy reference exists in this dataset for this subject.** Every reference comparison below therefore conflates the effects of walking very slowly with any stroke-related changes in motor control, and these two contributions cannot be separated from these data. Deviations should be read strictly as "differs from this pooled reference," not as a measured stroke effect, a severity grade, or a diagnosis. Group membership in the stroke cohort is documented, but it is not established by these numbers and does not itself explain any individual value here.

**Channels supplied and not supplied.** Seven EMG envelope channels (GAS, RF, VL, BF, ST, TA, ERS) and four sagittal-plane kinematic channels (ankle, knee, hip, pelvis) are supplied. Coronal and transverse plane kinematics are not part of this input. Individual toe-off timing, muscle-specific matched normal timing references, stride-by-stride data, raw-signal quality checks, and expert plots are outside this input; their absence here does not mean they are absent from the source release. Age, sex, time post-stroke, clinical stroke scores and non-paretic-side data are not provided in this summary.

## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

**How these numbers must be read.** Each row describes a single **ensemble-averaged envelope** per muscle. Amplitudes are normalized to a shared maximum across available strides for that participant, side, and muscle (in the checked stroke release the denominator is participant-, side-, and muscle-specific and is not shared across sides), and the released mean curves are used without further normalization. Consequently: amplitudes are **relative envelope values only** — they are not neural drive, force, or strength, and muscles cannot be ranked as stronger or weaker from them. An averaged curve also cannot show stride-to-stride variability, so nothing below describes consistency across strides.

"Above-threshold interval" means the region where the averaged envelope exceeds **20% of that same curve's own peak**, with a minimum retained length of 3% of the cycle. These are properties of the averaged envelope under that rule — not physiological onsets, offsets, contraction durations, or evidence of inactivity outside the intervals. An interval written with a start greater than its end wraps the 100%→0% boundary, meaning the mean envelope remains above its own threshold across the cycle-defining initial-contact boundary. Coverage percentages likewise describe the mean curve and should not be assumed to hold in every individual stride.

**Summary of the envelope pattern.** All seven averaged envelopes show boundary continuity — each remains above its own 20%-of-peak threshold across the initial-contact boundary. Retained coverage is high throughout: GAS and BF are reported at 100% of the cycle, ERS at 98%, TA at 87%, ST at 77%, and RF and VL at 73% each. ST is the only channel with two separate retained intervals (46%–77% and a wrapping 91%–36%), implying at least one below-threshold gap in that mean curve between those retained regions. For GAS and BF, the reported 100% coverage is consistent with no below-threshold segment in the mean envelope; because coverage figures here are rounded, this should be read as what the reported value states rather than as a verified continuous supra-threshold curve at full precision. For the remaining channels, coverage below 100% indicates below-threshold regions of the mean curve, but the exact location and extent of those regions beyond the stated interval endpoints is not resolvable from this summary.

Because no toe-off or matched muscle-specific timing reference is supplied, none of these intervals can be assigned to stance or swing, to loading response, mid-stance or pre-swing, or to a conventional 60% split. Anatomical actions may be named (e.g., GAS as an ankle plantarflexor, TA as a dorsiflexor, RF/VL as knee extensors, BF/ST as knee flexors and hip extensors, ERS as an erector spinae channel), but no interval here can be mapped to a functional role such as clearance, push-off or weight acceptance. Likewise, no timing in this report can be called normal, early, delayed or prolonged — the evidence required for that judgement is not supplied.

**Max |z| values.** Each max |z| is an **extreme-value statistic**: the largest standardized difference from the pooled reference across all 1001 phase points, not a single-observation z-score. The conventional 2-SD rule does not apply — in this dataset all 109 healthy controls exceed |z| = 2 on at least one muscle, and per-muscle healthy 95th percentiles for this statistic run from 3.65 (ERS) to 4.61 (TA), higher still under leave-one-out. Against those per-muscle reference values: GAS (13.7) is far above the ERS-to-TA band of healthy 95th percentiles and is by a wide margin the largest standardized difference in this subject; VL (6.1), ST (6.3), BF (5.1) and ERS (4.8) also exceed the corresponding healthy 95th-percentile range; TA (4.6) sits essentially at its own muscle's 95th percentile of 4.61; RF (4.5) is within the range spanned by the per-muscle percentiles. "Above reference" in each row means the curve lies above the **phase-specific reference mean** at that phase point — not above a healthy percentile limit.

Critically, the location of each max |z| (e.g., 57% for GAS, 33% for VL) marks where the standardized difference from the reference is largest. It is **not** necessarily where that muscle's envelope peaks, and it does not identify a gait event or phase. And because no speed-matched reference exists, exceeding the healthy percentile band here cannot be attributed to stroke pathology as opposed to very slow walking.

**Co-contraction indices.** CCI is the Falconer–Winter index over the **full continuous envelopes** across the whole cycle (2 × common area / summed areas). It is a whole-cycle overlap index of normalized envelopes: it is not time spent simultaneously active, not muscular force, and not a phase-specific result. It can be non-zero even when detected burst intervals do not overlap, because sub-threshold envelope area contributes.

- **TA vs GAS: 0.78.** This lies above the healthy 97.5th percentile of 0.59 for this pair (healthy median 0.39, interval 0.24–0.59).
- **RF vs BF: 0.65.** This lies inside the healthy interval of 0.48–0.83, slightly below the healthy median of 0.68.

Being outside the interval (TA–GAS) does not establish pathological co-activation, spasticity, stiffness, or impaired motor control; being inside it (RF–BF) does not establish physiologically normal co-activation or normal muscle function. Both values depend on the normalization and envelope pipeline used here and must not be compared with CCI values or thresholds from other studies or algorithms. The same speed-matching limitation applies: slow walking alone can shift envelope overlap.

**Composite (GPS-like) score: 10.56.** This is the RMS of the four sagittal Gait Variable Scores above — it is *not* the validated 9-variable GPS and should not be reported as such. Against the supplied composite reference (median 5.1, 95th percentile 10.7), 10.56 is roughly twice the median and sits just below the 95th percentile. Because that value is close to the 10.7 boundary and is displayed at rounded precision, it should be described as near that boundary rather than assigned to either side of it with confidence.

Among the individual channels, the knee GVS (14.96) is the largest, followed by hip (11.51), ankle (6.91) and pelvis (6.48). GVS and the composite are **summary magnitudes of difference** from the pooled reference mean across the cycle. They do not indicate the direction of the waveform difference (e.g., more flexion versus more extension), do not localize the difference to any part of the cycle, and do not identify which phase or event contributes to the deviation.

ROM values are reported here descriptively. ROM alone does not establish a clinical restriction or contracture; that would require an appropriate matched comparison, which is not available in this input — no per-joint ROM reference values were supplied.

The reference used is 138 pooled healthy subjects, pooled across sides and ages and not matched to this participant's age or speed; the pooled calibration percentiles quoted here are not matched leave-one-out normal limits. At 0.22 m/s, far below the reference floor of 0.80 m/s, the deviation scores necessarily blend slow-walking kinematics with any stroke-related changes. These comparisons therefore describe how this subject differs from that particular reference set; they cannot establish a disease effect, a clinical diagnosis, or a severity grade. This limitation would apply equally to a healthy-group participant walking outside the reference speed range.

## 4. Questions for Further Clinical Assessment

These are open questions raised by the data, posed without presupposing that any mechanism or measurement issue has been observed. None is a treatment recommendation.

1. **Speed-matched comparison.** Is a slow-walking reference cohort (at or near 0.22 m/s) available, or could one be assembled, so that deviations attributable to walking speed can be examined separately from stroke-related differences? Without this, the GPS-like score, per-muscle max |z| values and the TA–GAS CCI cannot be interpreted as stroke-specific.

2. **Event timing.** Can individual toe-off and other gait-event timings for this participant be retrieved from the release? These would be required before any retained envelope interval could be related to stance/swing or to functional roles, and before any timing could be characterized as early, delayed or prolonged.

3. **GAS channel review.** The GAS max |z| of 13.7 is an order of magnitude beyond the healthy per-muscle 95th-percentile band and much larger than any other channel in this subject. Would inspection of the raw GAS signal, electrode placement notes, and the normalization denominator used for that channel help determine whether this reflects the participant's envelope shape, a signal-quality or normalization artifact, or both? Raw-signal quality checks were not part of this input.

4. **Threshold-rule sensitivity.** Six of seven channels show ≥73% supra-threshold coverage with boundary continuity, and two are reported at 100%. Would re-running the detection with alternative threshold or minimum-duration settings, and reviewing the mean curves directly, clarify how much of this high coverage is a property of relative-to-own-peak normalization in low-amplitude or noisy envelopes versus a feature of the envelope shape?

5. **Stride-level data.** Are per-stride envelopes and stride counts available? The averaged curves used here cannot show variability, so questions about stride-to-stride consistency are unanswerable from this summary.

6. **Non-paretic side and non-sagittal planes.** Are contralateral EMG/kinematic data and coronal/transverse kinematics available for this participant? Note that EMG normalization denominators are side-specific in this release, so cross-side amplitude comparisons would not be valid without accounting for that.

7. **Symmetry definition.** The +8.6% index (paretic step longer) uses the successive-footfall definition. Would the instantaneous definition be computed for this subject, given that the two can diverge, before this value is used in any comparison?

8. **Missing-data accounting.** The array audit noted 614 of 700 evaluable combinations, with 86 lacking valid nonzero paired samples. Can it be confirmed which, if any, of this participant's channels or pairings were affected, so that "not supplied," "not applicable" and "documented absent recording" are correctly distinguished for this record?
