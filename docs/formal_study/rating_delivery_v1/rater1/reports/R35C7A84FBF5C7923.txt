## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

**How these numbers must be read.** The released curves are **ensemble-averaged envelopes**, normalized using a shared maximum across available strides before averaging (strides are not individually rescaled). Amplitudes are therefore **relative envelope values** — not neural activation, force or strength — and muscles may not be ranked as stronger or weaker from them. An averaged curve cannot demonstrate stride-to-stride variability, and the pattern described below must not be assumed to occur in every individual stride. Detected intervals are properties of the averaged envelope under the stated rule (**>20% of that curve's own peak, minimum retained interval 3% of cycle**) and are not physiological onset, offset, contraction duration or inactivity. Where an interval begins or ends at 0%/100%, that is the analysis-window boundary (initial contact of contributing strides), not a physiological event. - RF (4.2) falls **within** the quoted span of per-muscle 95th percentiles (3.65–4.61); without the RF-specific percentile I cannot place it above or below its own threshold.
- BF (6.0) is **above the highest quoted per-muscle 95th percentile (4.61)**. This is the largest standardized difference from the reference in this participant, located at 29% of the cycle. It identifies *where the curve differs most from the phase-specific reference mean* — it is **not** the time of peak envelope amplitude and not a gait event. It does not establish abnormality, pathology or a clinical finding in a healthy-group participant, and the comparison is to a non-age-matched, non-speed-verified pooled reference.

**Co-contraction indices (whole-cycle).** CCI is the Falconer–Winter index (2 × common area / summed areas) computed on the **full continuous envelopes over the whole cycle**. It is not simultaneous activation time, not muscular force, not a phase-specific result, and it can be non-zero even when detected burst intervals do not overlap, because sub-threshold activity contributes. Values depend on the normalization and envelope pipeline used here and cannot be compared with thresholds from other studies or algorithms.

Being inside these intervals does not establish physiologically normal co-activation or normal muscle function, and a value at or near an endpoint does not establish pathological co-activation, stiffness or impaired motor control.
