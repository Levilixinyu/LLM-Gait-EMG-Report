## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

The released envelopes were normalized using a participant-, side-, and muscle-specific maximum shared across available strides before averaging, with no further amplitude normalization. Values therefore describe **relative envelope amplitude**, not force, strength, or absolute neural activation.

Retained intervals exceed 20% of each averaged curve’s own peak and satisfy the minimum 3%-of-cycle duration rule.

RF, VL, BF, ST, and TA have retained intervals that wrap across the initial-contact cycle boundary. ERS is explicitly reported above threshold throughout the analysis window and across that boundary. These are **mean-envelope threshold properties**, not physiological onset/offset times, continuous contraction durations, or evidence that every stride had the same pattern. Segments outside retained intervals cannot be interpreted as muscular inactivity.

All listed maximum-|z| locations are **above the phase-specific reference mean**. They identify the largest standardized difference, not necessarily peak envelope amplitude or a gait event.

**Whole-cycle envelope overlap:**
- **TA–GAS CCI: 0.49**, above the supplied median of 0.39 and within the empirical 0.24–0.59 interval.
- **RF–BF CCI: 0.78**, above the supplied median of 0.68 and within the empirical 0.48–0.83 interval.

These Falconer–Winter indices summarize overlap of the full normalized envelopes, including sub-threshold contributions. They are not simultaneous activation time, force, or phase-specific findings, and depend on normalization and envelope processing. Being within the supplied intervals does not establish physiologically normal co-activation or muscle function.

**Reference limitations:** The stated deviation reference comprises 138 pooled healthy subjects over 1001 cycle points, sourced from `main_cohort_excel`. It is not an appropriately matched reference for this participant. Walking speed of 0.32 m/s is below the healthy cohort’s 0.80–1.79 m/s range, so no speed-matched healthy reference exists in this dataset. Slow-walking effects cannot be separated from stroke-specific effects here. The supplied pooled calibration percentiles are not matched leave-one-out normal limits; these comparisons do not establish a disease effect or clinical diagnosis.
