## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

**How to read these numbers.** All intervals and coverage values are properties of the **ensemble-averaged envelope** under the stated rule: envelope exceeds **20% of that same curve's own peak**, with a minimum retained interval of **3% of the cycle**. They are **not** physiological onset, offset, contraction duration, or continuous activation, and they do not imply the muscle is inactive outside the retained intervals. They describe the mean curve and **do not establish that any individual stride showed the same pattern**. Amplitudes are **relative envelope values** normalized per participant, per side, per muscle (the denominator is not shared between sides in this release); they are **not force, strength, or neural drive**, and muscles must not be ranked against each other on this basis.

| GAS | 1%–43%; 51%–78% | 69% | 0.41 | 7.7 above (69%) |
| RF | 0%–100% | 100% | 0.45 | 6.6 above (80%) |

**Boundary continuity.** VL, BF, ST, TA and ERS each retained an interval that **crosses the 100%→0% cycle boundary**, i.e., the mean envelope remained above its own 20% threshold across the cycle-defining initial-contact boundary. For RF and ERS the detector returned a single interval spanning the full analysis window with coverage reported as **100%**; the 0% and 100% endpoints here are the **analysis-window edges**, not identified physiological onset/offset. Because the reported coverage is a rounded whole-cycle figure, I cannot state from this evidence that there was no brief below-threshold segment anywhere in the averaged curve.

**Two-interval structure.** GAS shows two retained above-threshold regions separated by a gap at approximately 43%–51%, and ST shows a separate short region at 55%–71% in addition to its boundary-crossing interval. These are separations of the averaged envelope relative to that curve's own threshold; they are not labelled as any gait phase, and they are not characterized as early, delayed, prolonged or normal, since matched muscle-specific timing references and individual events are not supplied. Anatomical actions may be named (e.g., GAS as an ankle plantarflexor, TA as a dorsiflexor, RF/VL as knee extensors, BF/ST as knee flexors/hip extensors, ERS as a trunk extensor), but no interval is mapped here to clearance, push-off, weight acceptance or any other phase function.

**Interpreting max |z|.** Each max |z| is an **extreme-value statistic** — the largest standardized difference across all 1001 phase points — and its location marks where the curve differs most from the **phase-specific reference mean**, which is **not necessarily the time of peak envelope amplitude** and does not identify a gait event. "Above/below reference" means above/below the reference mean at that phase point, not above or below a healthy percentile limit. The conventional 2-SD rule does not apply: every one of the 109 healthy controls exceeds |z| = 2 on at least one muscle, and per-muscle healthy 95th percentiles for this statistic run **3.65 (ERS) to 4.61 (TA)**, higher still under leave-one-out.

Applying only the percentile information actually supplied:
- **GAS (7.7), RF (6.6), BF (5.3) and ERS (4.8)** exceed 4.61, and therefore exceed the 95th-percentile value of every muscle in the stated range — including ERS against its own stated 3.65 limit. These are the largest standardized differences in this profile.
- **VL (4.2) and ST (3.7)** fall inside the 3.65–4.61 band; muscle-specific 95th percentiles for VL and ST were not supplied, so I cannot classify these values either way.
- **TA (3.5)** is below 3.65 and therefore below the 95th-percentile value of every muscle in the supplied range; its maximum deviation is in the *below-reference-mean* direction at 71%.

None of this establishes abnormal physiology, a disease effect, or a diagnosis — particularly given the absence of any speed-matched reference at 0.33 m/s.

**Co-contraction indices.** CCI here is the Falconer–Winter index over the **full continuous envelopes of the whole cycle** (2 × common area / summed areas). It is a whole-cycle overlap index of normalized envelopes: it is **not** simultaneous activation time, muscular force, stiffness, or a phase-specific result, and it can be non-zero even when detected bursts do not overlap, because sub-threshold envelope area contributes.
- **TA vs GAS = 0.62**, compared with the supplied healthy median 0.39 (2.5th–97.5th pct 0.24–0.59). This value lies **above the upper endpoint** of that empirical interval, and close to it.
- **RF vs BF = 0.78**, compared with the supplied healthy median 0.68 (0.48–0.83). This value lies **within** that interval, in its upper portion, near the 0.83 endpoint.

Being inside the interval does not establish physiologically normal co-activation or normal muscle function, and being outside it does not establish pathological co-activation, spasticity, stiffness or impaired motor control. CCI values depend on the normalization and envelope pipeline used here and must not be compared with values or thresholds from other studies or algorithms.

**Composite (GPS-like) = 17.20**, computed as the RMS of the per-joint GVS values across the supplied sagittal channels. This is **not** the validated 9-variable GPS. Against the supplied composite reference (median 5.1, 95th percentile 10.7), this subject's value is **above the 95th percentile** — i.e., the summary difference from the pooled reference is larger than that of most of the reference cohort.

Within this profile, the **knee GVS (24.14) is the largest**, followed by hip (20.68) and ankle (13.06), with **pelvis (1.61) by far the smallest**. **No per-joint GVS reference values were supplied**, so these individual scores cannot be placed against normal limits — only ranked relative to one another within this subject.

GVS and the composite are **magnitude summaries of difference from the reference mean curve across the cycle**. They **do not indicate the direction of the waveform difference** (e.g., more flexion vs. more extension), **do not localize the difference to any part of the cycle**, and do not identify a gait phase. Likewise, the ROM values are stated as measured ranges; **ROM alone does not establish a clinical restriction** in the absence of an appropriate matched comparison, and none is available here. The pelvis ROM of 4.6° with a GVS of 1.61 indicates a small summary difference from the pooled reference mean for that channel — it does not by itself establish either normal or restricted pelvic motion.

Finally, the reference used is a **pooled calibration cohort**, not a matched leave-one-out normal limit, and it contains no walker at 0.33 m/s. A speed lying inside an overall cohort range would not by itself prove an appropriate matched reference exists — and here the speed lies outside that range entirely. These comparisons therefore describe *differences from an empirical reference* and cannot establish a disease effect, a severity grade, statistical significance, or a clinical diagnosis. The same caution would apply to a control participant.

## 4. Questions for Further Clinical Assessment

These are open questions raised by the limits of this summary. None presupposes that the mechanism or measurement issue in question has been observed.

1. **Gait events.** Can individual toe-off (and other event markers) for this limb be retrieved from the release, so that envelope intervals and maximum-|z| locations can be placed relative to this participant's own stance/swing division rather than to percentages alone?
2. **Matched reference.** Is any healthy or comparison reference available at or near 0.33 m/s, or a slow-walking control condition? Without one, slow-walking effects and stroke-related control changes remain confounded in every comparison above.
3. **Per-muscle percentile limits.** Can the muscle-specific 95th-percentile (and leave-one-out) values for the max-|z| statistic be supplied for **VL and ST**, whose values (4.2, 3.7) currently fall inside the unresolved 3.65–4.61 band?
4. **Per-joint GVS references.** Are joint-level GVS reference distributions available, so that the ankle, knee, hip and pelvis scores can be evaluated individually rather than only ranked within this subject?
5. **Stride-level data.** Can individual-stride envelopes and stride-to-stride timing be examined, to determine whether the averaged-envelope pattern (including the boundary-crossing intervals and the GAS gap at ~43%–51%) reflects a consistent pattern or is a product of averaging?
6. **Signal quality and channel checks.** Have raw-signal quality, electrode placement and possible cross-talk been reviewed for the channels with the largest deviations (GAS, RF, BF, ERS) and for the continuous 100%-coverage channels (RF, ERS)? A persistently elevated envelope baseline would affect both the 20%-of-own-peak threshold and CCI.
7. **Contralateral and non-sagittal data.** Are non-paretic-side EMG/kinematics and coronal/transverse joint data available? Note that EMG amplitudes cannot be compared across sides in this release, since the normalization denominator is side-specific.
8. **Symmetry definition.** Would the instantaneous step-length definition change the +16.1% value materially for this participant, and which definition is preferred for the intended comparison?
9. **Clinical context.** Are age, sex, time post-stroke, and stroke clinical scores available in the release for this participant? None were supplied here, and any clinical correlation would require them.
10. **Measurement conditions.** Were assistive devices, orthoses, handrail/therapist support, or footwear used during collection, and is that documented? Such factors would influence both spatiotemporal values and envelope patterns but are not captured in this summary.

**Overall statement of limits.** This report describes averaged-envelope properties, an overlap index, and summary differences from an unmatched pooled reference. It does not establish normal or abnormal physiology, does not identify a mechanism, does not grade severity, and does not support any diagnostic or treatment conclusion.
