## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

All EMG statements below are properties of the **ensemble-averaged envelope** for each muscle, normalized to a shared maximum across available strides (not per-stride rescaled) and thresholded at **20% of that curve's own peak**, with a **minimum retained interval of 3% of the cycle**. Amplitudes are relative envelope values — they are not force, strength or neural drive, and muscles cannot be ranked against each other on this basis. An averaged curve cannot show stride-to-stride variability, and the mean-curve pattern should not be assumed to repeat in every individual stride.

**Boundary continuity.** RF, VL, BF, ST, TA and ERS each have a retained interval that **wraps the 100%→0% boundary**, i.e. the averaged envelope remains above its 20%-of-peak threshold across the cycle-defining initial-contact boundary of the contributing strides. This is a continuity property of the mean envelope at that boundary; it is not a physiological onset, offset, contraction duration, or a statement about any single stride. GAS is the only muscle with a single non-wrapping retained interval (12%–47%).

**Coverage interpretation.** Coverage values state the proportion of the cycle in which the averaged envelope exceeded the muscle-specific threshold. Outside the retained intervals the criterion (threshold **and** ≥3% duration) was not met — this does not establish muscular inactivity or absence of physiological activity in those regions. For **ERS**, the single wrapping interval 86%–84% with 98% coverage does correspond to a short non-retained span in the vicinity of 84%–86%; given the displayed rounding I would describe this as a brief region where the criterion was not met rather than a precisely measured gap, and I would not claim the envelope is above threshold for the entire cycle. For every other muscle, a below-threshold region between the reported intervals follows directly from the interval endpoints, but its exact extent is limited by the displayed precision.

**Amplitudes.** Peak normalized envelope amplitudes range from 0.46 (VL) to 0.76 (BF). Because normalization uses a shared per-participant, per-muscle maximum, these are internally scaled relative values; they support no cross-muscle strength ordering and no absolute activation claim.

**Max |z| locations.** Each max |z| is the **largest standardized difference from the phase-specific reference mean across all 1001 points** — an extreme-value statistic. Its location marks where this curve differs most from the reference mean, **not** the time of peak envelope amplitude and not a gait event. "Above reference" means above the reference mean at that phase point, not above a healthy percentile limit. The conventional 2-SD rule does not apply: in this dataset every one of 109 healthy controls exceeds |z| = 2 on at least one muscle, and per-muscle healthy 95th percentiles for this statistic run from 3.65 (ERS) to 4.61 (TA), higher under leave-one-out. On that basis:
- **TA (2.0)** and **ERS (2.7)** are below their respective supplied per-muscle 95th-percentile limits (4.61 and 3.65).
- **GAS (1.6), RF (2.8), VL (2.4), BF (2.7), ST (4.1)** — the per-muscle 95th-percentile values for these specific muscles are **not supplied**; only the range endpoints are given. ST's 4.1 falls inside that 3.65–4.61 span, so whether it exceeds ST's own limit cannot be determined from this input, and I will not classify it. No value here is labelled abnormal, and none is assigned statistical significance.

**Timing characterization.** Individual toe-off and matched muscle-specific normal timing references are not supplied. I therefore do **not** describe any interval as normal, early, delayed or prolonged, and do not map any interval to a functional role such as clearance, push-off or weight acceptance. Anatomical actions may be named descriptively (e.g. GAS as an ankle plantarflexor, TA as a dorsiflexor, RF/VL as knee extensors, BF/ST as knee flexors/hip extensors, ERS as an erector spinae channel), but no phase-function mapping is made.

**Co-contraction indices (whole-cycle overlap of normalized envelopes; Falconer–Winter, 2×common area / summed areas):**
- **TA vs GAS = 0.39**, equal to the supplied healthy median of 0.39 and inside the supplied 2.5th–97.5th percentile interval 0.24–0.59.

CCI here is a **whole-cycle index of envelope overlap**. It is not simultaneous activation time, not muscular force, and not a phase-specific result; it can be non-zero even where detected burst intervals do not overlap, because sub-threshold envelope area contributes. Values inside the supplied interval do **not** establish physiologically normal co-activation or normal muscle function, and values outside would not establish pathological co-activation, stiffness or impaired control. These indices depend on the normalization and envelope pipeline used here, so they should not be compared with values or thresholds from other studies or algorithms.

It nonetheless pools both sides and all ages and is not matched on age or walking speed (speed is not supplied for this participant). They cannot establish a disease effect, a clinical diagnosis, or normal physiology, and pooled calibration percentiles must not be treated as matched leave-one-out normal limits for the EMG statistics in Section 2.

## 4. Questions for Further Clinical Assessment

These are open questions raised by the limits of the supplied data. None presupposes that a mechanism, impairment or measurement problem has been observed, and none implies a treatment.

1. **Spatiotemporal context.** Can this participant's self-selected gait speed and cadence be retrieved from the release? Without them, comparability of this record to the reference cohort's speed span cannot be evaluated in either direction.
2. **Event data.** Are individual gait events (particularly toe-off) available for this participant? Access to them would permit event-anchored timing description of the envelope intervals, which the present percentage-only reporting cannot support.
3. **ERS near-continuous coverage.** Is there a question of whether the 98% ERS coverage reflects a genuinely sustained mean-envelope elevation, a low peak-to-baseline ratio in that channel (which raises coverage under a peak-relative threshold), or a signal-quality/baseline issue? Raw-signal quality checks were not part of this summary, so this cannot be resolved here.
4. **Threshold sensitivity.** Would the retained intervals and coverage values change materially under a different threshold fraction or minimum-duration rule? The reported intervals are properties of the 20%-of-peak / 3%-minimum criterion, not fixed physiological landmarks.
5. **Per-muscle reference limits.** Can the per-muscle healthy 95th-percentile max-|z| values (and their leave-one-out equivalents) be obtained for GAS, RF, VL, BF and especially ST? Only the range endpoints were supplied, which prevents any classification of the ST value of 4.1.
6. **Stride-level data.** Are individual-stride envelopes available? The averaged curve cannot address stride-to-stride consistency, and any question about variability requires stride-level data.
7. **Out-of-plane kinematics.** Are coronal and transverse channels available for this participant? The present composite is sagittal-only and cannot represent out-of-plane mechanics.
8. **Pelvic sagittal ROM.** Is an appropriate matched reference distribution for sagittal pelvic ROM available for comparison? The 1.6° value cannot be characterized as restricted or typical on this input alone.
