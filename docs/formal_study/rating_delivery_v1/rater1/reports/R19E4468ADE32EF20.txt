## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

Envelopes were normalized using a shared maximum across available strides for each participant and muscle before averaging, without further amplitude normalization here. The healthy normalization and pooling account is documentary; healthy MAT aggregation and side-specific denominators were not numerically verified by the stroke audit. Amplitudes are relative envelope values, not strength, force or absolute neural activation.

Retained intervals exceed **20% of each averaged curve’s own peak**, with a minimum duration of **3% of the cycle by sample count**.

RF, VL, BF, ST, TA and ERS explicitly retain above-threshold continuity across the initial-contact boundary of contributing strides at the **mean-envelope level**. ERS has a retained interval spanning 0%–100%. These findings do not establish physiological onset/offset, continuous contraction, inactivity outside retained intervals, or the same pattern in every stride. No gait-phase function or normal/early/delayed timing classification can be assigned.

Maximum |z| identifies the largest standardized difference from the phase-specific reference mean—not peak envelope amplitude or a gait event. The conventional |z| > 2 rule is inappropriate for this extreme-value statistic. TA’s 3.1 and ERS’s 2.9 are below their supplied pooled healthy 95th-percentile values of 4.61 and 3.65, respectively; these are calibration comparisons, not individualized normality determinations.

**Whole-cycle CCI**
- **TA–GAS: 0.29**, within the empirical interval **0.24–0.59** and below its median **0.39**.
- **RF–BF: 0.77**, within **0.48–0.83** and above its median **0.68**.

CCI measures whole-cycle overlap of continuous normalized envelopes, including sub-threshold contributions. It is not simultaneous activation time, muscular force or a phase-specific result, and depends on normalization and envelope methods. These within-interval values do not establish physiologically normal co-activation or muscle function.
