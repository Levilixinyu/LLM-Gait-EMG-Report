**1. Objective Findings**
The participant (Sub28_P) was evaluated as part of the stroke group, with data collected from the paretic side. The analyzed gait cycle runs from initial contact (0%) to the successive initial contact (100%) of the paretic limb. Gait speed was 0.21 m/s, with a recorded cadence of 46.8 steps/min. Based on the speed band definition provided, this participant is classified within the household ambulator range (<0.4 m/s). The step length symmetry index was -62.4% (using the successive-footfall definition), indicating that the paretic-side step is shorter than the non-paretic-side step. 

**2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings**
EMG data were available and evaluated for all seven supplied muscles (GAS, RF, VL, BF, ST, TA, and ERS). Retained intervals represent regions where the averaged envelope exceeded 20% of its own peak for at least 3% of the cycle; this reflects relative amplitude and coverage, not absolute neural activation, force, or continuous muscle contraction.

A notable feature for this participant is that every evaluated muscle envelope remained above threshold across the cycle-defining initial contact boundary.
*   **GAS:** Exhibited four retained intervals (22%-53%; 56%-60%; 74%-89%; and 97%-20% wrapping the cycle boundary), yielding 73% total cycle coverage. Peak normalized amplitude was 0.57.
*   **RF:** Exhibited three retained intervals (14%-17%; 28%-80%; and 86%-3% wrapping the cycle boundary), yielding 78% total cycle coverage. Peak normalized amplitude was 0.45.
*   **VL:** Remained continuously above threshold from 93% through the cycle boundary to 80%, yielding 87% total cycle coverage. Peak normalized amplitude was 0.60.
*   **BF:** Exhibited three retained intervals (40%-64%; 76%-83%; and 92%-15% wrapping the cycle boundary), yielding 55% total cycle coverage. Peak normalized amplitude was 0.60.
*   **ST:** Exhibited two retained intervals (77%-88%; and 90%-62% wrapping the cycle boundary), yielding 82% total cycle coverage. Peak normalized amplitude was 0.53.
*   **TA:** Exhibited highly fragmented coverage comprising eight retained intervals (32%-36%; 42%-47%; 50%-53%; 60%-64%; 69%-75%; 75%-78%; 87%-97%; and 100%-3% wrapping the cycle boundary), yielding 52% total cycle coverage. Peak normalized amplitude was 0.53.
*   **ERS:** The envelope remained above threshold continuously (0%-100%), yielding 100% total cycle coverage. Peak normalized amplitude was 0.49.

Whole-cycle Falconer-Winter co-contraction indices (CCI) were calculated from the continuous normalized envelopes. The TA vs. GAS CCI was 0.62. The RF vs. BF CCI was 0.68.

**3. Kinematic and Reference Comparisons**
Comparisons are made against a pooled empirical reference of 138 healthy controls. The healthy cohort's self-selected walking speeds span 0.80 m/s to 1.79 m/s. Because this participant's speed is 0.21 m/s, no speed-matched healthy reference exists in this dataset. Consequently, all reported differences conflate the effects of very slow walking with stroke-specific kinematic and neuromuscular changes; these deviations do not establish a specific clinical pathology by themselves.

The participant's composite kinematic deviation score (GPS-like) was 16.32, which is higher than the reference median (5.1) and 95th percentile (10.7). Sagittal-plane kinematic ranges of motion (ROM) and individual Gait Variable Scores (GVS) were:
