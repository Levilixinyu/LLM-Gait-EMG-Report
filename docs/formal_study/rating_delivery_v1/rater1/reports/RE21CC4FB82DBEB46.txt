All seven envelopes are explicitly reported as remaining above threshold across the initial-contact cycle boundary. BF, ST and ERS have full-window retained intervals. These findings describe mean-envelope threshold coverage—not physiological onset, offset, continuous contraction or inactivity outside retained intervals. They do not establish the same pattern in every stride or permit assignment of gait-phase functions.

Normalization uses a participant-, side- and muscle-specific shared maximum across available strides before averaging, with no further amplitude normalization here. The amplitudes are relative envelope values, not strength, force or absolute neural activation.

Maximum |z| identifies the **largest standardized difference across 1001 cycle points**, not necessarily peak envelope amplitude or a gait event. “Above reference” means above the phase-specific reference mean. The conventional |z| > 2 rule is not an appropriate abnormality threshold for this extreme-value statistic. Against the supplied muscle-specific healthy calibration 95th percentiles, ERS 5.1 exceeds 3.65, while TA 3.3 is below 4.61.

**Whole-cycle CCI:**

- **TA–GAS: 0.67**, above the supplied empirical interval of 0.24–0.59 and median of 0.39.
- **RF–BF: 0.75**, within the supplied interval of 0.48–0.83 and above its median of 0.68.

CCI measures whole-cycle overlap of the continuous normalized envelopes, including sub-threshold contributions. It is not simultaneous activation time, muscular force or a phase-specific result. These comparisons do not establish normal muscle function, pathological co-activation or stiffness; values depend on the normalization and envelope methods.
