## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

Retained intervals identify portions of the **ensemble-averaged envelope** exceeding 20% of that curve’s own peak, subject to a minimum retained duration of 3% of the analysis cycle. They are not physiological onset/offset measurements or evidence of continuous contraction; portions outside retained intervals cannot be interpreted as muscle inactivity.

All seven averaged envelopes are reported above threshold across the initial-contact cycle boundary. GAS, BF and ERS have retained intervals spanning the full analysis window. RF’s two numerically adjoining intervals are preserved as separately reported rather than merged. These mean-envelope findings do not establish the same pattern in every stride or permit timing to be classified as normal, early, delayed or prolonged.

Normalization uses a participant-, side- and muscle-specific maximum shared across available strides before averaging, with no further amplitude normalization here. Peak values therefore represent **relative envelope amplitude**, not strength, force or absolute neural activation, and do not support cross-muscle strength rankings.

Maximum |z| identifies the largest standardized difference from the phase-specific reference mean—not necessarily the envelope-amplitude peak or a gait event. “Above” and “below” in the table refer to that mean at the listed percentage, not to a percentile limit. ERS 6.5 exceeds its supplied pooled healthy 95th percentile of 3.65; TA 3.8 is below its supplied percentile of 4.61. These extreme-value statistics should not be classified using a conventional |z| = 2 cutoff.

**Whole-cycle envelope overlap:**

- **TA–GAS CCI: 0.59**, above the supplied median of 0.39 and equal to the displayed upper endpoint of the 0.24–0.59 empirical interval. Rounding precludes classification of its hidden unrounded position relative to that endpoint.
- **RF–BF CCI: 0.63**, below the supplied median of 0.68 and within the 0.48–0.83 empirical interval.

CCI measures whole-cycle overlap of continuous normalized envelopes, including subthreshold contributions. It is not simultaneous activation time, force or a phase-specific result. These comparisons do not establish physiologically normal or pathological co-activation, and the values depend on the normalization and envelope methods used.
