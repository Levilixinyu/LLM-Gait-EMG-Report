## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

**What these numbers are.** All EMG findings below are properties of an **ensemble-averaged envelope** per muscle, normalized to a participant-, side-, and muscle-specific maximum shared across the available strides (no per-stride rescaling, no further amplitude normalization). Amplitudes are **relative envelope values** — not neural activation, force, or strength — and muscles must not be ranked as stronger or weaker from them. Intervals are the regions where the averaged envelope exceeds **20% of that curve's own peak**, with a **3%-of-cycle minimum retained duration**. They are detection results on a mean curve, not physiological onsets, offsets, contraction durations, or evidence of inactivity outside the intervals, and they should not be assumed to recur in the same form in every stride.

| Muscle | Retained interval(s) | Coverage | Peak norm. amplitude | Max \|z\| vs reference (location) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| GAS | 4%–63% | 59% | 0.78 | 20.3 above reference, at 57% |
| RF | 69%–77%; 84%–66% (wraps) | 91% | 0.68 | 7.4 above reference, at 37% |
| VL | 88%–61% (wraps) | 73% | 0.67 | 8.3 above reference, at 32% |
| BF | 48%–64%; 71%–78%; 83%–26% (wraps) | 68% | 0.83 | 3.1 above reference, at 95% |
| ST | 43%–68%; 85%–26% (wraps) | 67% | 0.55 | 5.6 above reference, at 57% |
| TA | 69%–35% (wraps) | 66% | 0.48 | 3.3 above reference, at 14% |
| ERS | 47%–32% (wraps) | 87% | 0.72 | 5.8 above reference, at 60% |

**Boundary continuity.** Six of the seven mean envelopes (RF, VL, BF, ST, TA, ERS) remain above their own 20% threshold **across the 0%/100% cycle boundary**, i.e. the detected interval wraps the cycle-defining initial-contact boundary. **GAS** is the only channel with no wrapping interval; its single retained interval begins at **4%**, numerically close to the initial-contact boundary, and ends at 63%. Because GAS has one retained interval covering 59% of the cycle, the remaining ~41% of the mean envelope did not yield a retained interval under this threshold-plus-duration rule; that is a statement about the detection output, not a demonstration that the muscle was electrically silent or below threshold throughout that span (a shorter-than-3% excursion would also be discarded). The same qualification applies to the gaps between RF's, BF's and ST's multiple retained intervals.

Anatomically, these channels correspond to plantarflexors (GAS), knee extensors (RF, VL), knee flexors/hip extensors (BF, ST), a dorsiflexor (TA), and erector spinae (ERS). No interval is mapped here to a functional role such as clearance, push-off, or weight acceptance, and no interval is characterized as normal, early, delayed, or prolonged, since the matched timing references required for such statements are not supplied.

**Max |z| interpretation.** Max |z| is an **extreme-value statistic** over 1001 phase points — the largest standardized difference from the phase-specific reference mean — and is not the time of peak envelope amplitude nor a gait event. "Above reference" means the curve sits above the reference **mean at that phase point**, not above a healthy percentile limit. The conventional 2-SD rule does not apply: every one of 109 healthy controls exceeds |z| = 2 on at least one muscle, and per-muscle healthy 95th percentiles for this statistic span **3.65 (ERS) to 4.61 (TA)**, higher still under leave-one-out. Against that span: **BF (3.1)** and **TA (3.3)** fall below the lowest listed per-muscle 95th percentile (TA's own is 4.61). **ERS (5.8)** exceeds its own stated 95th percentile of 3.65. **GAS (20.3), VL (8.3), RF (7.4), and ST (5.6)** exceed the upper end (4.61) of the stated per-muscle percentile span; GAS is by a large margin the largest standardized difference in this participant's set. These are descriptive comparisons to a pooled, non-matched, non-speed-matched reference and do not establish abnormality, pathology, or a disease effect.

**Co-contraction indices.** CCI here is the Falconer–Winter index computed on the **full continuous envelopes over the whole cycle** (2 × common area / summed areas). It is a whole-cycle overlap index of normalized envelopes — not simultaneous activation time, not muscular force, and not a phase-specific result — and it can be non-zero even where detected bursts do not overlap, because sub-threshold activity contributes. It depends on the normalization and envelope methods used here, so values or thresholds from other studies or algorithms do not transfer.

- **TA vs GAS = 0.60**, against a healthy-cohort median of 0.39 and a 2.5th–97.5th percentile interval of 0.24–0.59. This value lies **just outside the upper endpoint**, by 0.01. A near-boundary value of this kind should not be treated as a categorical classification, and being outside the interval does not establish pathological co-activation, stiffness, impaired motor control, or any diagnosis.
- **RF vs BF = 0.66**, against a median of 0.68 and an interval of 0.48–0.83 — **within** the interval and slightly below the median. Being inside the interval does not establish physiologically normal co-activation or normal muscle function.

Both comparisons inherit the pooled, unmatched, sub-speed-floor limitation described in Section 1.

**Per-joint structure.** Within this participant's own profile, the ankle GVS (13.71) is the largest and the knee GVS (12.00) the second largest, with hip (4.94) and pelvis (5.08) markedly smaller. **No per-joint GVS reference values are supplied**, so no individual joint score can be called normal, elevated, or abnormal — only their rank order within this subject can be described.

**What GVS/GPS-like do and do not convey.** Both are summary magnitudes of difference from the stated reference across the cycle. Neither localizes the **direction** of the waveform difference (e.g. more flexion vs more extension), nor the **phase** at which it occurs, and neither identifies a mechanism.

**ROM.** The listed ROM values are descriptive. No ROM reference distribution is supplied here, so **ROM alone does not establish a clinical restriction or excess** in any joint; that would require an appropriate matched comparison not available in this input. Sagittal values also say nothing about coronal or transverse motion, which was not measured in these channels.

As in Section 1, all of the above are differences from a pooled 138-subject healthy reference walking faster than this participant; they cannot be attributed wholly to stroke pathology and do not by themselves establish a disease effect or support a clinical diagnosis.

## 4. Questions for Further Clinical Assessment

These are open questions raised by the limits of this summary. None presupposes that the mechanism or measurement issue in question has been observed, and no treatment is recommended.

1. **Event data.** Can individual toe-off and other gait-event timings from the release be linked to this record, so that the detected intervals and max |z| locations can be placed relative to this participant's own phase boundaries rather than reported as bare cycle percentages?
2. **Matched reference.** Is a speed-band-matched (and, if possible, age-matched) healthy reference obtainable, or a leave-one-out comparison, given that this participant's 0.68 m/s lies below the reference cohort's 0.80 m/s floor? Without it, slow-walking effects and stroke-related changes remain inseparable in every comparison above.
3. **Stride-level behavior.** Are the individual strides retrievable, so it can be examined whether the wrapping, high-coverage envelope pattern seen on the mean curves is present stride to stride, or whether it reflects averaging across strides with differing timing? Average curves cannot address this.
4. **Signal integrity.** Have raw-signal quality checks (electrode placement, baseline, movement artifact, cross-talk — particularly between adjacent thigh or shank channels) been reviewed for this participant's recordings? The very large GAS max |z| of 20.3 and the high coverage values (RF 91%, ERS 87%) are questions to pose to the raw data, not findings that can be resolved from an averaged envelope.
5. **Normalization denominator.** Since each envelope is scaled to its own participant-, side- and muscle-specific maximum, would review of the underlying maxima clarify whether the 20%-of-own-peak criterion behaves comparably across these seven channels, especially for channels with lower peak normalized amplitude (TA 0.48, ST 0.55)?
6. **Contralateral and out-of-plane data.** Are non-paretic-side recordings and coronal/transverse kinematics available to be added, so that the step-length asymmetry (+5.7%, paretic step longer, successive-footfall definition) and the sagittal-only deviation scores can be examined in a fuller context? Would the instantaneous step-length definition be worth computing alongside it?
7. **Clinical context.** Can available clinical fields for the stroke cohort (time post-stroke, clinical scores, and the ambulation-related assessments that the speed-band label does not measure) be reviewed alongside these measures before any interpretive weight is placed on the deviation summaries?

*Scope statement: this report interprets only the supplied quantitative summary. It is not a diagnosis, does not establish or exclude any pathology or impairment, and does not address treatment selection.*
