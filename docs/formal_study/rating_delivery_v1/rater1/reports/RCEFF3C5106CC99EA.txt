## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

Retained intervals represent where the **averaged envelope exceeds 20% of its own peak**, subject to a minimum interval of **3% of the cycle**. They are not physiological activation onset/offset measurements or evidence of the same pattern in every stride.

All seven mean envelopes are explicitly reported as above threshold across the initial-contact cycle boundary. GAS and BF have supplied full-cycle above-threshold intervals. These findings describe threshold coverage and boundary continuity—not continuous physiological contraction. Regions outside retained intervals cannot be interpreted as muscle inactivity.

Normalization used a shared maximum across available strides for each participant, side, and muscle before averaging, without further amplitude normalization here. The denominator is not shared between sides. The amplitudes therefore do not measure strength or force, support stronger/weaker muscle rankings, or establish stride-to-stride variability.

Maximum |z| identifies the **largest standardized reference difference**, not necessarily peak envelope amplitude or a gait event. “Above reference” means above the phase-specific reference mean at the listed location. These are cycle-wide extreme-value statistics; the conventional |z| = 2 rule is inappropriate. TA **4.6** is close to the supplied pooled TA 95th percentile of **4.61**, without supporting classification of the hidden unrounded value. ERS **4.8** exceeds the supplied pooled ERS 95th percentile of **3.65**.

**Whole-cycle envelope overlap:**
- **TA–GAS CCI: 0.78**, above the empirical **0.24–0.59** interval and median **0.39**.
- **RF–BF CCI: 0.65**, within **0.48–0.83** and below median **0.68**.

CCI measures whole-cycle overlap of continuous normalized envelopes, including sub-threshold contributions. It is not simultaneous activation time, muscular force, or a phase-specific finding. These comparisons do not establish pathological or physiologically normal co-activation; values depend on the normalization and envelope methods.
