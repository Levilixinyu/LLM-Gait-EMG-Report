## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

Retained intervals identify where the **averaged envelope exceeds 20% of its own peak**, with a minimum retained interval of 3% of the analysis cycle. They are not physiological onset/offset measurements or evidence of the same pattern in every stride.

Normalization uses a participant-, side- and muscle-specific maximum shared across available strides before averaging, without further amplitude normalization here. Peak values are relative envelope amplitudes—not strength, force or absolute neural activation.

Wrapping intervals remain above threshold across the initial-contact cycle boundary. BF, ST and ERS are explicitly reported above threshold throughout the analysis cycle and across that boundary; this establishes no below-threshold segment in those mean envelopes, not continuous physiological contraction. An interval beginning at 0% does not identify physiological onset, and regions outside retained intervals do not establish inactivity.

**Whole-cycle CCI:**
- **TA–GAS: 0.59**, equal to the displayed upper endpoint of the empirical 0.24–0.59 interval; median 0.39. Displayed precision does not resolve any hidden unrounded boundary classification.
- **RF–BF: 0.76**, within the empirical 0.48–0.83 interval and above the median of 0.68.

CCI measures whole-cycle overlap of normalized continuous envelopes, including sub-threshold contributions. It is not simultaneous activation time, muscular force or a phase-specific result, and depends on the normalization and envelope methods.

All EMG maximum-|z| locations in the table are **above the phase-specific reference mean**. GAS has the largest reported standardized difference: 16.8 at 57% of the cycle. These locations identify maximum standardized differences, not necessarily envelope amplitude peaks or gait events. TA 5.1 and ERS 3.9 exceed their supplied pooled healthy 95th-percentile maximum-|z| values of 4.61 and 3.65, respectively. Corresponding muscle-specific percentile values are not supplied for the other muscles; the conventional |z| > 2 rule is inappropriate for this extreme-value statistic.

**Reference limitations:** The stated deviation reference comprises 138 pooled healthy subjects, evaluated at 1001 cycle points, from `main_cohort_excel`. Walking speed of 0.34 m/s is below the healthy cohort’s 0.80–1.79 m/s range, so no speed-matched healthy reference exists in this dataset. Reference differences therefore cannot separate slow-walking effects from stroke-specific changes. Pooled calibration percentiles are not matched leave-one-out normal limits. Neither these deviations nor CCI interval membership establish physiological normality, pathological co-activation or a clinical diagnosis.
