**1. Objective Findings**
This report evaluates the paretic side of a participant in the stroke group (Sub21_P). The gait cycle is defined from 0% at the paretic limb's initial contact to 100% at its successive initial contact. The participant walked at a speed of 0.34 m/s (categorized within the household ambulator band) with a cadence of 74.1 steps/min. Step length symmetry, using the successive-footfall definition, was 15.1%. 

It should be noted that the participant’s walking speed (0.34 m/s) is substantially below the lowest speed (0.80 m/s) recorded in the healthy reference cohort. Because no speed-matched reference exists in this dataset, observed deviations conflate the effects of slow walking with stroke-specific control changes; these two factors cannot be separated using the provided data.

**2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings**
EMG analyses are based on ensemble-averaged envelopes normalized to a shared maximum across available strides; they represent relative envelope amplitude and do not quantify absolute neural activation, muscle force, or stride-to-stride variability. Above-threshold intervals indicate regions where the mean envelope exceeded 20% of its own peak for at least 3% of the cycle. 

Several evaluated muscles demonstrated widespread above-threshold coverage, including continuity across the cycle-defining initial contact boundary:
*   **BF**: 100% coverage (0%-100%, wraps cycle boundary); peak normalized amplitude 0.52.
*   **ST**: 100% coverage (0%-100%, wraps cycle boundary); peak normalized amplitude 0.60.
*   **ERS**: 100% coverage (0%-100%, wraps cycle boundary); peak normalized amplitude 0.48.
*   **RF**: 96% coverage across a continuous interval of 59%-56% (wraps cycle boundary); peak normalized amplitude 0.46.
*   **TA**: 83% coverage with intervals at 68%-87% and 90%-54% (wraps cycle boundary); peak normalized amplitude 0.50.
*   **VL**: 69% coverage across an interval of 88%-54% (wraps cycle boundary); peak normalized amplitude 0.62.
*   **GAS**: 82% coverage with intervals at 0%-74%, 78%-82%, and 96%-100%; below-threshold segments were detected between these regions. Peak normalized amplitude 0.49.

Co-contraction indices (CCI) were calculated over the full continuous normalized envelopes across the entire cycle. The RF vs BF CCI is 0.76, which falls within the supplied empirical healthy reference interval (2.5th–97.5th percentile: 0.48–0.83; median 0.68). The TA vs GAS CCI is 0.59, which matches the upper boundary of its empirical reference interval (0.24–0.59; median 0.39). Being within or near the edge of these intervals does not establish physiologically normal or pathological co-activation.

**3. Kinematic and Reference Comparisons**
Comparisons are made against a pooled healthy cohort (n=138), subject to the speed limitations outlined in Section 1. The composite kinematic deviation score (GPS-like, based on sagittal plane Gait Variable Scores) is 20.60, which exceeds the healthy reference median (5.1) and 95th percentile (10.7).

Sagittal plane kinematics demonstrate the following joint ranges of motion (ROM) and individual Gait Variable Scores (GVS) representing differences from the healthy reference mean:

*   **Hip Angles**: ROM 38.1 degrees; GVS 29.35

Standardized deviations for the EMG envelopes (maximum |z|) identify the largest single-point difference from the phase-specific reference mean; this value does not necessarily coincide with peak amplitude or a specific gait event.

Metric definitions (read before interpreting any number below):
- All joint angles and ROM values are SAGITTAL PLANE only. Coronal/transverse data are not included.
- The released EMG curves are ensemble-averaged envelopes. For each participant and recorded muscle, normalization uses a shared maximum across the available strides before averaging; individual strides are not independently rescaled to their own peaks. This pipeline uses the released mean curves without further amplitude normalization. In the checked stroke release, the denominator is participant-, side-, and muscle-specific; it is not shared between sides. The local array audit verified 614 evaluable combinations, not all 700; 86 lacked valid nonzero paired samples. Amplitudes are relative envelope values, not absolute neural activation, force, or strength. Do not rank muscles as stronger or weaker from these amplitudes, or infer stride-to-stride variability from an average curve.
- Above-threshold intervals are the regions where the averaged envelope exceeds 20% of that curve's own peak, minimum retained interval 3% of the cycle (by sample count). An interval written as (X%-Y%) with X > Y wraps across the 100%->0% cycle boundary (envelope remains above threshold across the cycle-defining event). An onset of 0% is the analysis-window boundary, not necessarily a physiological onset. "No interval detected" means no region met the threshold + duration criteria; it does NOT mean the muscle is inactive or non-functional.
- CCI is the Falconer-Winter co-contraction index computed on the full continuous envelopes (2*common area / summed areas), so it can be non-zero even when the DETECTED burst intervals of the pair do not overlap (sub-threshold activity contributes).
- The composite kinematic deviation score is the RMS of the per-joint Gait Variable Scores over the sagittal channels provided ("GPS-like", not the validated 9-variable GPS).
- Step length symmetry index: 200*(paretic - non-paretic)/(paretic + non-paretic), in %. NEGATIVE means the paretic-side step is SHORTER. Two operational step-length definitions exist (successive-footfall: each heel's position at its own initial contact; instantaneous: both heels at the same instant) and give different values for some subjects -- the definition actually used is stated next to the symmetry value.
- "Max |z| deviation" is the LARGEST absolute z-score across all 1001 phase points of the cycle, comparing this curve to the healthy-cohort mean/SD at each phase. This is an extreme-value statistic, NOT a single-observation z-score: in this dataset every one of 109 healthy controls exceeds |z| = 2 on at least one muscle, and per-muscle healthy 95th percentiles for this statistic run 3.65 (ERS) to 4.61 (TA) -- higher still under leave-one-out. Do not label a value abnormal by the conventional 2-SD rule; use the healthy reference range given per muscle where provided.
- Healthy-cohort CCI reference (this dataset): RF-vs-BF median 0.68 (2.5th-97.5th pct 0.48-0.83); TA-vs-GAS median 0.39 (0.24-0.59). Composite score reference: median 5.1, 95th percentile 10.7.
