## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings

All values below are properties of the **ensemble-averaged envelope** for each muscle, normalized to a shared maximum across available strides before averaging (not re-scaled per stride). Amplitudes are **relative envelope values only** — not neural drive, force or strength — and muscles must not be ranked as stronger or weaker from them. An averaged curve cannot demonstrate stride-to-stride variability, and the interval pattern described here should not be assumed to recur identically in every individual stride. Intervals are regions where the mean envelope exceeds **20% of that same curve's own peak**, retained only if ≥3% of the cycle. "No interval detected" would mean no region met that threshold-plus-duration rule, not muscle inactivity; likewise, activity below threshold is not established as inactivity. An onset written as 0% is the analysis-window boundary, not a physiological onset. Timing is **not** characterized as normal, early, delayed or prolonged, because the required matched timing references are not supplied.

**Boundary continuity.** RF, VL, BF, ST, TA and ERS are each flagged as above threshold **across the cycle boundary** — i.e., the mean envelope does not drop below its 20% threshold at the initial-contact boundary of the contributing strides. GAS is the one muscle whose retained interval begins at the 0% analysis-window edge and terminates within the cycle (at 52%), without a boundary-crossing flag.

**Coverage and fragmentation (mean-envelope level only).** ERS is reported with a single retained interval spanning the full analysis window and 100% coverage; on the supplied evidence this indicates that the averaged ERS envelope did not fall below its own 20% threshold anywhere in the analysed window. For every other muscle, the reported coverage is below 100% and the retained intervals do not span the cycle, so the mean envelope was below threshold outside those intervals — but this is a statement about the averaged curve under this threshold rule, not about physiological silence. TA shows the most fragmented retained pattern (four intervals, two of them nearly contiguous at 81%–91% and 92%–34%), and BF three intervals; ST has the lowest coverage at 33%. Fragmentation of a thresholded mean envelope is a property of the curve and the rule, not evidence of a control disorder.

**Maximum |z| locations.** The max |z| value is the **largest standardized difference from the phase-specific reference mean across all 1001 phase points** — an extreme-value statistic. Its location marks where the standardized difference is largest; it is **not** necessarily the point of peak envelope amplitude and does not correspond to any gait event. "Above/below reference" refers to the reference mean at that phase point, not to a healthy percentile limit. The conventional 2-SD rule does **not** apply: in this dataset all 109 healthy controls exceed |z| = 2 on at least one muscle, and per-muscle healthy 95th percentiles for this statistic span 3.65 (ERS) to 4.61 (TA), higher still under leave-one-out. On that basis, RF (2.5), ST (2.0) and TA (2.4) are unremarkable for this statistic. BF (3.3) and ERS (3.3) sit within the range of the quoted per-muscle 95th-percentile values; the ERS value of 3.3 lies below the stated ERS 95th percentile of 3.65. The **VL value of 7.4 (above reference, at 39%)** is the largest here and exceeds the upper end of the quoted per-muscle 95th-percentile span (4.61 for TA); the VL-specific 95th percentile is not supplied, so it cannot be positioned precisely against its own muscle limit. Even so, a large extreme-value statistic in a single averaged curve does not establish pathology, abnormal activation, or a disease effect, and its cause (true inter-individual variation, normalization scaling, electrode placement, or other measurement factors) cannot be determined from this summary.

**Co-contraction indices.** CCI here is the Falconer–Winter index computed on the **full continuous normalized envelopes over the whole cycle** (2 × common area / summed areas). It is a whole-cycle overlap index of envelope shape — not simultaneous activation time, not muscular force, and not a phase-specific result. It can be non-zero even when detected burst intervals do not overlap, because sub-threshold envelope contributes.

- **TA vs GAS = 0.53.** Within the healthy reference interval 0.24–0.59 and above the healthy median of 0.39, positioned nearer the upper endpoint.
- **RF vs BF = 0.75.** Within the healthy reference interval 0.48–0.83 and above the healthy median of 0.68.

Both values falling inside their reference intervals does **not** establish physiologically normal co-activation or normal muscle function; a value outside such an interval would not establish pathological co-activation, stiffness or impaired motor control. These indices depend on the normalization and envelope pipeline used here, so values or cut-offs from other studies or algorithms must not be applied to them.
