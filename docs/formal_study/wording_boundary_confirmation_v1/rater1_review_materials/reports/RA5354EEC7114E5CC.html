25: ## 2. EMG Envelope and Threshold Findings
26: 
27: **How these numbers must be read.** Each curve is an **ensemble-averaged envelope**, normalized to a shared maximum across available strides before averaging (no per-stride rescaling, no further amplitude normalization). "Above-threshold" means the averaged envelope exceeded **20% of that same curve's own peak**, with a minimum retained interval of 3% of the cycle. Consequently:
28: - Interval boundaries are **envelope-threshold crossings on a mean curve**, not physiological onset, offset, or contraction duration.
29: - Below-threshold regions are **not** evidence of muscle inactivity.
30: - Coverage and intervals describe the mean envelope only; they do **not** establish that the same pattern occurred in every stride, and an average curve cannot show stride-to-stride variability.
31: - Amplitudes are **relative envelope values**, not force, strength, or neural drive, and muscles cannot be ranked stronger or weaker from them.
32: - Muscle actions may be named anatomically, but no interval is mapped to a functional task such as push-off, clearance, or weight acceptance, and no timing is labelled early, delayed, prolonged or normal — the required matched timing references are not supplied.

This reflects an envelope that stays above 20% of *its own* peak; it does not imply continuous contraction, tonic overactivity, or any amplitude judgement.

Sub-threshold segments shorter than 3% of the cycle would not be retained by the duration rule, so the listed intervals should not be read as an exhaustive map of where the envelope was or was not above threshold. Small differences between summed interval lengths and reported coverage are at the rounding level and are not interpreted further.
50: - A reported onset of 0% (ERS) and the TA interval ending at 9% / starting at 87% are described relative to the **initial-contact boundary of contributing strides**; 0% is also the analysis-window edge. No further event meaning is assigned.
51: 
52: **Max |z| versus the healthy reference.** This statistic is the **largest absolute standardized difference across all 1001 phase points** — an extreme-value statistic, not a single-observation z-score. Its location marks where this curve differs most from the phase-specific reference mean, which is **not necessarily the time of peak envelope amplitude** and is not a gait event. "Above/below reference" means above/below the reference **mean at that phase point**, not above or below a healthy percentile limit.

55: - TA 3.1 below reference at 0% (the initial-contact/window boundary); GAS 1.4 below at 4%; RF 1.3 below at 3%.
56: 
57: The conventional 2-SD rule does **not** apply here: in this dataset every one of 109 healthy controls exceeds |z| = 2 on at least one muscle, and the per-muscle healthy 95th percentiles for this statistic run from 3.65 (ERS) to 4.61 (TA), higher still under leave-one-out. **All seven values here, maximum 3.1, fall below the lowest of those per-muscle 95th percentiles.** No value is labelled abnormal, and no statistical significance is claimed.
58: 
59: **Co-contraction indices.** CCI is the Falconer–Winter index computed over the **full continuous normalized envelopes** across the whole cycle (2 × common area / summed areas). It is a whole-cycle overlap index of envelopes, **not** simultaneous activation time, muscular force, stiffness, or a phase-specific result, and it can be non-zero even when detected burst intervals do not overlap, because sub-threshold activity contributes. Values depend on the normalization and envelope pipeline and cannot be compared with thresholds from other studies or algorithms.

64: Falling inside these empirical intervals does not establish physiologically normal co-activation or normal muscle function; falling outside them would not establish pathological co-activation or impaired control. These reference intervals come from a pooled cohort not matched to this subject.

92: ## 4. Questions for Further Clinical Assessment
93: 
94: These are open questions for the clinician-investigator. None presupposes that the issue has been observed, and none should be read as a finding or as a recommendation for treatment.
95: 
96: 1. **Speed and cadence:** Can the self-selected walking speed and cadence for this trial be retrieved from the release? Without them, comparability of this subject to the reference cohort's speed range cannot be evaluated, and speed-related effects on both EMG envelope shape and kinematic deviation cannot be separated from other sources.
97: 2. **Pelvis channel provenance:** Is there a way to verify marker placement, pelvis model definition, and any static-calibration offset for this session? The question is whether a modelling or measurement factor could contribute to the combination of 1.3° sagittal pelvic ROM and a GVS of 8.19 — this has not been observed here, only left undetermined.
98: 3. **Hip deviation localization:** Can the hip waveform be inspected against the leave-one-out reference band to determine at which phases and in which direction the difference occurs? The GVS magnitude alone does not provide this.
99: 4. **EMG signal quality:** Are raw traces, electrode placement notes, and artefact/crosstalk screening available for the ERS, VL and TA channels? Whether baseline offset, crosstalk or low signal-to-noise influenced the ERS full-window above-threshold result or the TA boundary-located max |z| cannot be assessed from mean envelopes alone.
100: 5. **Stride-level data:** Can individual stride envelopes and stride counts be retrieved, so that consistency of the detected interval structure across strides — and stride-to-stride variability, which an averaged curve cannot show — could be examined?
101: 6. **Event data:** Are toe-off and other gait events available in the release for this participant? If so, the detected interval boundaries could be re-expressed relative to observed events rather than cycle percentages alone.
102: 7. **Matched reference feasibility:** Is a subset of the healthy cohort matched on age and walking speed to this participant available for a re-run of the deviation and CCI comparisons? The present pooled comparison limits how far any difference can be interpreted.
103: 8. **Contextual documentation:** Are participant-level notes (footwear, assistive device, trial repetitions, any symptoms reported on the day) recorded in the release for this control session, to contextualize a composite score above the cohort median?
